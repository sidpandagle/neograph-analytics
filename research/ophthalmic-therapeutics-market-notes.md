# Ophthalmic Therapeutics Market — research notes
**Slug:** ophthalmic-therapeutics-market   **Drafted:** 2026-10-16

## Scope boundary — read this first

This report covers **ophthalmic therapeutics**: drugs administered to or for the eye, prescription
and over-the-counter — anti-VEGF intravitreal agents, anti-glaucoma drops, anti-inflammatories,
anti-infectives, anti-allergy agents, dry-eye therapies and intravitreal implants. It does not
cover ophthalmic devices, intraocular lenses, surgical equipment or diagnostics.

**One overlap must be declared.** Roche's Vabysmo (faricimab) is an anti-VEGF-A / anti-Ang-2
**bispecific antibody**, and it is already covered in that capacity by the published NeoGraph
report **`bispecific-antibodies-market`**, which records its FY2025 revenue of **CHF 4,102
million** and places it inside a disclosed bispecific total of **$14.45 billion**, of which
Vabysmo and one other product are 74%. That report's finding about Vabysmo — that it is one of the
two products carrying the entire disclosed bispecific category — **is not re-presented here as a
new finding.** Vabysmo appears in this file only as one of the four disclosed ophthalmic revenue
lines used in the grounding arithmetic, and the cross-reference is stated in the report.

A small note on conversion, for anyone reconciling the two reports: `bispecific-antibodies-market`
converted Vabysmo at the **Swiss Federal Tax Administration 2025 annual average of CHF 0.83053 per
USD**, giving **$4,939M**. This report uses the **IRS 2025 yearly average of CHF 0.831**, giving
**$4,936M**. The $3 million difference is immaterial and is recorded so that the two reports are
not read as disagreeing.

## The spread

**At 2025, $34.90B to $49.3B — only 1.41x.** At the 2032–2035 endpoints, $61.1B to $76.51B. CAGRs
run 4.26% to 8.9%, a 2.09x spread.

1.41x is the narrowest spread in this batch, and the reason is the same as in plasma
fractionation: this is a well-measured industry. The largest products are brands sold by listed
companies that disclose brand-level revenue, the prescribing population is counted in national
registries, and there is no definitional argument about whether an eye drop is an eye drop.

**But the agreement is skin deep, because the publishers agree about the total and disagree about
what is in it — and in this market the structure is checkable brand by brand.** Two sources are
read directly and they are irreconcilable on the single most important question in ophthalmology:

| | IMARC ($49.3B) | Market Glass ($36.7B) |
|---|---|---|
| Drug classes listed | Anti-glaucoma, anti-infection, anti-inflammation, anti-allergy, **others** | **Anti-VEGF agents**, anti-glaucoma, anti-inflammatory, anti-infective, anti-allergy, other |
| Largest class, as stated | **Anti-glaucoma** | Not stated |
| Where anti-VEGF sits | Inside "others" | **A named class of its own** |
| US 2025 | Not given | **$10.0B** |
| 2026–2032 rate | **4.26%** | **7.6%** |

**IMARC does not list anti-VEGF as a drug class at all, and says anti-glaucoma is the largest
one.** Anti-VEGF is the highest-revenue drug class in ophthalmology by a wide margin, and the
arithmetic below shows by how much. Market Glass names the class correctly — and then publishes a
US figure that the disclosed anti-VEGF revenue does not fit inside.

**So no source in the set gets both the size and the structure right.** That is this report's
finding, and the disclosed record is detailed enough to prove it both ways.

**CHF 0.831** per US dollar, retrieved 16 October 2026.

### Test 1 — the disclosed sum against the published totals

```
$14,956M (four disclosures)        / $36,700M (Market Glass, 2025)  =  40.8%
$14,956M (four disclosures)        / $49,300M (IMARC, 2025)         =  30.3%

$12,846M (three anti-VEGF brands)  / $36,700M (Market Glass, 2025)  =  35.0%
$12,846M (three anti-VEGF brands)  / $49,300M (IMARC, 2025)         =  26.1%
```

Both are survivable at the whole-market level. 30% of a global drug market held by four companies'
disclosed lines, with the balance in generic glaucoma drops, generic antibiotic and steroid drops,
retail artificial tears and a long tail of national brands, is an ordinary concentration. 41% is
high but not impossible. **This test does not discriminate between the two totals. The next two
do.**

### Test 2 — Market Glass's own US figure cannot hold the anti-VEGF class

Market Glass publishes **United States = $10.0 billion in 2025**, which is 27.2% of its $36.7B
global total. Now use only disclosed numbers to ask how much of that the anti-VEGF class needs.

The US weighting of this class can be read straight off the Eylea franchise, because the rights
are split by geography and both halves disclose:

```
Regeneron, Eylea franchise, UNITED STATES ONLY      $4,400M
Bayer,     Eylea,           REST OF WORLD           $3,510M
                                                    -------
Eylea franchise, worldwide                          $7,910M
US share of the Eylea franchise                     55.6%
```

That is the measured US share of one anti-VEGF brand, and it is high for the obvious reason:
intravitreal injections are reimbursed under US medical benefit at prices several times ex-US
levels. Applying it to the disclosed anti-VEGF total:

```
$12,846M x 55.6%  =  $7,142M of US anti-VEGF revenue from three brands

$7,142M / $10,000M (Market Glass's own US total)  =  71.4%
```

**Three anti-VEGF brands would be 71% of Market Glass's entire United States ophthalmic drugs
market**, leaving about **$2.9 billion** for every US glaucoma drop, anti-infective, steroid,
anti-allergy agent, dry-eye therapy and the whole over-the-counter artificial tears category that
Market Glass says is in scope and forecasts growing at 8.1%. It also leaves nothing for Lucentis,
Beovu, Susvimo or the aflibercept biosimilars now selling in the US.

**$10.0 billion is too small to be the US ophthalmic drugs market on Market Glass's own
definition.** The 55.6% US share is a measured ratio from two disclosed halves of one franchise,
not an assumption — but applying it to the other two brands *is* an assumption, and it is labelled
as one.

### Test 3 — IMARC's structure is refuted by the disclosed record

IMARC states that **anti-glaucoma is the largest drug class** and does not list anti-VEGF as a
class at all; on its taxonomy, Eylea, Vabysmo, Lucentis and Beovu all fall inside "others".

```
Disclosed anti-VEGF revenue, three brands                        $12,846M
AbbVie's entire branded glaucoma portfolio
  (Lumigan/Ganfort + Alphagan/Combigan, our arithmetic)             ~$608M

Ratio                                                               21.1x
```

For anti-glaucoma to be the largest drug class, global glaucoma therapy would have to exceed
$12,846M — in a class whose leading branded portfolio books **$608M** and whose volume is
overwhelmingly **generic latanoprost, timolol, dorzolamide and brimonidine, dispensed in bottles
priced in single-digit dollars.** Glaucoma is unquestionably the largest ophthalmic class by
**prescription count**; it is nowhere near the largest by **revenue**, and a market-size report
denominated in dollars has to rank by revenue.

**IMARC's total survives this test and its structure does not.** Those are separable: a
total built bottom-up from national pharmacy data can be approximately right while the ranking
derived from it is wrong, and the residual between $49.3B and the $14.96B of disclosed brand
revenue is filled by precisely the high-volume, low-price generic and OTC business IMARC appears
to be over-weighting. **We therefore publish IMARC's headline total and rate and publish none of
its structural claims.**

### Test 4 — the trajectory, which is where the published CAGRs break

This is the test that decides which rate to publish, and every input is disclosed.

```
Regeneron, US Eylea franchise, 2025            $4.4B      -27%
Bayer, Eylea, rest of world, 2025              EUR 3,110M  -5.9% reported, -3.7% adj.
Bayer, 2026 guidance for Eylea                            -20% to -25%
Novartis, Lucentis, Q2 2025                    $173M      -39%
AbbVie, eye care portfolio, 2025               ~$2,110M   ~-6% (from ~$2,240M)
AbbVie, eye care, Q4 2025                      $580M      -11%  (US -19.7%)

Roche, Vabysmo, 2025                           CHF 4,102M +12%, Q4 8% below consensus
Regeneron, EYLEA HD, US, 2025                  $1.6B      +36%
Bayer, Eylea 8 mg share of Eylea sales         26% of the year, ~38% in Q4
```

And the single most useful sentence anyone said about this market in 2025, from Roche's pharma
chief explaining Vabysmo's shortfall:

> **the branded intravitreal injection market in the US shrank by 15% through 2025**, while
> Vabysmo still gained share.

Read the two columns together. **Everything growing is growing inside the class at the expense of
something else in the same class**: Eylea HD (+36%) cannibalises Eylea 2 mg, Eylea 8 mg is 38% of
Bayer's Eylea by Q4, Vabysmo gains share in a US branded market that is contracting 15%. Nothing
in the disclosed record is net new revenue at the class level.

So on one side we have a largest-class that is shrinking, five approved aflibercept biosimilars,
and the originator guiding its own ex-US franchise down 20–25% for 2026. On the other we have
published whole-market CAGRs of 4.26%, 5.33%, 6.32%, 7.6% and 8.9%.

```
Published rates:   4.26%  5.33%  6.32%  7.6%  8.9%
                     ^
          the only one consistent with a contracting largest class
```

**4.26% requires the rest of the market — generics, OTC, dry eye, emerging-market volume — to
grow fast enough to offset a declining anti-VEGF class and leave low-single-digit net growth.
That is a coherent story. 7.6% and 8.9% require the anti-VEGF class to be growing, and it is
not.** Note too that IMARC *cut* its own rate from 4.44% to 4.26% between editions, in the
direction the disclosed record moved — the only cross-edition revision in this batch that goes the
right way.

### The structural event, with prices

Biosimilar entry into the anti-VEGF class is the mechanism behind every negative number above:

| Biosimilar | Originator company | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Pavblu** (Amgen) | aflibercept | **Launched October 2024**, the first Eylea biosimilar in the US, after the Federal Circuit denied Regeneron's injunction request; at roughly a **10% discount**. One source states FDA has **not** designated it interchangeable |
| **Yesafili** (Biocon) | aflibercept | **Launched August 2026** at **$1,445**, the second US launch; interchangeability resting on Phase 3 comparability data |
| **Opuviz** (Samsung Bioepis / Biogen) | aflibercept | Approved **May 2024** with interchangeability; no US launch date found |
| **Ahzantive** (Formycon / Klinge) | aflibercept | Approved **June/July 2024**; launch due **Q4 2026** under litigation settlement |
| **Enzeevu** (Sandoz) | aflibercept | Approved **August 2024**; FDA **provisionally** determined interchangeable; launch expected **Q4 2026** |

(**Sources disagree on interchangeability status for Pavblu and Enzeevu**, and all of this was read
from legal-practice and trade pages rather than from FDA's own Purple Book. **Flagged.**)

Three of the five launch in Q4 2026 or later, which means **the disclosed declines of 2025 are the
beginning of biosimilar erosion and not the end of it.** Bayer's own -20% to -25% guidance for 2026
is the company's statement of that.

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope, structure and plausibility tests. They
establish that the published totals are unusually close (1.41x) while the published structures are
irreconcilable; that four companies disclose 30.3% of the largest published total and 40.8% of the
smallest; that three anti-VEGF brands would be 71% of Market Glass's entire published US market,
which its own scope cannot accommodate; that disclosed anti-VEGF revenue is 21.1x the leading
branded glaucoma portfolio, which refutes IMARC's class ranking while leaving its total standing;
and that every disclosed trajectory in the largest class is negative or substitutional against
published whole-market growth of 4.26% to 8.9%. They do **not** establish the correct size of this
market or of any class within it. AbbVie's eye-care total is our own sum of 10-K lines read from a
search summary, not a reported figure. The 55.6% US share is measured for Eylea and **assumed** for
Vabysmo and the rest of the class. Novartis's ophthalmology revenue is absent from the sum
entirely, so the disclosed total is a floor. Roche's growth rate and the US intravitreal
contraction figure were read from trade coverage rather than Roche's own release. And dividing
company revenue into a published market total compares differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $51.40B (2026) → $66.02B (2032) at 4.26%.**

Source: **IMARC Group, *Ophthalmic Drugs Market*, report SR112026A5287, last updated 26 June
2026**, which states **$49.3 billion for 2025**, $72.6 billion for 2034 and a **4.26% CAGR for
2026–2034**, with a historical period of 2020–2025. The 2026 base is **derived by advancing
IMARC's own published 2025 figure one year at IMARC's own stated rate** (49.3 × 1.0426 = 51.40).
The 2032 endpoint is **derived by continuing that rate** (51.40 × 1.0426^6 = 66.02). Both are
labelled as interpolation in the report. Internal check: the implied rate between our endpoints is
4.26%, identical to IMARC's stated rate.

Chosen because: (a) its **4.26% is the only published rate consistent with the disclosed
trajectory** — a largest class in which the US branded intravitreal market contracted 15% in 2025,
Regeneron's US franchise fell 27%, Bayer's ex-US franchise fell 5.9% with 2026 guidance of -20% to
-25%, and Lucentis fell 39% in a quarter; (b) its **total survives the disclosure test** at 30.3%
held by four companies, where Market Glass's smaller total puts three brands at 71% of its own
published US market; (c) it was **read directly from the publisher's own page** with a report ID
and a stated last-updated date; (d) it **publishes no numeric shares at all**, which is to its
credit and means there is nothing tempting to misuse; and (e) it is the **only house in this batch
that revised its own rate downward between editions** (4.44% to 4.26%) in the direction the
disclosed record actually moved.

**What we explicitly do not take from IMARC is its structure.** Its drug-class list omits anti-VEGF
and it states anti-glaucoma is the largest class; disclosed anti-VEGF revenue of $12,846M against
roughly $608M for the leading branded glaucoma portfolio makes that untenable by revenue, and the
report says so in `market_context` and `forecast_analysis`.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** No company discloses its share of ophthalmic therapeutics, and the
  class boundaries the publishers use do not match the brands. `competitive_landscape[].market_share`
  omitted entirely. The ratios we do publish — four disclosures at 30.3% of IMARC's total, three
  anti-VEGF brands at 21.1x AbbVie's branded glaucoma portfolio, the US at 55.6% of the Eylea
  franchise, three brands at 71% of Market Glass's US total — are stated in prose with both inputs
  named.
- **Market Glass's regional and country figures** (United States $10.0B in 2025; China at 11.4%
  reaching $13.1B by 2032). We use the US figure inside the grounding arithmetic specifically to
  show that it is too small on its own publisher's definition, and we publish neither it nor the
  China figure. `regional_analysis` and `country_analysis` stay `[]`.
- **Market Glass's segment figures** (prescription drugs $37.8B by 2032 at 7.2%; OTC at 8.1%).
  Attached to a total whose US component this report's arithmetic rejects.
- **IMARC's structural rankings** (anti-glaucoma the largest drug class; retinal disorders the
  largest indication; liquid the largest dosage form; hospital pharmacies the largest channel).
  Recorded here as a defect — the class ranking is refuted by disclosed brand revenue — and
  published nowhere. Segmentation goes out as plain strings.
- **A US anti-VEGF market size.** Our $7,142M is a disclosed three-brand total multiplied by a US
  share measured on a different brand. It is a labelled plausibility calculation stated in prose
  and is never published as a market size.
- **A Vabysmo US revenue figure.** Roche does not split Vabysmo by region. We decline to estimate
  it, which is why Test 2 applies the Eylea-measured US share rather than inventing a regional
  split.
- **Any Novartis ophthalmology revenue.** Lucentis, Beovu and Xiidra are not broken out in
  Novartis's FY2025 release. The Q2 2025 Lucentis figure of $173M (-39%) is quarterly and is used
  directionally only. Novartis is named and its absence from the sum is stated, rather than its
  revenue being estimated.
- **An AbbVie eye-care total as a reported figure.** The ~$2,110M is our sum of four 10-K product
  lines and is labelled as such everywhere it appears.
- **The Market Research Future series.** Two copies of the same report page give $39.77B at 6.32%
  and $34.90B at 8.9% for the same base year. Recorded as a defect; used for range only.
- **Biosimilar interchangeability status as settled fact.** Sources conflict on Pavblu and Enzeevu,
  and none was read from FDA's Purple Book. The approvals and launch dates are published; the
  interchangeability designations are described as contested.
- **Any claim that anti-VEGF is a stated share of the market.** It is the largest class by disclosed
  revenue and we say so with the disclosures named. We do not convert that into a percentage of a
  published total, because the two are differently constructed.
