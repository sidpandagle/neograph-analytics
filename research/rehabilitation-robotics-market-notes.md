## The spread

**At 2025, $717.27M (360iResearch) to $5.13B (Spherical Insights) — a 7.15x spread.** Strip out Spherical's deliberately wider "assistive technologies" scope and it is still **$717.27M to $1.51B, a 2.11x spread**; on 2026 bases, $830.96M to $1.77B, a 2.13x spread. At the far end, $926M (2032, therapy robots only) to $8.97B.

Published CAGRs run **6.32% to 22.4%, a 3.54x spread** — the widest rate spread in the batch, and the rate and the base move in opposite directions: the house with the biggest base publishes the smallest growth rate.

The scope problem here is not one boundary but three, and two of them are visible in the titles:
- **"Rehabilitation robots" versus "robotic rehabilitation assistive technologies."** Spherical's wider name buys a 3.4x bigger base.
- **"Rehabilitation robots" versus "therapy rehabilitation robots."** Global Market Insights publishes the narrower one as its own report, at $926M by 2032 — below where three other houses put the whole market *today*.
- **Clinical versus personal.** Mordor's scope is "clinical and supervised care settings." A personal exoskeleton bought by an individual under a Medicare brace benefit is a different transaction with a different buyer and a different price.

But the real finding in this market is not the spread. **It is that the market is almost entirely invisible, and the arithmetic below shows exactly how invisible.** This is the smallest and most countable market in the batch, with three listed pure-plays that disclose revenue outright and a published Medicare lump-sum price for one of its product families — and the disclosed record accounts for 5% of it.

**And the sector's membership changed inside our base year.** Applied Digital proposed on **29 December 2025** to spin out its cloud business and combine it with Ekso Bionics to launch **ChronoScale**; Ekso priced a **$5.852M private placement on 21 January 2026**; the separation completed on **5 May 2026**, establishing ChronoScale as an independent public company; and on **1 October 2026** ChronoScale announced agreements bringing total contracted run-rate revenue to $1 billion **and the sale of the Ekso Bionics business unit**. **One of the three listed rehabilitation robotics pure-plays has ceased to be one, by reverse merger into an AI infrastructure business, during the first year of every forecast in the table above.** That is not a detail. It is the clearest single statement available about what the capital markets think of this category's near-term economics, and no published forecast mentions it.

### Test 1 — three listed pure-plays against each published market

```
Myomo $40.9M + Lifeward $22.0M + Ekso Bionics $12.8M  =  $75.7M   (FY2025)

÷ $1,510M  (Mordor, 2025)          =   5.0%
÷ $717.27M (360iResearch, 2025)    =  10.6%
÷ $1,770M  (Mordor, 2026, our base)=   4.3%
÷ $5,130M  (Spherical, 2025)       =   1.5%
```

**Every listed pure-play in this industry, added together, is 5.0% of the published market.** In the other four markets in this batch the problem was disclosures that were too *big* for the published figure. Here it is the reverse, and it is a harder problem, because an absence cannot be contradicted — it can only be interrogated.

### Test 2 — so what would the other 95% have to be?

The brief requires this question to be answered rather than left hanging. **On Mordor's $1.51B for 2025, the remainder is $1,434M, or 95.0%.** It has to consist of something, and Mordor's own published segmentation says where to look:

```
Stationary platforms, 2025                  65.30%  of Mordor's market
Exoskeleton robots, 2025                    47.35%
Upper-limb systems, 2025                    54.60%
Rehabilitation centres as end user, 2025    53.20%
```

**Two thirds of the published market is stationary, not wearable, and more than half is upper limb.** All three listed pure-plays sell the opposite: a wearable lower-limb exoskeleton (Ekso, Lifeward), a wearable upper-limb orthosis (Myomo) and an antigravity treadmill (AlterG). The money, on the publisher's own account, is in **stationary clinical gait and upper-limb therapy platforms sold into rehabilitation centres** — the Lokomat and Armeo class of product.

That tier is owned almost entirely by companies that are either private, non-US-listed, or carry the business inside something larger: **Hocoma and Motek (DIH Holding)**, **Tyromotion**, **Fourier Intelligence**, **Motorika**, **Reha Technology**, **CYBERDYNE** (listed in Japan), **Wandercraft** (private, venture-funded), **B-Temia**, and a substantial and fast-moving Chinese manufacturing base. **We did not obtain a revenue figure for any of them in this research pass and we publish none.** Naming them is the honest answer to "what is the other 95%"; sizing them would be invention.

So the statement the report makes is precise: **the published figures are not wrong because the listed companies are small — they are forecasts about a private, mostly non-US, capital-equipment tier whose revenue is not publicly disclosed anywhere we could find.** A reader who takes "$1.8 billion rehabilitation robotics market" and imagines it is the exoskeleton companies they have heard of is reading the wrong 5%.

### Test 3 — the one published price in this market, and the unit count it implies

This market has a single administered price, and it is unusually large.

```
HCPCS Level II code K1007
  first issued for the ReWalk personal exoskeleton, effective        1 October 2020
  the first HCPCS Level II code for an exoskeleton medical device
  exoskeletons added to the Medicare BRACE BENEFIT category,
  reimbursed on a lump-sum basis, effective                          1 January 2024
  PDAC contractor's initially proposed payment level                 $94,617
  final amount, set April 2024 and applied retroactively to 1 April  $91,031  (one source: $91,032)
```

(The one-dollar discrepancy between $91,031 in an earnings-call transcript and $91,032 in Ekso's own press release is **flagged**. All of these figures were **read from trade-press and company accounts rather than from the CMS DMEPOS fee schedule itself — flagged** — and the 2024 amount may have been updated in annual revisions we did not check.)

Apply the price to the publisher's own exoskeleton segment:

```
47.35% (Mordor's stated exoskeleton share)  ×  $1,510M (2025)   =  $715M of exoskeleton robots

$715M ÷ $91,031 per device  =  7,854 personal exoskeletons a year
```

Now the comparable disclosed revenue. Only two of the three listed companies sell a powered exoskeleton at all — Myomo's MyoPro is a powered arm orthosis billed under different codes, and Lifeward's AlterG is an antigravity treadmill:

```
Lifeward, ReWalk Personal exoskeleton        $8.5M
Ekso Bionics, whole company                 $12.8M
--------------------------------------------------
Disclosed powered exoskeleton revenue       $21.3M

$21.3M ÷ $715M (Mordor's own exoskeleton segment)  =  2.98%
$21.3M ÷ $91,031 per device                        =  234 devices
```

**Two companies disclose the revenue equivalent of about 234 personal exoskeletons a year against a published exoskeleton segment that implies roughly 7,850.** The gap is 7,600 devices, each nominally reimbursed at $91,031, sold by companies that do not disclose.

There is a coherent partial explanation and the report gives it: the exoskeleton segment also contains **clinical** gait exoskeletons sold to rehabilitation centres at capital prices rather than personal devices sold at the K1007 lump sum, so the true average price is a blend and the implied unit count is lower than 7,850. Wandercraft, Fourier and CYBERDYNE all sell into that tier. But the direction of the correction does not rescue the listed companies' share: at any blended price above the K1007 rate the implied unit count falls, and the two disclosed exoskeleton lines remain under 3% of the segment.

The other direction is worth stating too. **Lifeward's ReWalk Personal units rose 22% in 2025 while its revenue was flat at $8.5M** — the clearest single piece of evidence in this market that volume is growing and realised price per device is falling, which is the opposite of what a 17.1% revenue CAGR assumes.

### Test 4 — the growth rates, disclosed against published

```
Published market CAGRs                              6.32%  to  22.4%
Our published series (Mordor)                       17.10%

Actual FY2025 revenue growth, the three listed pure-plays:
  Ekso Bionics                                      -29%
  Lifeward                                          -14%
  Myomo                                             +25%  (our own calculation: $40.9M against
                                                     an implied prior year; see caveat below)
  Myomo's own 2026 guidance                         +5% to +15% on $40.9M
  Lifeward unrestricted cash at 31 December 2025     $2.2M
```

**Two of the three listed pure-plays shrank in 2025, one of them by 29%, and that one has since been merged out of the industry.** The third grew and guides to high-single to mid-teens growth for 2026. Against that, 17.10% is a forecast about the undisclosed tier, not a description of the visible one, and the report says so.

*Caveat stated plainly:* the **+25% for Myomo is our own inference and should not be relied upon** — we did not obtain Myomo's FY2024 revenue in this pass, only its FY2025 revenue of $40.9M, its Q4 of $11.4M and its 2026 guidance. **Flagged, and the figure is excluded from the report.** What the report publishes is Myomo's own guidance range against its own disclosed 2025 base, which needs no inference.

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope and plausibility tests. They establish that all three listed rehabilitation robotics pure-plays sum to $75.7M, which is 5.0% of the published market we adopt and 10.6% of the lowest published figure in the set; that two thirds of that published market is stationary rather than wearable on the publisher's own segmentation, which locates the remainder in a private, mostly non-US capital-equipment tier; that disclosed powered exoskeleton revenue of $21.3M is under 3% of the publisher's own exoskeleton segment and equivalent to roughly 234 devices at the CMS K1007 lump sum against an implied 7,850; and that two of the three listed companies shrank in 2025 against a published 17.1% CAGR. They do **not** establish that the published figures are wrong — an undisclosed tier cannot be measured, only named — and they do not establish a market size of our own. Ekso's figure is whole-company revenue including clinical devices and services, not an exoskeleton-only line; Lifeward's $22.0M was read from third-party summaries of its results rather than from its own release, which returned HTTP 403; the K1007 amount was read from trade-press and company accounts rather than the CMS fee schedule and may have been revised since 2024; the exoskeleton segment contains clinical systems priced well above the K1007 lump sum, so the implied unit count is an upper bound; and dividing company revenue by a third party's market total compares differently constructed numbers.

The 2026 base is **Mordor's own published 2026 figure**, not a derivation. The 2032 endpoint **continues Mordor's own stated rate one year past its own horizon**: 1.77 × 1.1710^6 = **4.56**. Internal check: interpolating forward lands 2031 on **3.90** against Mordor's published **3.89**, and the implied rate between our endpoints is **17.09%** against a stated 17.10%.

Chosen because: (a) it is the **only source in the set read directly from the publisher's own page**, and every other figure came through a search summary, a Google Groups repost or trade press; (b) it is the **only one that states both what is in and what is out** — clinical and supervised care settings in, surgical robots and consumer fitness devices out — which in a category this loosely named is the single most useful thing on the page; (c) it publishes **both a base and a mid-horizon value plus a rate that reconcile to the cent**, so the trajectory is checkable; and (d) its published segment shares are what make Test 2 possible at all — **it is the only source that tells a reader where the 95% it cannot see is supposed to be.**

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Medical Devices.

**This is the thinnest evidence base in the batch and the report says so in its first paragraph.** 360iResearch's $717.27M for 2025 — less than half Mordor's — is stated as the low end, with the note that the listed pure-plays are 10.6% of it rather than 5.0%, so the lower reading is the one the disclosed record sits less awkwardly against. We publish Mordor for the reasons above and tell the reader plainly that a market 95% of which is undisclosed cannot be validated by anyone, including us.

The report states plainly in `executive_summary`, `market_context`, `forecast_analysis` and `research_methodology` that the three listed pure-plays sum to $75.7M against a published $1.51B for 2025; that two of the three shrank and one has been merged into an AI infrastructure company and then sold; that two thirds of the published market is stationary clinical equipment sold by undisclosed private suppliers; that disclosed powered exoskeleton revenue is under 3% of the publisher's own exoskeleton segment; and that ReWalk Personal units rose 22% on flat revenue.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Three companies disclose revenue; none discloses a share. The 5.0%, 10.6%, 4.3%, 1.5% and 2.98% ratios are **our own division** of disclosed revenue by third parties' published totals, stated in prose with both inputs named. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **Any revenue figure for Hocoma, DIH Holding, Motek, Tyromotion, Fourier Intelligence, Motorika, Reha Technology, CYBERDYNE, Wandercraft or B-Temia.** These companies are **named** as the tier that the published market must largely consist of, and **not sized**. We found no figure for any of them in this pass. That absence is the finding; filling it with an estimate would destroy it.
- **Mordor's segment shares as segment percentages.** Exoskeleton 47.35%, upper limb 54.60%, geriatric 61.40%, stationary platforms 65.30% and rehabilitation centres 53.20% are **quoted in prose with the publisher named**, because they are the evidence for where the undisclosed remainder sits — which is the report's central finding. They are **not** reproduced in `market_segmentation`, which goes out as plain strings. One source, one page, no stated method.
- **Mordor's North America 39.60% of 2025.** A single house's apportionment of a market that is 95% undisclosed, with no method stated. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`. `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **An exoskeleton unit count.** The 7,854 and 234 device figures are **arithmetic on a reimbursement rate**, used to show the gap between the published segment and the disclosed revenue. The segment contains clinical systems priced far above the K1007 lump sum, so 7,854 is an upper bound. Neither figure is published as a unit count.
- **A Myomo growth rate for 2025.** We obtained its FY2025 revenue of $40.9M but not its FY2024 figure, so any growth rate would be our inference. **Flagged.** The report uses Myomo's own 2026 guidance of $43–46M, later raised to $45–47M, against its own disclosed 2025 base.
- **The K1007 amount as a current rate.** $91,031 (or $91,032 — sources differ by a dollar) was set in April 2024 and read from trade-press and company accounts, not the CMS DMEPOS fee schedule. The PDAC contractor's initially proposed $94,617 is recorded as a proposal only. **All flagged**, and the figure is used for a ratio rather than published as a price.
- **A device price for any clinical rehabilitation robot.** Lokomat-class systems are widely described as capital equipment costing six figures, but we found no published price we could source. Named as capital equipment; not priced.
- **Spherical Insights' $5.13B as the rehabilitation robotics market.** It is scoped to "robotic rehabilitation assistive technologies", a wider category, and is recorded to show what the name change buys. Not used for the headline.
- **The NRTimes $3B/2024 to $8B/2032 series and the Notion-hosted 22.4% listing.** Neither reconciles with any other source in the set and both came through third parties. Range and proofreading evidence only.
- **Global Market Insights' $926M by 2032 as the whole market.** It is scoped to "therapy rehabilitation robots", a named sub-scope published as its own report, and is below where three other houses put the whole market today. Recorded as evidence of inconsistent slicing.
- **Any statement that the published figures are wrong.** They cannot be checked. The report says the disclosed record accounts for 5% of the published market and names what the rest would have to be; it does not claim to know the market is smaller than published.
