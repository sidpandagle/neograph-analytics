# Ambulance and Emergency Medical Services — research notes
**Slug:** ambulance-and-emergency-medical-services-market   **Drafted:** 2026-10-18

Pipeline row 55, round 7A. Industry: Healthcare IT.

This market has an unusually good public register and an unusually bad published
literature. The payment rate is statutory and published annually by CMS; the
national activation count is a federal registry (NEMSIS) covering 99% of US
counties; the cost of delivering a transport was measured for the first time by
a congressionally mandated CMS data collection (GADCS) and analysed by MedPAC in
June 2026; the largest US operator became an SEC registrant in May 2026. Against
that, the commercial estimates of "the ambulance market" and "the EMS market"
disagree by 8.94x at the same base year, and one house's two reports on the same
subject disagree with each other by 1.90x. Almost nothing below has to be taken
on trust, and the notes take as little on trust as possible.

Two boundary questions govern everything and are answered from sources in
"Grounding arithmetic", not assumed: **(1)** an EMS *activation* is not a
billable *transport*, and **(2)** a majority of US ground ambulance
organisations are government-owned and a large part of their revenue is not
billed at all.

## The spread

Four comparisons have to be kept apart.

**1. Across the whole published set at a 2026 base — 8.94x, and it is almost
entirely scope.** Highest is Fortune's ambulance services at USD 63.76 bn;
lowest is Market Growth Reports' EMS at USD 7.13 bn.

```
$63.76B / $7.13B  = 8.94x   full set, 2026 base
$63.76B / $32.11B = 1.99x   excluding the Market Growth Reports outlier
```

**2. Within one house, same subject, same update date — 1.90x.** Fortune
Business Insights publishes both an "Ambulance Services Market" and an
"Emergency Medical Services Market", both last updated 28 September 2026:

```
$60.05B / $31.65B = 1.90x   Fortune's own two 2025 base-year figures
$63.76B / $33.56B = 1.90x   Fortune's own two 2026 figures
```

The same publisher's two products on overlapping subject matter differ by more
than the entire air ambulance segment. Neither page tells a reader the other
exists. This is the single most useful fact in the published literature,
because it fixes the size of the definitional problem without needing any
register at all.

**3. On growth — 1.72x, a real but secondary disagreement.** Published CAGRs
run 5.7% (Fortune EMS) to 9.82% (360iResearch ambulance services): `9.82 / 5.70
= 1.72x`. Unlike the scope gap, this one is not resolvable from public data.

**4. The named tiers.** Four distinct things are being sold under two titles:

- **Tier A — EMS products and consumables.** USD 32.11-35.49 bn at a 2026 base.
  Mordor labels this correctly; **Global Growth Insights sells the same model
  under an EMS *services* title**, which is why its figure sits in the products
  cluster and its named vendors are Stryker and Cardinal Health. Market Research
  Future's USD 38.38 bn and Market Growth Reports' USD 7.13 bn both mix products
  and services in one model.
- **Tier B — EMS as billed pre-hospital service revenue.** USD 33.56 bn
  (Fortune EMS, 2026). Coincides numerically with Tier A, which is a trap: the
  same number means two different things in two different reports.
- **Tier C — ambulance services, all modes, billed.** USD 51.73-63.76 bn at a
  2026 base, of which 360iResearch puts **USD 14.66 bn (28.34%) in air
  ambulance alone**.
- **Tier D — the cost of delivering US ground ambulance service, measured.**
  **USD 51.11 bn** (derived below from CMS's own GADCS collection as analysed by
  MedPAC). Not a published estimate; a federal measurement, for one country and
  one transport mode.

**Verdict: the headline gap is a scope disagreement, and Tier D is the finding.
The measured cost of delivering ground ambulance service in the United States
alone is 0.988x the entire global ambulance services market as published.**

## Grounding arithmetic

### (a) What "market size" cannot be: the measured US ground cost base

CMS's Ground Ambulance Data Collection System was built under section 50203(b)
of the Bipartisan Budget Act of 2018 precisely because no cost data existed.
MedPAC's June 2026 mandated report is the first analysis of it. Its Table 6-4
and Table 6-6 give, for the same organisations, both the transport count and
the mean cost per transport by ownership. Multiply them out:

```
for profit   20,995,123 transports x $563   = $11,820,254,249
nonprofit     9,692,384 transports x $830   =  $8,044,678,720
government   19,213,923 transports x $1,626 = $31,241,838,798
                                              ---------------
total        49,901,430 transports            $51,106,771,767

$51,106,771,767 / 49,901,430 = $1,024.15  transport-weighted mean cost
$51,106,771,767                           = $51.11 bn
```

The three ownership transport counts sum to exactly the 49,901,430 total
MedPAC prints, which is the arithmetic check that the table was read correctly.

Now against the published headlines:

```
$51.11B / $51.73B  (360iResearch ambulance services, global, 2026) = 0.988x
$51.11B / $63.76B  (Fortune ambulance services, global, 2026)      = 0.802x
$51.11B / $33.56B  (Fortune EMS, global, 2026)                     = 1.523x
$51.11B / $32.11B  (Global Growth Insights EMS, global, 2026)       = 1.592x
$51.11B /  $7.13B  (Market Growth Reports EMS, global, 2026)       = 7.168x
```

**What it rules out.** It rules out every one of these figures being a measure
of the resources consumed by emergency medical services worldwide. One country,
ground only, excluding air ambulance entirely, excluding every response that did
not end in a transport, accounts for 98.8% of the largest-scope global service
estimate and exceeds three of the five outright. The published figures are
**billed-revenue** quantities for a commercially addressable subset, not
cost-of-delivery quantities for the system.

**Caveats stated plainly, and they all run one way.** MedPAC deflates all GADCS
dollar figures to 2022 levels, and the transports are a 12-month period in
2022-2023, while the headlines are 2026 — so the 2026 US figure is *larger* than
$51.11 bn, not smaller. The GADCS sample is 10,358 organisations in Table 6-4
(10,581 across the four cohorts CMS drew, about 71% of all US ground ambulance
organisations), so $51.11 bn is a **floor** on the national total, not an
estimate of it. The sampled organisations are not a random fraction of national
volume, so the floor is *not* grossed up here: 49,901,430 / 0.71 would be an
invented number and is not used anywhere.

### (b) The unit question: an activation is not a billable transport

The brief's volume denominator is NEMSIS, and the two units are not the same
thing. NEMSIS states the definition itself: "An EMS activation is an occurrence
which initiates an EMS response with the potential of patient contact... The data
are reflected as the number of activations instead of the number of patients
because there can be more than one activation per patient per call." MedPAC
states the payment rule in the opposite direction: "Medicare pays for a transport
but not for a response that does not result in a transport."

2024 (NEMSIS National EMS Data Report, 54 states and territories, 14,756
agencies, incidents in 99% of US counties):

```
60,298,684  EMS activations
36,367,261  treated and transported, 911 response   = 60.31% of activations
30,123,274  type-of-destination total (911-initiated, patient contact made)
            = 49.96% of activations
11,300,000  FFS Medicare transports (MedPAC, 2024)  = 18.74% of activations
49,901,430  GADCS transports, all payers            = 82.76% of activations
```

**What it rules in and out.** It rules out using the activation count as a
transport count: 39.7% of 2024 activations did not end in a treated-and-
transported 911 response, and under the AFS none of those is billable at all. It
rules in the GADCS transport count as the right denominator for revenue
arithmetic — 49.9 m GADCS transports against 60.3 m activations is 82.8%, which
is higher than the 911 treated-and-transported share because GADCS includes
interfacility and non-emergency transports that NEMSIS's 911-only cut excludes.
The two registers are consistent once the inclusion criteria are matched, and
anyone dividing a market size by 60.3 million is dividing by the wrong number.

NEMSIS 2025 (public-release research dataset): **63,635,893 activations**,
14,801 agencies, 54 states and territories.

```
63,635,893 / 60,298,684 = 1.0553  -> +5.53% activations, 2024 to 2025
```

That is a one-year volume growth rate, and it is not a revenue growth rate: the
agency count rose from 14,756 to 14,801 in the same period, so part of the
increase is reporting coverage, and NEMSIS itself says the database is "not
entirely comprehensive." It is recorded because it brackets the published CAGRs
(5.7%-9.82%) from below on volume, which means the upper half of that range has
to come from price and intensity, not from more calls.

### (c) The statutory price: the 2026 ambulance fee schedule, divided through

This is the rare market where the unit price is a published legal fact. For 2026
CMS set the conversion factor at **$284.56** and the mileage base rate at
**$9.15 per mile**; the RVUs have not been updated since 2002. Service payment =
`RVU x $284.56 x (0.7 x PE GPCI + 0.3) x` the add-on. Computed at PE GPCI =
1.000 so the geographic term is exactly 1:

| Service level | HCPCS | RVU | Urban (x1.02) | Super-rural (x1.03 x1.226) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BLS non-emergency | A0428 | 1.00 | $290.25 | $359.34 |
| BLS emergency | A0429 | 1.60 | $464.40 | $574.94 |
| ALS1 non-emergency | A0426 | 1.20 | $348.30 | $431.20 |
| ALS1 emergency | A0427 | 1.90 | $551.48 | $682.74 |
| ALS2 | A0433 | 2.75 | $798.19 | $988.18 |
| Specialty care transport | A0434 | 3.25 | $943.32 | $1,167.84 |
| Paramedic ALS intercept | A0432 | 1.75 | $507.94 | $628.84 |

Mileage, urban: `$9.15 x 1.02 = $9.3330` per mile. Rural/super-rural first 17
miles: `$9.15 x 1.03 x 1.5 = $14.1367` per mile.

Now the realised average, from MedPAC's 2024 totals, against the measured cost:

```
$5,300,000,000 / 11,300,000 transports = $469.03  realised Medicare payment per transport
$469.03 / $1,024.15 (GADCS transport-weighted mean cost) = 0.4580
```

**Medicare's realised average payment per ground transport is 45.80% of the
measured transport-weighted cost of providing one.** The realised $469.03
includes mileage and sits between the urban BLS-emergency base rate ($464.40)
and the urban ALS1-emergency base rate ($551.48), which is exactly where a mix
of 43-66% BLS transports should land it, and below the $458.60 that GADCS's
average urban RVU of 1.58 implies for the service component alone
(`1.58 x $284.56 x 1.02`) once you remember the average includes non-emergency
and rural-to-urban mix effects.

**What this rules in: the tax subsidy is structural, not incidental.** A payment
system covering 45.8% of measured cost cannot be the whole revenue of the
sector, which is the mechanism behind section (d).

If every US ground transport were paid at Medicare's realised rate:

```
49,901,430 x $469.03 = $23.41 bn
$23.41B / $33.56B (Fortune EMS global 2026) = 0.697x
$23.41B / $51.73B (360iResearch global 2026) = 0.452x
```

So even at Medicare's below-cost rates, US ground transports alone are 69.7% of
one house's entire global EMS figure. Both bounds — cost at $51.11 bn, Medicare
rates at $23.41 bn — sit inside the same global headlines, which is the span the
published literature has to fit a whole world into.

### (d) The structural feature, established from sources: tax-funded municipal delivery

The brief asked that this be found, not assumed. It is in three independent
places.

**MedPAC / GADCS, nationally.** CMS classifies ambulance organisations as
suppliers — "such as local fire departments or private for-profit entities
unaffiliated with institutions" — which are **96%** of all ambulance
organisations, and providers (hospital-affiliated institutions), **4%**. In
2024 there were **approximately 10,600** ground ambulance suppliers and
providers. Within GADCS:

```
government-owned   5,652 of 10,358 organisations = 54.57%  of organisations
                  19,213,923 of 49,901,430 transports = 38.50% of transports
for profit         1,937 orgs (18.70%)  20,995,123 transports (42.07%)
nonprofit          2,769 orgs (26.73%)   9,692,384 transports (19.42%)
```

MedPAC states the consequence directly: "over half of the organizations are
government owned, which is different from other health care sectors. For
example, only about 20 percent of acute care hospitals are government owned. In
addition, **most of the government-owned ambulance organizations also provide
fire or other first response services.**" Government ownership is the majority
in urban (51%), rural (56%) and super-rural (65%) service areas alike.

**The money that is not billed.** MedPAC: "52 percent of the overall revenue
reported by [government-owned] organizations stemmed from" sources such as local
government funding. Separately, **30%** of government-owned organisations
reported local government funding, averaging **$322 per transport** — against
**$32** for nonprofit and **$12** for for-profit organisations. Government-owned
organisations also have by far the highest measured cost per transport
(**$1,626**, against $830 nonprofit and $563 for profit) and the lowest
revenue-to-cost ratios (median weighted **0.98**, unweighted **0.72**, against
1.11 and 1.03 for profit).

```
0.30 x 5,652 = 1,696 government-owned organisations reporting local government funding
```

**One state's licence register, as a cross-check.** Washington's Office of the
Insurance Commissioner, Ground Ambulance Balance Billing Study (1 October 2023),
from Department of Health 2022 licence data:

```
licensed EMS agencies, all        : 478   (public 404, private 68, tribal 6)
licensed for ground transportation: 299   (public 244, private 50, tribal 5)

244 / 299 = 81.61%  of ground-transport-licensed agencies in Washington are public
```

Washington also measured the funnel: **818,000 dispatch calls, of which 684,000
(83.6%) resulted in transport** to a secondary location — an independent
corroboration, at state scale, of the activation-to-transport ratio in (b). And
it priced the gap: provider-surveyed average cost per transport ran **$1,370.87**
(BLS non-emergency) to **$2,246.61** (specialty care transport), while commercial
allowed amounts ran **152% to 646%** of the Medicare allowed amount for the same
code — public providers at the low end, private at the high. Public agencies are
described as "funded through local government taxes, levies" under RCW 84.52.069.

**What this rules out.** It rules out reading any of the published market sizes
as the cost of running EMS. A majority of US ground ambulance organisations are
government-owned, most of them also run fire or first response, 52% of their
revenue is not from billing, and the median government organisation recovers
0.72 of its costs from all revenue combined. A revenue-based market model
either excludes that money or double-counts a tax base; no public source says
which any of these houses did.

### (e) One operator against both the register and the headline

Global Medical Response became an SEC registrant in 2026 (GMR Solutions Inc.,
NYSE: GMRS; IPO prospectus dated 12 May 2026), so the largest US operator's
volumes are now disclosed quarterly. H1 2026, annualised by doubling:

```
ground transports  2,019,992 x 2 = 4,039,984
air flights           70,494 x 2 =   140,988
total transports   2,090,486 x 2 = 4,180,972

4,039,984 / 49,901,430 (GADCS US ground) = 8.10%  of US ground transports
```

FY2026 guidance, reaffirmed 12 August 2026: net revenue **$5,890 m to $6,180 m**,
midpoint **$6,035 m**.

```
$6,035M / $51,730M (360iResearch global ambulance services 2026) = 11.67%
$6,035M / $63,760M (Fortune global ambulance services 2026)      =  9.47%
$6,035M / $33,560M (Fortune global EMS 2026)                     = 17.98%
```

**One US company with 8.10% of US ground transports is 11.67% to 17.98% of the
entire global market as published.** Its own disclosed net transport revenue per
ambulance transport is **$1,365** (H1 2026; $1,370 in Q2, $1,360 in Q1, $1,305
in H1 2025), which is `1,365 / 1,024.15 = 1.333x` the GADCS transport-weighted
mean cost — a 1.33x premium, not an 11x one. So the revenue share cannot be
explained by GMR being an unusually expensive operator. It is explained by the
global headline being, in substance, a US-weighted ground-ambulance billed-
revenue quantity.

### (f) Air ambulance is a separate price regime, and the houses half-admit it

360iResearch publishes both its ambulance services total and its air ambulance
segment for the same 2026 base year, from the same house in the same month:

```
$14.66B / $51.73B = 28.34%  of the house's own total is air ambulance
```

GMR's disclosed mix, H1 2026: `140,988 / 4,180,972 = 3.372%` of transports are
flights. If air is 28.34% of value on 3.37% of volume, the implied unit-price
ratio is:

```
(0.2834 / 0.03372) / (0.7166 / 0.96628) = 8.405 / 0.7416 = 11.33x
```

Against measured prices, 11.33x is **too low**. GAO's 2019 report (GAO-19-292,
published 20 March 2019, about 20,700 transports) found 2017 median prices of
**$36,400** for helicopter and **$40,600** for fixed-wing air ambulance
transports for privately insured patients, with **69%** out of network.

```
$36,400 / $1,024.15 = 35.5x   helicopter median price to mean ground cost
```

**What this rules in:** air ambulance is a genuinely separate segment at an order
of magnitude higher unit price, and a blended "ambulance services market" figure
is dominated at the margin by its air component in a way its volume share would
never suggest. **What it rules out:** treating the 28.34% air share as
consistent with any national volume mix — either the air share of value should
be far higher than 28.34%, or the air volume assumption behind it is far above
GMR's 3.37%. GMR's mix is one company's and is not national, so the direction is
established and the magnitude is not claimed.

### (g) The chosen headline's own regional split, against the register

360iResearch publishes no country figure. Fortune does, for its ambulance
services series: North America **37.81%** of 2025 (USD 22.71 bn) and the US
**~34.6%** of global 2026 sales (**USD 22.04 bn**; `0.346 x 63.76 = 22.06`,
agreeing with the published figure to 0.1%). Test the US figure against the
measured floor from (a):

```
$51.11B / $22.04B = 2.319x   measured US ground cost floor / Fortune's published US 2026
$51.11B / $63.76B = 80.15%   the US share Fortune's own global headline forces
$51.11B / 0.346   = $147.71B = 2.317x Fortune's headline, if 34.6% is the true US share
```

**Fortune's United States, in 2026, is 2.32x below what US ground ambulance
service measurably cost to deliver in 2022-2023 dollars** — ground only, no air,
71% of organisations only, deflated to 2022. So the two numbers cannot both be
right: **either the US is at least 80.2% of the global total and not 34.6%, or
the global headline is 2.32x larger than published.** The same test on Fortune's
EMS series is worse: `0.410 x 33.56 = $13.76B`, and `$51.11B / $13.76B = 3.71x`.

This is a measurement bounding an estimate, not two estimates disagreeing, and
it generalises past this publisher: **any global ambulance-services headline near
$50-65 bn for a 2026 base must put the United States at roughly 80%, because the
US ground component alone is federally measured and is most of it.** A house
printing ~35% is understating either the US or its own global total. That is why
re-sourcing to the house that prints no regional split is not a fix — it hides
the finding instead of stating it — and why `country_analysis` stays empty.

## What we publish

**Headline: USD 51.73 bn (2026) → USD 90.93 bn (2032) at 9.86%.** 360iResearch
via Research and Markets, "Ambulance Services Market — Global Forecast
2026-2032", September 2026, 185 pages. Chosen because it is the most recently
published estimate in the set, it carries the most recent base year, it is the
only series whose stated scope explicitly covers **all three transport modes
(road, air, water), emergency and non-emergency, and all payment methods
including government funding**, and its endpoints reconcile to its own stated
rate to within 0.04 points. Verified on two publisher-side URLs carrying the
same three figures.

**The CAGR published is 9.86%, not the publisher's printed 9.82%, and the page
says so.** The publisher's own endpoints imply
`(90.93 / 51.73)^(1/6) - 1 = 9.8569%`. Publishing 9.82% against a series
interpolated to land on 90.93 would leave the chart and the headline
disagreeing; publishing 9.86% keeps the two endpoints as the publisher gives
them and states the derivation. Both figures appear in these notes.

`market_forecast` interpolates geometrically between the two published endpoints
at 9.8569%: **51.73, 56.83, 62.43, 68.58, 75.34, 82.77, 90.93**. The
intermediate years are interpolation, not separately sourced observations, and
the chart title says so.

**Published with its contradictions stated, not silently.** Three of them reach
the page with their arithmetic:

1. The measured US ground cost base of **USD 51.11 bn** is **0.988x** the chosen
   global headline (section a). The headline is therefore a billed-revenue
   boundary, and the report says that in the summary, the executive summary, the
   forecast analysis and a FAQ.
2. The spread is **8.94x** across the set and **1.90x inside a single house**
   (section 2 of "The spread"). Reported as the main finding about the
   literature.
3. Any ~$50-65 bn global headline forces the US to ~80% (section g). Reported,
   with the arithmetic, as the reason no regional or country table is published.

Everything else published in the JSON is a register figure, a statutory rate or
an SEC-registrant disclosure, each traceable to a numbered source below.
