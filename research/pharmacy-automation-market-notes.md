## The spread

**At 2025, $7.2B to $9.0B — 1.25x.** At 2032, **$7.87B to $18B — 2.29x.** Published CAGRs run
6.10% to 12.2%, a 2.0x spread.

The endpoint spread is the interesting one, and it produces an outright contradiction:

```
Meticulous Research, 2032 forecast                 $7.87B
IMARC Group, 2025 actual                           $7.20B
```

**One house's seven-years-out forecast is 9.3% above another house's present.** Meticulous
publishes a 7.6% CAGR but no base-year value, so the only way to back one out is from its own
endpoint: 7.87 / 1.076^7 = **$4.67B for 2025**, which is 35% below IMARC's figure for the same
year and 48% below HTF's. A house that forecasts 7.6% growth from an unpublished base 35%
below the consensus will land below the consensus' present day, and it does.

The scope decision that explains the spread is visible in the one segmentation that discloses
it. GMI reports **products at 67.6% of its 2022 component market**, with services and software
making up the rest. A publisher that counts only the robots and cabinets gets roughly
two-thirds of the number a publisher that counts the software subscriptions, the service
contracts and the consumables gets. Omnicell's own disclosure confirms this is where the money
is moving: its 2025 growth came from "connected devices and technical service offerings, along
with SaaS, Expert Services, and consumables."

## Grounding arithmetic

Pharmacy automation has two countable denominators — US retail pharmacies and US hospitals —
and one disclosed pure-play revenue line. All three are used.

### Anchor 1 — the first test is Omnicell

Omnicell is the listed pure-play. Becton Dickinson's Medication Management Solutions unit is
the other large disclosed figure.

```
Omnicell FY2025 total revenue (reported 5 Feb 2026)          $1,185M   (+7%, +$73M on 2024)
  Q4 2025 revenue                                              $314M   (+2% YoY)
  FY2025 GAAP net income                                         $2M   ($0.04/diluted share,
                                                                        down from $13M / $0.27)
BD Medication Management Solutions, FY2025                    ~$3,500M  (chart read - flagged)
                                                              -------
  Sum of two                                                  $4,685M
```

Against the published 2025 market sizes:

```
1,185 / 7,200  = 16.5%   Omnicell alone, against IMARC's $7.2B
1,185 / 9,000  = 13.2%   Omnicell alone, against HTF's $9.0B
1,185 / 7,870  = 15.1%   Omnicell alone, against Meticulous' 2032 ENDPOINT
4,685 / 7,200  = 65.1%   Omnicell + BD MMS, against IMARC's $7.2B
4,685 / 9,000  = 52.1%   Omnicell + BD MMS, against HTF's $9.0B
```

**Two companies are between 52% and 65% of this market**, before Swisslog Healthcare,
ScriptPro, ARxIUM, Capsa Healthcare, Yuyama, Willach, TCGRx/Parata and the central-fill
specialists are counted at all. And Omnicell's single-company share of 16.5% is the cleanest
figure in the set, because Omnicell does essentially nothing else.

*Caveat stated plainly, and it matters more here than in the other four reports:* **BD's
Medication Management Solutions segment includes BD Alaris infusion pumps and
medication-management software that are not pharmacy automation in the sense these market
reports use.** The ~$3.5B figure therefore substantially overstates BD's pharmacy-automation
content, and the 65.1% figure is an upper bound, not a measurement. Omnicell's $1,185M is also
wider than hardware — it includes SaaS, Expert Services and consumables. The 16.5% figure is
the one to trust; the 65.1% is a ceiling.

Also: the ~$3.5B BD figure was **read from a revenue-by-segment chart (Statista reproducing
BD's annual report presentation), not from a segment table in the 10-K. Flagged.**

### Anchor 2 — dollars per retail pharmacy per year

IMARC publishes the segment shares needed to make this test work: retail pharmacies **51.2%**
of the 2025 market and North America **54.4%**.

The denominator comes from the NCPA Digest, which publishes an independent-pharmacy count and
the share of US retail pharmacies that independents represent:

```
NCPA 2026 Digest (released 4 Oct 2026)
  Independent community pharmacies, 2026                      18,988
  (2025: 18,960 as of July 2025; June 2024: 18,984)
  Independents as a share of all US retail pharmacies         ~37%
  Independent pharmacy market size, 2025                      $103B, >96% of sales from Rx

Implied total US retail pharmacies = 18,988 / 0.37 =          ~51,319
```

*The ~51,319 total is **our own division** of NCPA's count by NCPA's own stated percentage.
NCPA publishes no total. The 2025 Digest put independents at "nearly 36%", the 2026 Digest at
"nearly 37 percent", which on the same numerator gives totals between 51,319 and 52,744 — a
2.8% range. Flagged as a derived figure.*

```
IMARC 2025 market                                            $7.20B
x retail pharmacies, 51.2%                                   $3.69B
x North America, 54.4%                                       $2.01B
/ ~51,319 US retail pharmacies                               $39,100 per pharmacy per year
```

Run the same arithmetic on the high end:

```
HTF 2025 market, $9.0B x 51.2% x 54.4% / 51,319            = $48,900 per pharmacy per year
```

**Both are in the right order of magnitude, and that rules the whole range in.** A counting and
dispensing robot is a six-figure capital item with a five-to-ten-year service life, so $39,000
to $49,000 a year of annualised hardware, software, service and consumable spend per store is
plausible — demanding for an independent pharmacy, routine for a chain location. This is the
one anchor in this batch that **confirms** rather than refutes the published range.

*Two caveats:* IMARC's "US 84.50% of the total market share" is ambiguous — of the global
market or of North America — and if it means of North America, the per-pharmacy figure rises
to about $33,000 after the US-only cut. And applying North America's 54.4% to a US-only
pharmacy count assigns Canadian and Mexican spend to US stores, which biases the figure up.
Both caveats push the same way: the true US per-store figure is somewhat lower than $39,100,
which strengthens rather than weakens the conclusion.

### Anchor 3 — dollars per hospital, and why the low CAGRs are the credible ones

```
AHA Fast Facts on U.S. Hospitals, 2026 edition (2024 AHA Annual Survey)
  Total US hospitals                                          6,100
  Community hospitals                                         5,121
  of which rural                                              1,797
(2025 edition, from the 2023 survey: 6,093 total / 5,112 community)
```

```
Omnicell FY2025 revenue, $1,185M / 6,100 US hospitals = $194,262 per US hospital per year
```

Omnicell is global and has a large retail, outpatient and central-fill business, so the true
per-US-hospital figure is well below $194,262. But the direction is the point: **automated
dispensing cabinets are already standard equipment in US hospitals.** BD's Pyxis MedStation ES
won a 2025 Best in KLAS award for Automated Dispensing Cabinets, which is an award category
that only exists because the product class is mature and competitively evaluated at every
hospital. 5,121 community hospitals is not a growing denominator — the 2025 and 2026 AHA
editions differ by 7 hospitals and 9 community hospitals respectively.

**An installed base that is already near-universal in a denominator that is flat grows by
replacement, software attach and price — not by penetration.** That is a 6% market, not a 12%
one. It is the structural argument for IMARC's 6.10% and Meticulous' 7.6% against HTF's 12.2%,
and it is corroborated by Omnicell's own numbers: 7% revenue growth in 2025, Q4 up just 2%,
and GAAP net income down from $13M to $2M. **A 12.2% market in which the pure-play grows 7%
and its Q4 grows 2% is not a 12.2% market.**

### Published figures that fail their own arithmetic

```
IMARC:      $7.2B (2025) -> $12.4B (2034) at a stated 6.10% (2026-2034)
            7.2 x 1.061^9 = 12.27,  not 12.4.  Endpoint implies 6.23%.  Gap: 0.13 points

Meticulous: 7.6% CAGR (2025-2032) -> $7.87B (2032), with NO published base.
            Back-solved base: 7.87 / 1.076^7 = $4.67B for 2025.
            A base-year value that is 35% below IMARC's and 48% below HTF's,
            and which the report does not print.
```

The Meticulous case is the more serious of the two. **A forecast published without a base-year
value cannot be audited**, and when the base is reverse-engineered from the publisher's own
rate and endpoint it turns out to be a third below everyone else's. That is not a forecasting
disagreement; it is an undisclosed starting point.

## What we publish

**Headline: $7.64B (2026) → $10.90B (2032) at 6.10%.**

Source: IMARC Group, *Pharmacy Automation Market*, report SR112026A1864, last updated
9 June 2026, which states **$7.2B for 2025, $12.4B for 2034 and a 6.10% CAGR over 2026–2034**,
with a historical period of 2020–2025.

The 2026 base is **derived by advancing that house's own published 2025 figure one year at its
own stated rate**: 7.2 × 1.061 = 7.64. The 2032 value is **interpolated on that same stated
rate**: 7.64 × 1.061^6 = 10.90. Labelled as interpolation in the report. We publish the stated
**rate** rather than the stated 2034 endpoint for the same reason as the vaccine cold chain
report: the two disagree (7.2 × 1.061^9 = 12.27 against a published 12.4, an implied 6.23%),
and our window ends inside their series rather than at its endpoint.

Chosen because: (a) it is the most recent edition in the set (June 2026) with a 2025 base and a
2026-forward window; (b) its scope and segmentation are fully disclosed, down to the end-user
and application splits, and — uniquely in this batch — it publishes the segment percentages
that **make the per-pharmacy grounding test possible at all**; (c) the per-pharmacy arithmetic
confirms it rather than refuting it, at roughly $39,100 per US retail pharmacy per year; and
(d) its 6.10% is the low end of the published range, and the installed-base argument and
Omnicell's own 7% revenue growth with 2% Q4 growth both argue that the low end is the credible
one.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Healthcare IT.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Omnicell discloses total revenue and BD discloses a segment that is
  wider than pharmacy automation; Swisslog Healthcare, ScriptPro, ARxIUM, Capsa, Yuyama and
  Willach disclose nothing. The 16.5% and 65.1% figures are our own division of disclosed
  revenue by a third party's published total, presented as prose with both inputs named and
  with the 65.1% explicitly labelled a ceiling.
  `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **IMARC's "North America 54.4% of 2025" and "US 84.50% of the total market share" as a
  regional analysis.** They are used inside the grounding arithmetic, where their purpose is to
  test the total and where the ambiguity in the US figure's wording is disclosed. They are not
  reproduced as a regional finding. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`, because a single
  unexplained percentage from one page — with the second one's denominator unclear on the page
  itself — is not a regional analysis.
- **Country splits.** IMARC names 24 countries in its coverage and publishes a figure for one,
  ambiguously. `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **IMARC's and GMI's segment shares** (dispensing systems 24.8%, retail pharmacies 51.2%,
  products 67.6%, medication dispensing cabinets 32.6%, North America 48.6% in 2022). Used in
  the arithmetic above; not published as findings. Segmentation goes out as plain strings.
- **A total count of US retail pharmacies as a cited figure.** NCPA publishes 18,988
  independents and a percentage; the ~51,319 total is **our own division** and the percentage
  moved from "nearly 36%" to "nearly 37 percent" between the 2025 and 2026 Digests, which gives
  a 2.8% range on the derived total. Labelled derived everywhere it appears.
- **A count of US hospital pharmacies.** AHA publishes hospital counts, not pharmacy counts.
  6,100 hospitals is the closest countable proxy and is labelled as a proxy; no hospital
  pharmacy count is asserted. ASHP and NABP are the right primary sources and we did not reach
  them.
- **BD's Medication Management Solutions revenue as a pharmacy automation figure.** It includes
  BD Alaris infusion pumps and wider medication-management software. The ~$3.5B is used only to
  construct a labelled upper bound, and it was read from a segment revenue chart rather than a
  10-K segment table.
- **Meticulous Research's $7.87B 2032 endpoint as comparable to the others.** It is in the
  estimates table and in the spread discussion, where its significance is that it is *below*
  another house's present. The back-solved $4.67B base is our own arithmetic and is labelled as
  such.
- **The central fill sub-scope figure ($1,471.57M by 2032).** Read from a commercial reseller
  listing rather than a research report. Recorded only to show the category is sliced.
- **Any medication-error-reduction or labour-saving figure.** Universally cited as a driver in
  this category's marketing literature; not publicly registered at a checkable level.
