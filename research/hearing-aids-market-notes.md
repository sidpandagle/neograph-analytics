## The spread

**At 2025, $8.8B (GMI) to $15.11B (Fortune, via secondary sites) — a 1.72x spread.** At 2032, $12.2B to $29.58B — 2.42x. Published CAGRs run 5.6% to 11.7%, a 2.09x spread, and the two Fortune-attributed versions disagree with each other.

For this corpus 1.72x is moderate, and the reason the houses are as close as they are is that most of them are measuring the same tier of the same industry. **That tier is the manufacturer, and none of them says so.** This is the whole finding of this report, and hearing aids is the market where it can be proved three independent ways, because this is the best-instrumented market in the batch: an industry trade body publishes unit shipments, four of the five major manufacturers publish revenue, one of them publishes its wholesale and retail revenue separately in the same statement, and a large buyer survey publishes measured transaction prices.

## Grounding arithmetic

**Registers used:** the European Hearing Instrument Manufacturers Association (EHIMA) global unit sales series; FY2025 and FY2025/26 reported revenue for Sonova, Demant, WS Audiology and GN Store Nord; HearingTracker's buyer price survey; and the FDA's over-the-counter hearing aid final rule.

### The unit register

EHIMA publishes global unit sales by its member companies — which is most of the industry, since Sonova, Demant, WS Audiology, GN and Starkey are all members:

| Year | Units sold globally by EHIMA members | Growth |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 21.81 million | +7.7% |
| 2024 | **22.69 million** | +4.0% |
| 2025 | **23.16 million** | **+2.1%** |

(EHIMA's own release describes the figure as member companies' **global** sales, not European sales; at least one trade outlet mis-reported it as European. **Flagged.** The series covers members only, so it is a floor on total world units.)

### Test 1 — price per unit, which identifies the tier

```
$8.8B  (GMI, 2025)                 ÷ 23.16M units = $380 per hearing aid
$10.35B (MarketsandMarkets, 2025)  ÷ 23.16M units = $447 per hearing aid
$15.11B (Fortune, 2025)            ÷ 23.16M units = $652 per hearing aid
```

Now the measured transaction prices. HearingTracker surveyed more than 1,100 buyers (figures dated 2026):

| Channel | Price per **pair** | Per aid |
|---|---|---|
| Measured overall average | **$2,694** | $1,347 |
| Over-the-counter | $502 | $251 |
| Prescription, private pay (includes Costco) | $3,432 | $1,716 |
| Traditional clinic, without insurance | $4,727 | $2,364 |
| Traditional clinic, with insurance | $2,567 | $1,284 |
| Costco | $1,674 | $837 |
| Comparable technology at a traditional clinic | over $5,225 | over $2,613 |

(Soundly separately cites a national average of around $6,500 for prescription devices; ConsumerAffairs gives $2,000–$8,000 prescription and $600–$1,000 OTC. **All read from search summaries of consumer-advice sites — flagged.** HearingTracker's is the only one based on a stated survey of actual buyers and is the figure used below. Its figures are dated 2026, one year after our price base — flagged.)

```
23.16M units × $1,347 per aid (HearingTracker's measured average transaction price)
= $31.2B of consumer spend on hearing aids

$31.2B ÷ $8.8B  (GMI)     = 3.5x
$31.2B ÷ $10.35B (M&M)    = 3.0x
$31.2B ÷ $15.11B (Fortune)= 2.1x
```

**$380 to $652 per hearing aid is a factory-gate price, not a retail price.** A reader who takes "the global hearing aids market is $8.8 billion" and imagines the money consumers spend on hearing aids is out by a factor of roughly three and a half. The published figures are manufacturer revenue. No publisher in the set states this.

### Test 2 — Demant proves the two-tier structure from inside one company

Demant A/S is the only company in this industry that publishes its wholesale and its retail revenue in the same statement, which makes the structure visible without any assumption at all. FY2025 (calendar), annual report:

| Demant A/S, 2025 | DKK million | 2024 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group revenue | **22,971** | 22,419 | +2% reported, +2% organic, +5% local currencies |
| **Hearing Aids — external revenue** (wholesale to third parties) | **9,841** | 10,022 | **-2% reported** |
| Hearing Aids — total including internal sales to Demant's own retail | 12,473 | 12,413 | +0.5% |
| **Hearing Care** (Demant's own retail clinics) | **10,724** | 9,932 | **+8% reported**, helped by acquisitions |
| EBIT before special items | 3,960 | — | 17.2% margin |

Two things fall out of that table:

```
Hearing Care (retail)  DKK 10,724M  >  Hearing Aids external (wholesale)  DKK 9,841M
```

**Inside the same company, retail dispensing revenue is larger than wholesale hearing-aid revenue.** A market definition that counts only the manufacturer tier captures less than half the money in a business that owns both ends of it.

```
Hearing Aids total DKK 12,473M - external DKK 9,841M = DKK 2,632M of internal sales
                                                        to Demant's own retail

Demant's own retail buys DKK 2,632M of aids at transfer price
and sells DKK 10,724M of hearing care       = 4.1x
```

(That 4.1x is not a pure device markup — Hearing Care revenue includes fitting, audiology services, accessories and batteries — but it is the right order for the gap Test 1 measured, and it comes from one company's own statement rather than from our arithmetic on two sources.)

### Test 3 — sum the disclosed revenue against the published market

Four of the five major manufacturers disclose. Conversions are **ours**, at rates implied by the USD figures the sources' own coverage printed alongside the local-currency ones (CHF 1 ≈ USD 1.272, EUR 1 ≈ USD 1.084, DKK 1 ≈ USD 0.1453), and are **flagged as approximate**.

| **Sum of the four** | | | **$11,769M** |

```
$11,769M ÷ $8,800M  (GMI, 2025)            = 1.34x   ← disclosed revenue EXCEEDS the
                                                       published market by a third
$11,769M ÷ $9,080M  (Ken Research, 2025)   = 1.30x
$11,769M ÷ $10,350M (MarketsandMarkets)    = 1.14x
```

**Four companies disclose more revenue than three of the five published markets contain** — and Starkey, the fifth major manufacturer, is privately held and not in the sum at all, while Amplifon, a pure hearing-aid retailer of substantial scale, sits outside every published definition of this market. (We do not size Amplifon: we found no figure for it in this research pass.)

### Test 4 — the reconciliation, which is where the finding becomes useful

The excess in Test 3 is not evidence that the publishers are wrong. It is evidence of what they are counting, and the reconciliation proves it. Sonova's and Demant's group revenue include **retail dispensing** — and Sonova's also includes cochlear implants. Strip retail out where it is disclosed:

```
Demant, Hearing Aids external (wholesale only)   DKK 9,841M  = $1,430M
GN Store Nord, Hearing division                  DKK 7,214M  = $1,048M
WS Audiology (predominantly wholesale)           EUR 2,579M  = $2,796M
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Disclosed, predominantly manufacturer-tier                     $5,274M

+ Sonova Wholesale   not separately disclosed in the FY2025/26 figures we could read
+ Starkey            privately held, no disclosure
```

Sonova renamed Hearing Instruments to **Wholesale** and Audiological Care to **Retail** with the FY2025/26 results and reported the combined Hearing Instruments segment at approximately **CHF 3.35B** (+7.5% in local currencies, +1.4% in CHF), with Wholesale growth **accelerating to 10.9% in local currencies in the second half** — but the full-year Wholesale figure was not in the material we were able to read. **Flagged.** If Sonova's Wholesale is of the order of $2.0–2.5B and Starkey of the order of $0.8–1.0B — both **our own estimates, not published figures, and used for nothing but this reconciliation** — the manufacturer tier totals roughly **$8.1–8.8B**.

**That is GMI's $8.8B almost exactly.** The published figures are the manufacturer tier, they are approximately right for that tier, and the consumer tier is about 3.5x larger. Both statements are true and neither is printed anywhere in the published research.

### Test 5 — units, prices and the published CAGR do not agree

```
EHIMA unit growth:    +7.7% (2023)  →  +4.0% (2024)  →  +2.1% (2025)     decelerating

Measured consumer price per pair:  $4,672 (2018)  →  $2,694 (2026)        -42%
                                   (HearingTracker survey)

Manufacturer revenue growth, 2025, as disclosed:
   Demant, Hearing Aids external   -2% reported
   WS Audiology                     0% organic (FY2024/25)
   GN Store Nord, Hearing          +5% organic
   Sonova, Wholesale               +7.5% local currencies (FY2025/26), H2 at +10.9%
   → weighted, roughly +2% to +3%

Published market CAGRs:            5.6%  to  11.7%
```

**Units are growing around 2%, measured consumer prices have fallen 42% since 2018, and the manufacturers' own disclosed growth is roughly 2–3% — against published forecasts of 5.6% to 11.7%.** The same publisher revised its own rate from 5.6% to 6.5% between editions while the industry decelerated.

There is a coherent story that reconciles part of this: manufacturer wholesale prices can hold while retail margins compress, so factory-gate revenue grows faster than consumer spend. Demant's own numbers show exactly that pressure, with wholesale external revenue **down 2%** while retail revenue rose **8%** on acquisitions, and Demant explicitly cited "slower-than-normal hearing aid market growth and intense competition" as drags on profitability. But that story supports a rate near the bottom of the published range, not the top, and nothing in the disclosed record supports double digits.

**The FDA rule is the mechanism behind the price collapse.** The over-the-counter hearing aid final rule was published **17 August 2022** (87 FR 50698), effective **17 October 2022**, creating an OTC category for air-conduction hearing aids not requiring surgical implantation, for adults 18 and over with perceived mild-to-moderate hearing loss, and repealing the prior conditions for sale. Devices already on the market had to comply by **14 April 2023**. In HearingTracker's survey, OTC pairs transact at **$502** against **$3,432** for a prescription private-pay pair — a 6.8x gap — and the measured overall average has fallen 42% since 2018. A regulatory change that cuts the consumer price of entry-level devices by most of an order of magnitude while unit growth decelerates to 2.1% is not a tailwind for a revenue CAGR, whatever the published reports say.

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope and plausibility tests. They establish that the published figures are manufacturer-tier revenue of roughly $380–$652 per hearing aid against measured consumer transactions of about $1,347 per aid; that four disclosures exceed three of the five published totals; that the excess reconciles once retail is stripped out, landing on the published manufacturer figure; and that unit growth, price trend and disclosed manufacturer growth all sit well below the published CAGR range. They do not establish the correct figure for either tier. All currency conversions are ours at approximate rates; HearingTracker's prices are dated 2026 against our 2025 price base and are a survey of self-selected buyers; EHIMA's series covers member companies only and is therefore a floor on world units; the Sonova Wholesale and Starkey figures inside Test 4's reconciliation are our own estimates and are published nowhere; and dividing company revenue into a published market total compares differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $9.37B (2026) → $13.67B (2032) at 6.5%.**

Source: Global Market Insights, *Hearing Aids Market*, report GMI5082, published **September 2025**, which states $8.3B for 2024, **$8.8B for 2025**, $15.5B for 2034 and a 6.5% CAGR for 2025–2034, with a published history of $7.2B (2021), $7.6B (2022) and $7.9B (2023). The 2026 base is **derived by advancing GMI's own published 2025 figure one year at GMI's own stated rate** (8.8 × 1.065 = 9.37). The 2032 endpoint is **derived by continuing that rate** (9.37 × 1.065^6 = 13.67). Both are labelled as interpolation in the report. Internal check: the implied rate between our endpoints is 6.50%, identical to GMI's stated rate.

Chosen because: (a) it carries a **stated publication date** and the **longest published history** in the set — five dated annual values from 2021 to 2025 — which makes its trajectory checkable rather than asserted; (b) it is the **only source read directly from the publisher's own page**, where every competing figure in the set came through a search summary or a secondary site; (c) its $8.8B for 2025 is the figure our Test 4 reconciliation lands on almost exactly, so it is the published series best supported by the disclosed record; and (d) 6.5% sits in the lower half of a range whose top end is contradicted by decelerating unit growth, a 42% fall in measured consumer prices and manufacturer revenue growing 2–3%.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Medical Devices.

The report states plainly in `market_context`, `forecast_analysis` and `research_methodology` that this is manufacturer-tier revenue of about $380 per hearing aid against measured consumer transactions near $1,347, that consumer spend on 23.16 million units is therefore roughly $31 billion, that Demant's own retail revenue exceeds its own wholesale revenue, and that unit growth has decelerated to 2.1%.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Forum and trade commentary puts Sonova at anywhere between 25% and 31% and Demant and WS Audiology each at 20–25%, with no method and no source in a filing. None of it is usable. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely. The ratios we do publish — four disclosures summing to 1.34x GMI's total, Demant's retail exceeding its own wholesale — are stated in prose with both inputs named.
- **GMI's regional figures** (North America 40% of 2024, U.S. $3.1B in 2024 at 4.9%, Europe $2.7B in 2024, Asia Pacific fastest at 7.8%). A single source's apportionment of a manufacturer-tier total, with no method stated, and for a base year two years before ours. `regional_analysis` and `country_analysis` stay `[]`.
- **GMI's distribution-channel split.** Its e-commerce figure of **$3.2B for 2024 — 38.6% of its own $8.3B total — is not credible** against an industry in which Demant's own retail clinic revenue is DKK 10,724M, Sonova operates a large Retail network and Amplifon is a pure clinic business. We record it here as a defect and publish nothing from it.
- **GMI's product-type values** (BTE 34.9% of 2024, RITE/RIC $2.4B, CIC/IIC $1.2B, ITC $623.3M, adult $7.7B, paediatric 8.5%). One source, one page, 2024 base. Segmentation goes out as plain strings.
- **A consumer-tier market size.** Our $31.2B is 23.16 million EHIMA member units multiplied by a 2026 survey average transaction price. It is a labelled plausibility calculation stated in prose and is never published as a market size: EHIMA's units are members-only, and a survey mean is not an ASP.
- **Sonova's Wholesale revenue and Starkey's revenue.** Sonova's FY2025/26 Wholesale figure was not in the material we could read, and Starkey is private. Our order-of-magnitude placeholders exist only inside the Test 4 reconciliation in these notes and appear nowhere in the report.
- **Amplifon's revenue.** We found no figure in this pass. Amplifon is named as a pure retailer outside every published definition, and not sized.
- **The two Fortune Business Insights series.** They disagree with each other ($29.58B at 10.1% versus $34.76B at 11.7%) and both were read from secondary sites rather than the publisher.
- **Any specific industry share for EHIMA's member coverage.** EHIMA does not publish what fraction of world units its members represent, so 23.16 million is used strictly as a floor.
- **The HearingTracker prices as current-year figures.** They are dated 2026 against our 2025 price base, and they are a survey of self-selected buyers. Used for the tier identification, labelled, and not published as prices.
