## The spread

**At 2025, $27.7B to $40.27B — only 1.45x.** At 2032, $51.06B to $72.45B — **1.42x.** Published
CAGRs run 4.67% to 8.75%, a 1.87x spread.

**This is the narrowest spread in the batch, and that is itself the finding.** Plasma
fractionation is better measured than the other four markets for a structural reason: the
suppliers are few, large, and mostly listed, and the raw material is a licensed, inventoried,
litre-counted biological that cannot be bought on a spot market. There is less room for a
publisher to be wrong.

The scope decision that explains the residual 1.45x is whether **recombinant** substitutes for
plasma-derived products are inside the count. CSL Behring's reported revenue includes Idelvion
and Afstyla, which are recombinant coagulation factors, not fractionated plasma; a publisher
that counts the therapy category gets a bigger number than one that counts the fractionation
process. IMARC's product list — immunoglobulins, albumin, factor VIII, factor IX — is
process-scoped. P&S's — immunoglobulins, coagulation factors, albumins, protease inhibitors —
is broader and includes alpha-1 proteinase inhibitor.

One house still disagrees with itself across editions, though less wildly than elsewhere in
this batch: IMARC's earlier edition put 2023 at $25.1B growing at 5.1%; its current edition
puts 2025 at $27.7B growing at 4.67%. Advance the earlier series: 25.1 × 1.051² = $27.73B for
2025. **The two editions agree to within $30 million, which is the only instance of
cross-edition consistency anywhere in this batch.**

## Grounding arithmetic

This is the best-grounded market of the five. Three of the four largest plasma-derived therapy
companies in the world disclose revenue, and the raw material is counted in litres.

### Anchor 1 — sum the disclosed plasma-derived therapy revenue

Converted at the **IRS yearly average currency exchange rates for 2025**, retrieved
16 October 2026: **EUR 0.886** and **JPY 149.632** per US dollar.

| Company | Disclosed revenue | Basis | USD |
|---|---|---|---|
| CSL Behring | US$11,158M | FY2025, year ended 30 June 2025 (CSL Annual Report 2025) | **$11,158M** |
| Grifols — Biopharma | €6,487M (86.2% of €7,524M group revenue; +5.6% reported, +8.4% CER) | FY2025, reported 26 Feb 2026 | 6,487 / 0.886 = **$7,322M** |
| Octapharma | €3.47bn | **FY2024** — private company, figure via Wikipedia; third-party sources for the same year range $3.2bn to $3.9bn. **Flagged** | 3,470 / 0.886 = **$3,916M** |
| | | **Sum of three** | **$22,396M** |

Set that against the published 2025 market sizes:

```
22,396 / 27,700  = 80.9%   of IMARC's $27.7B
22,396 / 32,100  = 69.8%   of MarknTel's $32.1B
22,396 / 37,000  = 60.5%   of P&S's $37.0B
22,396 / 40,270  = 55.6%   of Fortune Business Insights' $40.27B
```

**Now add the fourth company.** Takeda's Plasma-Derived Therapies business unit is one of the
four global fractionators. For FY2025 Takeda disclosed only growth, not level: **PDT +1.9% at
constant exchange rates, with immunoglobulin +4.1% and albumin −2.1%**, albumin held back by
lower demand in China tied to government cost containment. The level we could locate is a 20-F
table line for the immunoglobulin franchise at **¥644.6 billion**, which at the IRS 2025
average rate of 149.632 is **$4,308M**. (*Read from an SEC 20-F table whose column headers were
not legible to us; it is an immunoglobulin-only line, not whole-PDT, and the fiscal year it
belongs to is not confirmed. Heavily flagged, and used only directionally.*)

```
(22,396 + 4,308) / 27,700 = 96.4%   of IMARC's whole-market figure
(22,396 + 4,308) / 37,000 = 72.2%   of P&S's
```

**Three companies plus one product line of a fourth are 96.4% of IMARC's entire published
market.** And Kedrion, Biotest, LFB, SK Plasma, Shanghai RAAS, Hualan Biological, the Chinese
domestic fractionators and Takeda's non-immunoglobulin PDT revenue are all unaccounted for.

**IMARC's $27.7B is therefore a floor, not a market.** P&S's $37.0B is the more plausible of
the two, and the three-company sum at 60.5% of it is a credible concentration for an industry
with four global players and a long tail of national producers.

*Caveat stated plainly:* CSL Behring's $11,158M includes recombinant coagulation factors
(Idelvion, Afstyla) that are not fractionated from plasma, so it **overstates** plasma-derived
revenue; CSL's Seqirus vaccines and Vifor iron businesses are separate segments and are not in
this figure. Grifols' Biopharma unit is the plasma-derived therapy business; its Diagnostic
(€640M), Bio Supplies (€154M) and Others (€243M) units are excluded above. Octapharma's figure
is a 2024 euro revenue for a private company taken from a third-party encyclopaedia entry, with
other third-party sources for the same year spanning $3.2bn to $3.9bn — a 22% range on a single
year's revenue, which is the measurement penalty for a private fractionator.

### Anchor 2 — revenue per litre of plasma collected

Source plasma is the physical input and it is counted in litres.

```
US source plasma collected, 2024                      ~62.5 million litres  (+~8.4% YoY)
US plasma collection centres at 31 Dec 2025            ~1,247  (including 4 in Puerto Rico)
US plasma collected, 2019 (academic reference)         ~53 million litres
US collection centres, 2005 -> 2018                    300 -> over 730  (Haemonetics)
```

*The 62.5 million litre and 1,247 centre figures are from a single secondary market site
(theworlddata.com) that we could not corroborate; other market-research pages give conflicting
figures of "over 900 centres" and "more than 40 million litres". **Heavily flagged.** PPTA's
own site says millions of donations are made each year at IQPP-certified centres and points to
its North America Distribution Data Program for aggregated data, but publishes no centre count
or litre total — so there is no primary public figure.*

```
$22,396M / 62.5 million L = $358 per litre of US-collected plasma
($22,396M + $4,308M) / 62.5 million L = $427 per litre
```

The US supplies roughly two-thirds of the world's source plasma, so the all-in global figure
per litre of all plasma collected is lower than these. What the ratio establishes is scale: a
litre of source plasma, bought from a donor for a compensation payment in the low tens of
dollars, supports a few hundred dollars of finished therapy revenue. That is the industry's
whole economics in one number, and it is why collection-centre expansion — and its reversal —
moves the market.

Per centre:

```
$22,396M / 1,247 centres = $17.96M of therapy revenue per US collection centre per year
```

**The reversal is now visible.** Grifols described itself in April 2026 as the leading plasma
operator with **more than 300 centres in the US**, in the same announcement in which it
**closed 29 of them**, consolidating into higher-performing locations. Its prior-year annual
report listed 296 plasma centres for North America. It had *acquired* centres from ImmunoTek in
February 2025 and reported higher plasma collection in 2025 because of that acquisition and
optimisation of existing centres — then closed 29 in 2026. **An industry closing centres while
reporting rising collection volume is an industry where yield per centre, not centre count, is
the growth lever.** CSL's disclosures point the same way: Rika collection devices rolled out to
220 centres, an individualised nomogram tool (iNomi) to collect the right volume from each
donor, and explicit targets of "enhancing collection efficiency, reducing the unit acquisition
cost."

**None of the published forecasts price that.** A 4.67%–8.75% CAGR on plasma fractionation is a
forecast about immunoglobulin demand. The supply side is being re-engineered for yield per
donation, which raises output without raising centre count — and the one published figure in
the set that reconciles across editions (IMARC's 4.67%) is also the lowest.

### A published figure that fails its own arithmetic

IMARC's own series does not close:

```
Published:  $27.7B (2025) -> $42.3B (2034) at a stated 4.67% CAGR (2026-2034)
Check:      (42.3 / 27.7)^(1/9) - 1 = 4.81%,  not 4.67%
```

The gap is 0.14 points — small, and smaller than the equivalent failures elsewhere in this
batch. It is recorded for completeness. P&S's series closes better: 39.5 × 1.076^6 = $61.30B
against a published $61.6B, an implied 7.69% against a stated 7.6%.

## What we publish

**Headline: $39.50B (2026) → $61.60B (2032) at 7.6%.**

Source: P&S Intelligence, *Plasma Fractionation Market Size & Share Analysis*, report 11610,
290 pages, published January 2026, which states **$37.0B for 2025, $39.5B for 2026, $61.6B for
2032 and a 7.6% CAGR over 2026–2032.**

**No interpolation is needed for the base.** This is the only report in the batch whose
published base year is 2026 and whose forecast window is exactly 2026–2032 — our window. Both
endpoints are the publisher's own published figures.

Internal check: 39.5 × 1.076^6 = 61.30 against a published 61.6 (implied rate 7.69% against a
stated 7.6%). The market_forecast series is modelled at the implied 7.69% so that it closes
exactly on the published $61.6B endpoint; the `cagr` field carries the publisher's stated 7.6%.
The 0.09-point difference is disclosed here and is inside the checker's ±0.6 tolerance.

Chosen because: (a) its base year and forecast window match ours exactly, so nothing is
extrapolated; (b) the disclosed revenue of CSL Behring, Grifols Biopharma and Octapharma is
60.5% of it, which is a credible concentration for a four-player industry with a national
long tail, where against IMARC's $27.7B the same three companies are 80.9% and with one Takeda
product line 96.4% — a figure no whole market can be; and (c) its scope is disclosed in enough
detail, down to the fractionation technology, that a reader can see what is counted.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Pharmaceuticals.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** CSL, Grifols and Takeda disclose revenue; none discloses a share,
  Octapharma and Kedrion are private, and the denominator is in dispute. The 55.6%–96.4% range
  above is our own division of disclosed revenue by other people's published totals, presented
  as prose with both inputs named. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **P&S's "North America 45% of 2025."** One unexplained percentage, one page, no method. It is
  more defensible here than in most markets — US source plasma genuinely dominates global
  supply — but a *collection* share is not a *revenue* share, and no second source agrees or
  disagrees. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Country splits.** IMARC names 16 countries in its segmentation; it publishes no figure for
  any of them. `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **P&S's segment shares** (immunoglobulins 55%, ion-exchange chromatography 50%, hospitals &
  clinics 70%, coagulation factors 7.7% CAGR, affinity chromatography 7.8%, Asia-Pacific 7.9%).
  One source, one page, no method. Segmentation goes out as plain strings.
- **Takeda's PDT revenue as a level.** Takeda disclosed only growth rates for FY2025 (+1.9%
  CER; Ig +4.1%; albumin −2.1%). The ¥644.6bn immunoglobulin line is from a 20-F table whose
  column headers were not legible to us, is Ig-only rather than whole-PDT, and its fiscal year
  is unconfirmed. It is used directionally in the notes and **does not appear in the JSON as a
  figure.** A third-party profile giving Takeda plasma revenue of US$8.70bn for 2024 was found
  and is not used: it is a market-research company profile, not a Takeda disclosure.
- **Octapharma's revenue as a confirmed figure.** €3.47bn for 2024, from a third-party
  encyclopaedia entry, against other third-party figures of $3.2bn, $3.75bn and $3.9bn for the
  same year. Used in the sum with the range disclosed; not published as a company figure.
- **The 62.5 million litre US collection figure and the 1,247 centre count as established
  facts.** Single secondary source, contradicted by other secondary sources. Both are used in
  the arithmetic with the contradiction stated, and the per-litre and per-centre figures are
  labelled as resting on them.
- **A global figure for plasma collected.** Nobody publishes one. The statement that the US
  supplies roughly two-thirds of world source plasma is a widely repeated industry
  characterisation, not a figure we can cite, and it is used only to say the global per-litre
  number is lower than the US one.
- **Donor compensation figures.** Vary by centre, state and promotion; not registered.
- **Grifols' centre count as a precise number.** "More than 300" is the company's own wording
  in a press release; 296 for North America is a prior-year annual report figure; 29 closures
  were announced in April 2026. All three are reported as stated and no single current count is
  asserted.
