## The spread

**$77M to $1.8B at or around 2025 — a 23.4x spread** across the two halves of this slug's own name.

The structure of the gap is unusually legible:

- **Organ-on-chip alone, 2024–2025:** $77M, $98.2M, $100M, $153.2M — a 1.99x spread within a
  tight band. These houses are counting microfluidic devices, their consumables and the
  services run on them.
- **Organoids alone, 2023–2025:** $1.18B, $1.68B, $1.8B, $3.03B. These are 12–23x the
  organ-on-chip figures. They are counting 3D cell-culture consumables broadly — plates,
  scaffolds, hydrogels, matrices and media — which is a commodity reagents business with a
  very large installed customer base, not a device market.
- **Combined, 2025:** $500.6M, from the one house whose scope matches this slug.

### The combined figure fails an arithmetic test, and that is informative

A combined-scope figure must be at least as large as its largest component.

```
Astute Analytica, combined (organoids + chips + consumables + services), 2025:   $500.6M
Global Market Insights, organoids and spheroids alone, 2025:                      $1,800M
500.6 / 1,800 = 0.28x
```

**The only combined-scope estimate is 0.28x one house's organoid-only estimate.** Both cannot
be measuring what they say. Taking the component figures at face value instead:

```
Roots Analysis, organs-on-chips, 2025:                 $100M
Global Market Insights, organoids and spheroids, 2025: $1,800M
-----------------------------------------------------------------
implied combined                                       $1,900M
  of which chips:     100 / 1,900 =  5.3%
  of which organoids: 1,800 / 1,900 = 94.7%

Astute's combined figure as a fraction of that sum: 500.6 / 1,900 = 26.3%
```

So at least one of these three published numbers is wrong by a factor of **3.8x or more.**
The reconciling explanation, and the one the scope language supports, is a *use-case*
restriction rather than a product restriction: Astute limits itself to human-derived 3D and
microfluidic tissue models used for **drug testing, disease modelling and toxicology** and
excludes conventional two-dimensional culture, whereas the $1.8B organoid-and-spheroid figure
counts the whole 3D cell-culture consumables base, most of which is routine laboratory work
that has nothing to do with replacing an animal study.

**That distinction is the whole report.** The sector's narrative — that regulatory change is
about to substitute human tissue models for animal testing — applies only to the narrow,
qualification-grade use case. The large published numbers belong to the commodity reagents
business that narrative does not touch.

## Grounding arithmetic

The countable register here is regulatory, because regulatory substitution is the entire
demand-side thesis.

### What the register actually contains

**FDA's Innovative Science and Technology Approaches for New Drugs (ISTAND) programme** is the
Drug Development Tool qualification route a microphysiological system must travel to become
usable across submissions without re-justification in each IND. Qualification has three steps:
letter of intent, qualification plan, full qualification package.

| Count, as at the ISTAND page's content date of 22/09/2026 | Number |
|---|---|
| Organ-on-a-chip letters of intent accepted into ISTAND | **1** |
| Organ-on-a-chip models that have completed DDT qualification | **0** |

The one acceptance is **Emulate's Liver-Chip**, whose letter of intent CDER accepted on
**24 September 2024**, for assessing drug-induced liver injury risk in adults, with data
intended to support an investigational new drug application. It is step 1 of 3. The ISTAND
programme page itself lists no submission table and directs readers to the CDER and CBER DDT
Qualification Project Search database; its two spotlight announcements as at 22 September 2026
are a wearable-sensor outcome measure (22 September 2026) and the first in-silico
drug-induced-liver-injury tool (3 June 2026) — **neither is an organ chip.**

### The arithmetic

Astute Analytica's series implies, on its own base and rate:

```
2026 base (500.6 x 1.246)                 =   $623.8M
2032 (623.8 x 1.246^6)                    = $2,334.1M
net new revenue required, 2026-2032        = $1,710.3M
```

**$1.71 billion of net new revenue, against 1 accepted letter of intent and 0 qualified
organ-chip drug development tools.** Per accepted regulatory precedent, that is the entire
forecast increment resting on a single step-1 submission filed in September 2024 that had not,
two years later, produced a qualification.

That does not make the forecast wrong. Qualification is not a precondition for commercial use —
a sponsor can run a Liver-Chip study today and include the data in an IND voluntarily, and
contract research volume needs no FDA tool at all. But it does mean the forecast is a claim
about *how fast a qualification pipeline will fill*, not an extrapolation of anything
currently measurable.

### Being precise about what the FDA did in 2025, because the sector is not

On **10 April 2025** FDA announced a plan to **phase out the animal testing requirement** for
monoclonal antibodies and other drugs, replacing it with new approach methodologies (NAMs)
including AI-based toxicity models, cell lines and organoid testing. The precise content:

- It is a **roadmap**, phased, beginning with monoclonal antibodies and similar biologics
  before extending to other drug classes.
- It applies "immediately to investigational new drug applications" by **encouraging the
  inclusion of NAM data** — it does not require any specific model, does not qualify any
  specific organ chip or organoid, and does not prohibit animal studies.
- It commits FDA over three years to reviewing existing data, working with sponsors on NAM
  studies, and establishing an open-access drug-toxicity data repository.
- On efficacy, it says FDA will draw on existing human safety data from countries with
  comparable regulatory standards; at least one legal analysis notes FDA gives no further
  detail on how that would work.

**It is an encouragement, not a mandate.** The statutory authority it builds on is the
**FDA Modernization Act 2.0** (enacted late 2022), which removed the statutory *requirement*
for animal testing before human trials without requiring anything in its place.

**FDA Modernization Act 3.0 is not law.** As at the latest record located: the Senate passed
**S. 355** with an amendment by unanimous consent on **16 December 2025**; the House passed
**H.R. 2821** under suspension by voice vote on **20 July 2026**, and it was received in the
Senate on **21 July 2026**. The bill would direct FDA to issue an interim final rule within a
year of enactment replacing references to "animal" with "nonclinical" across the IND
regulations. It has passed each chamber in some form and has not been enacted.

Anyone reading a published 24–34% CAGR should know that the regulatory event underneath it is a
roadmap plus a bill in conference, not a requirement.

### Scale check against the literature

PubMed, retrieved 16 October 2026, term
`"organ-on-a-chip" OR "organ on a chip" OR "microphysiological system"`: **2,948** records.
For comparison, `"single-cell RNA sequencing"` returns **31,041** and
`"spatial transcriptomics"` returns **8,121**. The organ-chip literature is 9.5% the size of
the single-cell literature, while its published CAGRs (24–34%) are roughly double single-cell's
(12–15%). Recorded as scale, not as a denominator — publication counts are cumulative and a
paper is not a purchase.

**These are plausibility and consistency tests, not market estimates.** They establish that the
combined-scope and component-scope published figures cannot all be right, by at least 3.8x, and
that the regulatory substitution the forecasts rest on has produced one accepted letter of
intent and no qualified tool. They do not establish the correct market size.

## What we publish

**Headline: $623.8M (2026) → $2,334.1M (2032) at 24.6%.** Unit: USD **Million**.

Source: Astute Analytica, *Organoid and Organ-on-Chip Model Market*, last updated
15 September 2026, which states $500.6M for 2025, $4,515.3M for 2035 and a 24.6% CAGR over
2026–2035. The 2026 base is **derived by advancing that house's own published 2025 figure one
year at its own stated rate** (500.6 × 1.246 = 623.8) and the 2032 value by continuing that
rate (623.8 × 1.246^6 = 2,334.1). Labelled as interpolation in the report.

Internal check: Astute's own series closes almost exactly —
500.6 × 1.246^10 = $4,514.4M against its published $4,515.3M, a **0.02%** difference. By
contrast, IMARC's own page carries a $98.2M base for 2025 alongside a trends statement that the
technology is "projected to reach a market size of $220 million by 2025", and Roots Analysis
gives North America as both ~45% and ~42% on the same page.

Chosen because: (a) it is the **only** published series whose scope matches this slug —
organoid models and microphysiological systems together, with consumables and services, and an
explicit exclusion of conventional 2D culture and animal models; (b) its arithmetic is
internally consistent to 0.02%, which none of the alternatives is; and (c) its scope language
makes the use-case restriction explicit, which is what allows the 23.4x spread to be explained
rather than merely reported.

The report states plainly that this is the drug-testing, disease-modelling and toxicology use
case, and that houses counting the whole 3D cell-culture consumables base publish organoid-only
figures 3.6x higher.

Industry: Life Sciences.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Emulate, CN Bio Innovations, Mimetas, Nortis, InSphero, Hesperos
  and Vitro Biopharma are private and disclose no revenue; the organoid reagent suppliers
  (Thermo Fisher, Corning, Merck KGaA, STEMCELL Technologies) report no organoid line.
  `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **Roots Analysis's North America share.** The same page gives ~45% in its key takeaways and
  ~42% in its FAQ, for the organs-on-chips scope rather than this one. Two numbers on one page
  is not a regional analysis. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Country splits.** No named source gives any. `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Segment shares.** No source publishes a percentage split between organoids, chip devices,
  bioreactors, consumables and services. Segmentation goes out as plain strings.
- **A combined organoid-plus-chip figure built by adding two houses' numbers.** The $1,900M
  sum above is shown only to demonstrate that the published figures are mutually inconsistent;
  it is explicitly not offered as a market size and does not enter the JSON.
- **Any claim that FDA now requires, mandates or will require organ-chip or organoid data.**
  It does not. The April 2025 roadmap encourages NAM data in IND applications; the FDA
  Modernization Act 3.0 has passed each chamber in some form and is not law.
- **Emulate's, CN Bio's or Mimetas's revenue.** Not disclosed, not estimable.
- **A count of qualified organ-chip models greater than zero.** As at the ISTAND page's
  22 September 2026 content date, one letter of intent has been accepted (step 1 of 3) and no
  organ chip has completed qualification.
- **"Eight ISTAND submissions."** A sponsor-published page states this without a date; we
  could not confirm it against FDA, and the FDA ISTAND page lists no submission table at all.
  Not used.
- **Animal-use reduction figures, cost-per-study savings, or avoided-attrition estimates.**
  Widely quoted in advocacy material, not publicly registered at a checkable level.
