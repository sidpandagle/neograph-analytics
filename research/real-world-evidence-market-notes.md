## The spread

- **Base-year values for 2025: $2.0B (IMARC) to $20.03B (Fortune Business
  Insights) — a 10.0x spread.** Restricting to the three houses that cluster,
  $2.0B to $3.5B, the spread is 1.75x.
- **Published CAGRs: 7.01% to 16.3% — a 2.32x spread.** This is unusually wide.
  Unlike most markets in this corpus, the houses disagree about growth as well as
  about scope.

The scope decision that explains the 10x gap is identifiable. The cluster houses
size **solutions sold by vendors** — platforms, datasets, analytics and study
services. $20.03B can only be the **total cost of generating real-world
evidence**, including pharmaceutical companies' own internal epidemiology,
HEOR and medical-affairs headcount, which is spend but is nobody's revenue. The
distinction matters commercially: a vendor sizing its addressable market off
$20.03B is counting its customers' salary bill.

A second, subtler problem: nobody agrees whether late-phase and post-approval
interventional research belongs in RWE. IQVIA's own 2026 reorganisation decided
that it does not — see below.

## Grounding arithmetic

**Register used:** a company's own disclosed revenue, which in this market is
better than any register, because the largest participant publishes a
real-world-specific line item.

IQVIA's fourth-quarter and full-year 2025 results (5 February 2026) report:

| IQVIA FY2025 | Revenue |
|---|---|
| Total company | $16,310M |
| Research & Development Solutions (R&DS) | $8,896M |
| **Technology & Analytics Solutions (TAS)** | **$6,626M** |
| Contract Sales & Medical Solutions (CSMS) | $788M |
| **Real-World Late Phase and certain other Real-World offerings** | **$674M** |

That last line exists because, effective 1 January 2026, IQVIA moved Real-World
Late Phase and certain other Real-World offerings — *"representing $674 million
in 2025 revenue"* — out of TAS and into R&DS, folded CSMS into TAS and renamed
TAS "Commercial Solutions". The reorganisation accidentally published the single
most useful number in this market.

### Test 1 — one vendor's real-world line against the whole market

```
$674M (IQVIA Real-World Late Phase + certain other Real-World, FY2025)
/ $3,100M (GMI's entire global market, 2026)
= 21.7%
```

One disclosed sub-line of one company is more than a fifth of the entire global
market as the most clearly scoped house sizes it. That is high but not
impossible — IQVIA is the dominant participant — and it sets a floor: any
whole-market figure materially below about $3B cannot be right.

### Test 2 — the low estimate

```
$6,626M (IQVIA's whole Technology & Analytics Solutions segment, FY2025)
/ $2,000M (IMARC's entire global market, 2025)
= 3.31x
```

**One company's segment is 3.3 times the whole market as IMARC sizes it.** TAS
is broader than RWE — it carries information offerings, commercial analytics and
technology — so this is not a share calculation. It is a bound. A $2.0B global
market is not reconcilable with a $6.6B segment from a single participant whose
real-world business is only part of that segment. Since $674M of that segment
*was* explicitly real-world, $2.0B implies IQVIA alone holds 34% of the world
market from one reporting line, before TriNetX, Flatiron, Komodo, Veradigm,
Optum and HealthVerity are counted at all.

### Test 3 — the high estimate

```
$20,030M (Fortune Business Insights, 2025, global)
/ $6,626M (IQVIA's whole TAS segment)
= 3.02x
```

Fortune's market is three times the whole technology-and-analytics segment of
the sector's largest company. That is only coherent if the figure counts
pharmaceutical sponsors' internal evidence-generation cost alongside vendor
revenue. It is not a vendor-addressable market.

### Supporting count

ClinicalTrials.gov, API v2, retrieved **16 October 2026**: **675** of 607,160
registered studies text-match `"real-world evidence"` (0.11%). Useful only as a
reminder that most real-world evidence is generated outside the trial register
entirely, which is the definitional point of the field.

**These are plausibility tests, not market estimates.** They bracket the honest
answer between roughly $3B and something well below $20B. They do not establish
the correct size.

## What we publish

**Headline:** $3.10B (2026) → $7.67B (2032) at 16.3%.

Source: Global Market Insights report GMI9187, February 2026, which publishes
$3.1B for 2026 and $11.9B for 2035 at a stated 16.3% CAGR over 2026–2035. The
2032 endpoint of $7.67B is the published 2026 value carried forward six years at
that published rate ($3.10B × 1.163⁶ = $7.67B). It is interpolation on a
published rate, not a separately published observation, and is labelled as such.

Chosen because GMI is the only house in the set that **states what it counts**
and **names its data sources**, it is current (February 2026), it publishes a
multi-year series rather than two endpoints, and it is the only series that
survives all three grounding tests.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Healthcare IT.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** IQVIA is the only participant with a disclosed
  real-world line, and even that line changed segments in 2026. TriNetX, Komodo
  Health and HealthVerity are private. Flatiron Health is inside Roche with no
  separate disclosure. Veradigm was delisted from Nasdaq for non-compliance with
  the timely-filing rule — the Hearings Panel decided to delist on 27 February
  2024 and Nasdaq filed Form 25 on 25 April 2024 — and it filed a Form NT 10-K
  for FY2025 on 17 March 2026, so it is still not current in its periodic
  reporting. No denominator-and-numerator pair exists for anyone.
  `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted.
- **A share figure derived from the $674M / $3,100M arithmetic.** It belongs in
  prose, labelled as our own division of two differently scoped published
  numbers, because the numerator is one company's sub-line and the denominator
  is another house's whole market. It is not a market share.
- **Regional splits.** GMI publishes North America 43.3% of 2025, Europe
  $719.6M and Asia Pacific $548.5M; Fortune publishes North America 42.11%.
  We did not inspect either method and the two scopes differ by ten times, so
  apportioning would be meaningless. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Country splits.** `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **The Meticulous Research figures.** Two pages for the same report ID publish
  $3.5B/2025 → $8.9B/2036 at 12.2% and $6.42B/2026 → $12.84B/2036 at 7.2%.
  Recorded in the spread table as internally inconsistent; never averaged in.
- **The Fortune Business Insights series as a vendor-addressable market.**
  Recorded as the high bound, explained, not used.
- **GMI's component and deployment percentages** (services 58.4%, cloud 86.4%,
  pay-per-use 65.2%, pharma/device end use 60.5%). One house's unaudited
  apportionment. `market_segmentation` carries plain strings.
