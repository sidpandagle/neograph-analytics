## The spread

**At 2024, $10.11B (Credence) to $35.6B (DelveInsight) — a 3.52x spread.** Carried to 2025 at each source's own rate, $11.66B to $37.44B — 3.21x. At 2032, $31.57B to $53.0B — 1.68x. Published CAGRs run 5.17% to 15.3%, a **2.96x spread**, and they run in the wrong direction: the house with the lowest base claims the highest growth and the house with the highest base claims the lowest, which is what happens when two publishers are reverse-engineering a rate to reach a predetermined 2032 number from incompatible starting points.

The scope decision that produces the gap is visible in GMI's own taxonomy. GMI's $20.00B for 2025 breaks down as:

| GMI segment, 2025 | Value |
|---|---|
| Insulin pens (38% of total) | $7.60B |
| Insulin pumps | $6.50B |
| — tubed pumps | $3.50B |
| — tubeless/patch pumps (15% of total) | $3.00B |
| Pen needles | $3.00B |
| Insulin syringes (8% of total) | $1.60B |
| Pump consumables | $0.90B |
| Testing strips (2% of total) | $0.40B |
| **Total** | **$20.00B** |

That sums exactly, and so does GMI's regional table for the same year (North America $8.80B, Europe 28% = $5.60B, Asia Pacific $4.00B, Latin America 5% = $1.00B, Middle East & Africa $0.60B = $20.00B). GMI is internally consistent, which is more than most sources in this corpus manage, and it is why we use it.

But notice what is inside the total: **$5.00B of pen needles, syringes and testing strips** — commodity disposables with 2–2.5% growth on GMI's own figures — alongside **$6.50B of pumps**, whose vendors grew 10% to 54% in FY2025. A single 7.9% rate blends a stagnant commodity business with a fast-growing capital-and-consumable one. Any reader applying 7.9% to either half will be wrong in opposite directions.

A sharper way to say it: **the spread is a question about whether needles and strips are an "insulin delivery device".** DelveInsight's ~$35.6B almost certainly says yes and adds more besides; Credence's $10.11B — on a page that also discusses asthma inhalers — looks like a narrower device count. Nobody says which.

### Test 1 — the pump consumables segment cannot hold Insulet

Insulet sells one thing: disposable Omnipod pods. There is no capital pump — the controller is an app on the user's phone or a low-cost handset — so essentially the whole of Insulet's $2.7B of Omnipod revenue is **consumable**.

```
$2,700M  (Insulet Omnipod revenue, FY2025)
÷ $900M  (GMI's entire GLOBAL "pump consumables" segment, 2025)
= 3.0x
```

**One company's consumable revenue is three times the world's pump-consumables segment.** The only way out is to classify pods as "tubeless/patch pumps" instead, which GMI sizes at 15% of its 2025 total, or $3.00B. On that reading:

```
$2,700M ÷ $3,000M = 90% of the global tubeless/patch pump segment is Insulet
```

That leaves **$300M** for Ypsomed's mylife, Medtrum, Equil, Sequel, SOOIL and every Chinese and Indian patch-pump vendor combined. It is not impossible — Omnipod genuinely is the patch-pump category — but it means GMI's taxonomy has only one coherent place to put Insulet, and a $900M global pump-consumables line that excludes the world's largest pump consumable.

### Test 2 — disclosed revenue against the whole pump side of the market

GMI's pump-attributable total for 2025 is **$6.50B of pumps plus $0.90B of pump consumables = $7.40B.**

```
Insulet              $2,734M   (Omnipod $2,700M + Drug Delivery $34M)
Tandem               $1,015M
Beta Bionics           $100M
---------------------------------
Pure-play subtotal   $3,849M   = 52.0% of $7,400M

+ Medtronic Diabetes $2,755M   (INCLUDES CGM — an upper bound, not a pump figure)
+ Ypsomed Diabetes Care ~$301M (CHF 236.9M at CHF 1 ≈ USD 1.27, our conversion, FY2024/25)
---------------------------------
Five-company total   $6,905M   = 93.3% of $7,400M
```

Three pure-play pump companies, none of which is the global installed-base leader, already account for **52%** of the entire pump side of the published market. Add the leader and the fifth vendor and **93.3% of it is consumed by five disclosures**, leaving about **$495M** for Roche's Accu-Chek portfolio, Sequel Med Tech, Medtrum, Equil, SOOIL, every third-party infusion set and every Chinese, Indian and Latin American pump vendor.

The Medtronic figure is the honest weakness here: it includes continuous glucose monitoring revenue, which GMI does not count in this market at all. So $6,905M overstates what is pump-attributable. But the pure-play subtotal does not have that problem, and **$3,849M from three companies against a $7,400M global pump market is already the finding**: Medtronic's installed pump base is larger than any of theirs, and once it is added at any plausible pump-only figure the segment is full.

### Test 3 — revenue per patient, which two companies make directly checkable

This is the rarest thing in medical device research: companies publishing revenue and patient counts in the same release.

```
Insulet:        $2,700M ÷ >600,000 active Omnipod users  = $4,500 per user per year (an upper
                                                           bound: the user count is a floor)
Beta Bionics:   $100.3M ÷ 35,011 users                   = $2,865 per user per year
Tandem:         $1,015M ÷ >300,000 U.S. supply-ordering customers = ~$3,383 (ignores
                                                           international customers, so high)
```

(The spread between $2,865 and $4,500 is itself informative: Insulet's figure is higher because a pod is a 3-day disposable, while Beta Bionics' and Tandem's revenue mixes durable pumps with infusion sets and cartridges.)

Now invert the published market:

```
$7,400M (GMI pumps + pump consumables, 2025) ÷ $4,500/user  = 1.64 million pump users worldwide
$7,400M                                       ÷ $2,865/user = 2.58 million pump users worldwide
```

**GMI's pump-side total implies a global pump-using population of roughly 1.6 to 2.6 million people.** That is the right order of magnitude for insulin pump therapy worldwide, and it is a far more useful statement than the dollar figure, because a reader can check it against their own clinical knowledge in a way they cannot check $7.4 billion.

### Test 4 — against the population denominator

The IDF Diabetes Atlas 11th edition (published 7 April 2025 at the World Diabetes Congress, Bangkok) puts **589 million adults aged 20–79 living with diabetes**, of whom **252 million are undiagnosed**, rising to a projected 853 million by 2050.

```
$20.00B (GMI total insulin delivery devices, 2025) ÷ 589 million adults with diabetes
= $34 per person with diabetes per year

1.6-2.6 million pump users ÷ 589 million adults with diabetes
= 0.27% - 0.44%
```

**Thirty-four dollars a year per person with diabetes** is what a device market looks like when the overwhelming majority of insulin in the world is delivered by a disposable syringe or a basic reusable pen, and when 252 million people do not know they have the disease. The pump business that produces all of the growth and all of the press releases reaches **under half of one percent** of the diabetic population. Every forecast in this market is, implicitly, a claim about how fast that fraction moves.

### Test 5 — the single CAGR describes nothing

```
FY2025 reported growth, the companies:        FY2025 growth, GMI's own commodity segments:
  Beta Bionics      +54%                        Insulin syringes       +2.5% (2026-2035 CAGR)
  Insulet           +31.6%                      Testing strips         +2.0% of total, declining share
  Tandem            +12%                         Pen needles           $3.00B to $4.68B = +4.5%/yr
  Medtronic Diabetes +10.7%
```

GMI's blended 7.9% sits between a pump business compounding at 11% to 54% and a needles-syringes-strips business compounding at 2% to 4.5%. It is arithmetically defensible and operationally meaningless: no company in this market experiences 7.9%.

**And there is a measurement hazard inside the revenue series itself.** Tandem is moving U.S. customers to a pay-as-you-go pharmacy model with no upfront pump reimbursement. Its own 2026 guidance of $1.065–1.085B includes **$85–95M of headwind from the business-model change**, while management points to U.S. pump shipments — guided up 10–11% — as the metric that matters. Reported revenue can fall while patients served rises. Beta Bionics shows the same migration from the other side: pharmacy-channel sales rose 295% in Q4 to $9.8M and the pharmacy share of new patient starts went from a high single-digit percentage to the high twenties in one year. **A market measured in dollars is about to understate a market measured in patients.**

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope and plausibility tests. They establish that GMI's pump-consumables taxonomy cannot accommodate the world's largest pump consumable, that five disclosures consume 93% of the published pump side, that the implied global pump population of 1.6–2.6 million is plausible, that pump therapy reaches under half a percent of people with diabetes, and that a single blended CAGR misdescribes both halves of the market. They do not establish the correct figure. Medtronic's Diabetes revenue includes CGM and is therefore an upper bound on its pump contribution; Insulet's ">600,000" and Tandem's ">300,000" are floors, so the per-user figures derived from them are upper bounds; the Swiss franc conversion is ours; and dividing company revenue into a published market total compares differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $21.90B (2026) → $34.56B (2032) at 7.9%.**

Source: Global Market Insights, *Insulin Delivery Devices Market*, report GMI1649, published **October 2026**, which states $18.50B for 2024, $20.00B for 2025, **$21.90B for 2026**, $42.96B for 2035 and a 7.9% CAGR for 2026–2035. The 2026 base is GMI's own published 2026 figure, unaltered. The 2032 endpoint is **derived by compounding that figure at GMI's own stated rate** (21.90 × 1.079^6 = 34.56) and is labelled as interpolation in the report. Internal check: the implied rate between our endpoints is 7.90%, identical to GMI's stated rate.

Chosen because: (a) it is **the only source in the set that is internally consistent** — its 2025 product segments sum exactly to its 2025 total, and so does its 2025 regional table, which no other source here can demonstrate; (b) it is **the most recently published** (October 2026) with a dated base year; (c) it publishes the **most explicit taxonomy**, which is what made it possible to find the pump-consumables defect — a vaguer source could not have been checked at all; and (d) its $20.00B for 2025 sits in the middle of a 3.2x spread, above a Credence page carrying content about asthma inhalers and below a DelveInsight figure nearly twice as large.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Medical Devices.

The report states plainly in `market_context` and `forecast_analysis` that the published total is roughly $34 per person with diabetes per year, that pumps reach under half a percent of the diabetic population, that the single 7.9% rate blends a 2% commodity business with an 11–54% pump business, and that the pay-as-you-go pharmacy transition means reported revenue will understate patients served.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** No company discloses insulin-delivery share, and the disclosures that exist are not comparable: Insulet's revenue is pods, Medtronic's includes CGM, Tandem's is being restructured by a pricing-model change. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **GMI's regional figures** (North America $8.80B and U.S. $7.92B for 2025, Europe 28%, Asia Pacific $4.00B, Germany $1.09B, China $1.32B, Japan $840M, Latin America 5%, Middle East & Africa $600M). They are a single source's apportionment with no stated method, and although they sum correctly that demonstrates internal consistency, not sourcing. `regional_analysis` and `country_analysis` stay `[]`.
- **GMI's segment dollar values and percentages** (pumps $6.50B, tubed $3.50B, tubeless 15%, pens 38%, pen needles $3.00B, syringes 8%, pump consumables $900M, strips 2%). Used in the notes to run the grounding tests and quoted in prose where the arithmetic is the point, never published as a segmentation structure. Segmentation goes out as plain strings.
- **A global insulin pump patient count.** Our $7.40B ÷ per-user arithmetic brackets it at 1.6–2.6 million. That is a plausibility range published as such in prose, never as a count.
- **Tandem's installed base.** The ">500,000 worldwide including ~325,000 in the U.S." figure comes from an analyst summary and could not be confirmed in Tandem's own release. We publish only what Tandem states: more than 126,000 pumps shipped in 2025 and more than 300,000 U.S. customers regularly ordering supplies.
- **A pump-only figure for Medtronic.** Its $2.755B Diabetes revenue includes CGM and the company does not split it. Used only as a labelled upper bound.
- **Ypsomed's current Diabetes Care revenue.** The CHF 236.9M figure is FY2024/25, the last year it was a reported segment before the business was sold. Labelled as such, and the Swiss franc conversion is ours.
- **Insulin prevalence or insulin-user counts.** The IDF Diabetes Atlas gives 589 million adults with diabetes and 252 million undiagnosed; it does not publish a count of people using insulin, and we do not estimate one.
- **Credence's, DelveInsight's, SkyQuest's, MarketsandMarkets' or Grand View's series as anything but spread.** Four of the five were read from search summaries; the one read directly carries content about asthma inhalers and cystic fibrosis.
