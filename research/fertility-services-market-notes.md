## The spread

**At 2025, $39.27B to $51.9B — only 1.32x** across the three current whole-market series.
Published CAGRs run **6.67% to 12.15%, a 1.82x spread**. The narrower-scope IVF-only estimate sits
at $28.35B for 2024, which is a scope difference and not a disagreement.

1.32x is narrow, and the reason is the same as in plasma fractionation and ophthalmics: this is a
counted industry. **Cycles are counted by national registries, clinics are licensed and listed, and
one US buyer publishes its own cycle volume alongside its own revenue.** There is little room to be
wrong about the volume. The room to be wrong is entirely in what you multiply it by, and in what
you include.

**But this market offers something the others do not: two published forecasts of 2025 made in 2018
and 2019, which we can now mark.**

```
Progressive Markets, August 2018:   IVF services to reach  $23.2B  by 2025
Global Market Insights, 2019:       IVF services to exceed $17.3B  by 2025

Current published figures for 2025: fertility services     $39.3B - $51.9B
                                    IVF services (2024)     $28.4B
```

The 2018 forecast for IVF services in 2025 was $23.2B; the current IVF-services estimate for 2024
is $28.4B. **Those are roughly compatible — the 2018 forecast was approximately right about IVF
services.** What has changed by 1.7x to 3.0x is not the market. It is the **word "fertility"**,
which now carries surrogacy, donor gametes, egg and embryo banking, cryobanks and fertility drugs
alongside the clinical procedure.

**So the headline growth in this category has come substantially from scope widening, and the way
to test any figure is per cycle.** That is what the registers allow, and the arithmetic below does
it with a measured rate rather than an assumed one.

## Grounding arithmetic

This is the best-instrumented market in this batch. Four public registries count cycles and
clinics; a listed US fertility benefits manager publishes **its own ART cycle count beside its own
revenue, split between services and drugs**; and a listed clinic operator outside the US discloses
revenue and cycle direction.

Conversions are at the **IRS yearly average currency exchange rates for 2025** — the source used in
round 3 of this corpus and reused here — **GBP 0.759** and **AUD 1.551** per US dollar, retrieved
16 October 2026.

### Test 1 — revenue per cycle, measured, against published list prices

This is the decisive test the brief asks for, and it needs no assumption, because Progyny publishes
revenue and cycles in the same release:

```
Fertility benefit services  $830.9M   / 65,006 ART cycles  =  $12,782 per cycle  (services)
Pharmacy benefit services   $457.7M   / 65,006 ART cycles  =   $7,041 per cycle  (drugs)
Total revenue             $1,288.7M   / 65,006 ART cycles  =  $19,824 per cycle  (all-in)

Split: services 64.5%  |  drugs 35.5%
```

Against published US list prices: a full IVF cycle including clinic procedures, bloodwork, fertility
medications and embryo genetic testing is commonly put at **$15,000 to $30,000**, with stimulation
drugs at **$3,000 to $5,000**, egg retrieval at **$2,500 to $4,000** and embryo freezing at **$800
to $1,500**.

**$19,824 lands inside that range, and the composition matches too** — Progyny's measured $7,041 of
drugs per cycle sits above the $3,000–5,000 stimulation-drug line, which is what you would expect
from a pharmacy benefit covering more than stimulation. **The per-cycle anchor validates.**

(Two caveats, both stated. Progyny's cycle count mixes fresh IVF, freeze-all, frozen embryo
transfers and egg freezing, and FETs and egg freezing cost far less than a full fresh cycle — so
$19,824 is a **blended rate across cycle types**, not the price of an IVF cycle. That is the right
comparison for CDC's 435,426, which is also a blended count, 42.4% of it banking cycles. And
Progyny is a **benefits manager negotiating rates on behalf of 6.7 million covered members**, so its
per-cycle economics sit below cash-pay list prices. The list-price range comes from a consumer
health guide, not a primary cost study. **Flagged.**)

### Test 2 — total United States ART spend

```
435,426 CDC cycles x $19,824 (Progyny blended all-in)       =  $8.63B   US ART spend, incl. drugs
435,426 CDC cycles x $12,782 (Progyny services only)        =  $5.57B   US ART clinical services
435,426 CDC cycles x $15,000 (bottom of the list range)     =  $6.53B
435,426 CDC cycles x $30,000 (top of the list range)        = $13.06B
```

**So the whole United States ART industry — the most expensive market in the world, 20.5% of
published registry cycles — turns over somewhere between $6.5 billion and $13.1 billion, with the
measured rate giving $8.63 billion.**

### Test 3 — the upper bound on world ART services, which is the decisive one

Now build the most generous possible estimate of world ART clinical services: take the published
registry floor of 2,128,848 cycles and bill **every one of them at the United States rate**. This is
deliberately rigged in the published figures' favour, because a cycle in Spain, Czechia, Poland,
India or Japan costs a fraction of a US cycle — UK survey data put a private cycle at **£11,950
all-in ($15,740)** and a self-funded cycle at an NHS facility at **£6,990 ($9,210)**, and Japan's
cycles have been largely publicly subsidised since 2022.

```
2,128,848 cycles x $12,782 (US services-only rate)  =  $27.21B   upper bound, world ART services
2,128,848 cycles x $19,824 (US all-in rate)         =  $42.20B   upper bound, world ART + drugs

$27.21B / $51.9B  (IMARC, 2025)   =  52.4%
$42.20B / $51.9B  (IMARC, 2025)   =  81.3%
$27.21B / $39.27B (Astute, 2025)  =  69.3%
```

**At most half of the published "fertility services market" can be ART clinical services — and that
is on an assumption that prices every cycle in Europe, Japan and Australasia at the American rate.**
On any realistic weighting the share is well under half.

That is not a defect in the published figures. **It is a definition that nobody reading the headline
would guess**, and IMARC's own scope table says so if you read it: the procedure list includes
**surrogacy**, the service list includes **egg and embryo banking**, and the end-user list includes
**cryobanks**. A single US surrogacy arrangement runs into six figures. Add fertility drugs — which
Progyny measures at **35.5% of per-cycle spend** — and donor gametes and genetic testing, and the
residual is accounted for.

**The correct reading is therefore: an ART clinical-services market of roughly $20–27 billion
worldwide sitting inside a published "fertility services" total of $39–52 billion.**

### Test 4 — revenue per clinic

```
$8.63B US ART spend / 457 CDC-reporting clinics      =  $18.9M per US clinic per year
435,426 cycles      / 457 clinics                    =     953 cycles per US clinic
1,156,316 cycles    / 1,473 European centres         =     785 cycles per European centre

$51.9B (IMARC) / 1,930 clinics (457 US + 1,473 European)  =  $26.9M per clinic
```

**$26.9 million per clinic exceeds the measured US figure of $18.9 million**, even though European
centres run 18% fewer cycles each at substantially lower prices, and even though 1,930 counts only
US and European clinics while the $51.9B is global. **Points the same way as Test 3**: the published
total is larger than clinic revenue can account for, because much of it is not clinic revenue.

(The per-clinic figures are averages across a heavily skewed distribution — large US networks run
thousands of cycles a year while small clinics run under 200 — so they are order-of-magnitude checks
and nothing more.)

### Test 5 — the trajectory, against a 12.15% published CAGR

```
VOLUME, where it is counted:
  US cycles, 2020 -> 2022              326,468 -> 435,426    +33.4%  (off a COVID-depressed base)
  US banking cycles, share of 2022     184,423 of 435,426    42.4%
  UK egg freezing cycles, 2022 -> 2023   4,700 -> 6,900      +47%    (7% of all UK cycles)
  Europe, 2020 -> 2023 (prelim)        923,318 -> 1,156,316  +25%    (country count 41 -> 36, NOT comparable)

DISCLOSED COMPANY VOLUME:
  Progyny ART cycles, 2024 -> 2025      61,114 -> 65,006     +6.4%
  Progyny all-member utilisation          1.31% -> 1.32%     flat
  Progyny FEMALE-ONLY utilisation         1.07% -> 1.04%     DOWN
  Progyny clients                          473 -> 555        +17.3%
  Monash IVF domestic stimulated cycles, FY25               -5.0%
  Australian industry stimulated cycles, FY25               -1.7%
  Monash IVF international stimulated cycles, FY25          -6.4%

DISCLOSED COMPANY REVENUE:
  Progyny total revenue                                     +10%  (+20% ex-transitioned client)
  Monash IVF revenue, FY25                                  +6.7%
  Monash IVF revenue, FY26                 A$271.9M -> A$269.5M    DOWN
  Monash IVF statutory NPAT, FY26                           -67.6%

PUBLISHED CAGRs:                                     6.67%  9.4%  12.15%
```

Two things are true at once, and they are not in tension once you see what is growing:

1. **Real volume growth is concentrated in banking, not treatment.** 42.4% of US ART cycles in 2022
   were egg or embryo banking cycles in which everything was frozen for later — **nearly half of all
   American ART cycles made no attempt at a pregnancy at all.** UK egg freezing rose 47% in one year
   to 7% of cycles. That is a genuinely fast-growing, discretionary, cash-pay business, and it is
   where a double-digit CAGR can live.
2. **Treatment-cycle growth in mature markets is low single digit or negative.** Progyny's cycles
   grew 6.4% while utilisation was flat and female-only utilisation *fell* — its revenue growth came
   from signing clients (473 to 555), not from the market growing. Monash IVF's domestic cycles fell
   5.0% against an Australian industry down 1.7%, its international cycles fell 6.4%, and its FY26
   revenue was lower than FY25.

**12.15% is therefore defensible only as a blend in which banking, surrogacy, donor gametes and
drugs grow fast while clinical treatment volume in mature markets grows at low single digits.** It
is not a statement about IVF. And it carries a specific policy risk the publishers do not price:
**the NHS-funded share of UK IVF cycles fell from 35% in 2019 to 27% in 2023**, which is demand
migrating to self-pay rather than demand growing — good for revenue, and dependent on households
continuing to fund it.

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope, composition and plausibility tests. They
establish that a measured blended revenue per cycle of $19,824 — $12,782 of services and $7,041 of
drugs, from one company's own revenue and cycle disclosures — falls inside the published US list
range and validates the per-cycle anchor; that US ART spend is therefore $6.5–13.1 billion against
a measured $8.63 billion; that billing every published registry cycle in the world at the US rate
still accounts for at most 52.4% of the largest published total as clinical services; that
per-clinic arithmetic points the same way; and that disclosed company volume in two mature markets
is growing at 6.4% and shrinking at 5.0% respectively against published CAGRs up to 12.15%. They do
**not** establish the correct size of this market. The registry floor sums four different data years
and excludes China, India, Latin America and the Middle East, so it is a floor and the per-cycle
figures derived from it are ceilings. Progyny is a benefits manager negotiating rates for 6.7
million covered lives, not a price-setter, and its cycle mix includes low-cost frozen transfers and
egg freezing. Monash IVF's absolute cycle count was not obtained, so no second measured per-cycle
rate exists in this file. The US list-price range comes from a consumer health guide, the UK
cost figures from a 304-patient academic survey, and the UK regulator's own position — as reported
by the competition authority — is that **an average UK cycle cost cannot be determined because
clinics do not collect the information.** And dividing company revenue into a published market
total compares differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $58.21B (2026) → $115.82B (2032) at 12.15%.**

Source: **IMARC Group, *Fertility Services Market*, released 3 February 2026**, which states
**$51.9 billion for 2025**, $150.2 billion for 2034 and a **12.15% CAGR for 2026–2034**, with a
historical period of 2020–2025. The 2026 base is **derived by advancing IMARC's own published 2025
figure one year at IMARC's own stated rate** (51.9 × 1.1215 = 58.21). The 2032 endpoint is **derived
by continuing that rate** (58.21 × 1.1215^6 = 115.82). Both are labelled as interpolation in the
report. Internal check: the implied rate between our endpoints is 12.15%, identical to IMARC's
stated rate.

Chosen because: (a) it is the **only series in the set read directly from the publisher's own page**,
with a stated release date — the two competing whole-market figures came through a search summary
and a news republication, and one publisher's own pages are gated; (b) it carries **by far the most
explicit scope statement**, naming surrogacy as a procedure, egg and embryo banking as a service and
cryobanks as an end user, which is what makes the composition arithmetic above possible at all —
a publisher that states its scope that plainly can be audited, and the two that do not cannot; (c)
its **12.15% rate is reconcilable with the registers** once you see that 42.4% of US ART cycles are
banking cycles and UK egg freezing grew 47% in a year, which is where a double-digit blend can
legitimately come from; and (d) it **publishes no numeric regional or segment shares at all**, so
there is nothing in it to misuse.

**What we state alongside it, in every prose field, is the composition.** This is not an IVF market
and it is not a clinic-revenue market. Measured against the published cycle registries at the
measured US rate, **ART clinical services worldwide are of the order of $20–27 billion, at most
52.4% of the published total**, with the balance in fertility drugs — 35.5% of per-cycle spend on
Progyny's own split — surrogacy, donor gametes, banking and genetic testing. A reader who takes
$51.9 billion and imagines what fertility clinics bill is out by roughly a factor of two.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Healthcare IT.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Progyny is a benefits manager and Monash IVF an Australian clinic
  operator; neither discloses a share of anything, and the clinic market is fragmented across 457
  US and 1,473 European centres. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely. The ratios
  we do publish — $27.21B at 52.4% of IMARC's total, drugs at 35.5% of per-cycle spend, banking at
  42.4% of US cycles, the US at 20.5% of registry cycles — are stated in prose with both inputs
  named.
- **Regional and country splits.** IMARC names five regions and publishes no values; no other source
  in the set gives any. `regional_analysis` and `country_analysis` stay `[]`. **The registry cycle
  counts by country are published in these notes and are deliberately not converted into revenue by
  region**, because doing so would require a per-cycle price for each country and only two exist.
- **IMARC's procedure and service segment values.** None are published numerically, which is why
  there is nothing to reject. Segmentation goes out as plain strings.
- **A world ART services market size.** Our $27.21B is a four-year-mixed registry floor multiplied
  by one US company's negotiated services rate applied to every country on earth. It is a labelled
  upper-bound plausibility calculation stated in prose and is never published as a market size.
- **A per-cycle price as a price.** Progyny's $19,824 is a benefits manager's blended revenue across
  fresh IVF, freeze-all, frozen transfers and egg freezing for 6.7 million covered members. It is a
  measured revenue-per-cycle figure, published as such, and not presented as what an IVF cycle
  costs.
- **A Monash IVF per-cycle figure.** The company reported percentage changes in stimulated cycles
  and we could not locate the absolute count. **No second measured per-cycle rate is derived, and
  the gap is stated.**
- **A single-year world cycle total.** Our 2,128,848 sums US 2022, Europe 2023, Japan 2020 and
  Australasia 2020, and omits China, India, Latin America and the Middle East. Used strictly as a
  floor, labelled every time it appears.
- **ESHRE's 2020-to-2023 cycle growth as a growth rate.** The reporting-country count falls from 41
  to 36 across the same series. Levels published, growth not.
- **A UK average cycle cost.** The competition authority's reported position is that it cannot be
  determined because clinics do not collect the information; the £5,000 figure attributed to the
  regulator could not be confirmed against the regulator's own site; the academic survey is 304
  self-selected patients; and the price-comparison figures come from commercial sites, one of them
  commissioned by a clinic. The range is recorded in these notes and no UK average is published.
- **Any US clinic count other than CDC's 457.** That is the number of clinics that reported to CDC
  for 2022, which is not identical to the number operating. Stated as "reporting clinics" wherever
  it appears.
- **The two 2018–2019 forecasts as current estimates.** They are used only for the retrospective
  test, which is their whole value.
- **The blog-hosted IVF services figure as a published estimate.** Recorded for scope contrast only.
