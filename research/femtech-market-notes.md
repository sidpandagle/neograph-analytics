## The spread

**At 2026, $9.78B (Mordor) to $73.4B (Global Market Insights) — a 7.5x spread.** At 2032, $28.89B to $132.20B — 4.6x. Published CAGRs run **13.52% to 17.9%, a 1.32x spread.**

**This is the widest base-year spread in the batch and femtech is the hardest market in it to scope. The report should say so plainly in its first paragraph, and it does.** The reason is that "femtech" is a coinage, not a category boundary. It is stretched across at least six businesses that share a customer and nothing else:

1. **Women's health medical devices** — mammography, breast biopsy, hysteroscopy, endometrial ablation, pelvic floor devices. Capital and consumable sales to hospitals.
2. **Women's health pharmaceuticals** — contraceptive implants and rings, fertility drugs, postpartum haemorrhage devices.
3. **Fertility and IVF services** — clinic chains and laboratory services.
4. **Digital and consumer software** — period and ovulation trackers, menopause platforms, telehealth.
5. **Consumer durables** — breast pumps, pelvic-health trainers, wearables.
6. **Menstrual care consumer products** — pads, tampons, cups, period underwear.

No publisher in the set says which of the six it is counting. Mordor comes closest, and its own numbers place it: **devices are 51.62% of its market and hospitals and maternal centres are 46.10% of its end users.** Mordor's femtech is predominantly a **hospital women's-health device and solutions market**, not the consumer app category the word usually evokes.

The arithmetic below shows that this matters by a factor of nine, and it shows what the wide readings must contain.

**A note on what we did not include.** Hologic's Diagnostics segment ($1,827.2M) contains cervical cytology and HPV molecular testing, which are unambiguously women's health. We **excluded** it from the sum because it also contains respiratory and other molecular assays and Biotheranostics oncology lab testing, and no split is disclosed. Including it would raise the sum to $5,743.1M and every ratio below with it. **The exclusion is stated, and it makes our sum a floor rather than a maximum.**

### Test 1 — two women's health franchises against each published femtech market

```
Hologic Breast Health + GYN Surgical  $2,163.9M
Organon Women's Health                $1,752.0M
-------------------------------------------------
Sum                                   $3,915.9M   (2025)

÷  $8,560M  (Mordor, 2025)                        =  45.7%
÷  $9,780M  (Mordor, 2026 — our published base)   =  40.0%
÷  $7,810M  (Fortune-attributed, 2024)            =  50.1%
÷ $56,440M  (Report Prime, 2026)                  =   6.9%
÷ $73,400M  (Global Market Insights, 2026)        =   5.3%
```

**Two companies are 45.7% of one published femtech market and 5.3% of another.** Both ratios are informative, and together they identify what each reading is:

- **At 45.7%, Mordor's reading is coherent.** A market that is 51.62% devices and 46.10% hospital-and-maternal-centre revenue on its own account, in which the world's largest breast imaging franchise plus the world's largest contraceptive implant franchise come to a little under half, is internally consistent. It is a **women's health devices and pharmaceuticals market**, and it is a plausible one.
- **At 5.3%, the Global Market Insights reading requires a $69.5 billion remainder.** The question the brief insists be answered is what that remainder consists of, and there is only one honest answer available: **it has to be overwhelmingly consumer menstrual care products and wellness services** — pads, tampons, cups, period underwear, breast pumps and subscription platforms. Those are sold predominantly by consumer packaged goods companies: Procter & Gamble (Always, Tampax), Kimberly-Clark (Kotex) and Essity (Bodyform, Libresse). **Nobody calls those companies femtech.** We did not obtain a sourced figure for the global menstrual care products market in this research pass and **we publish none**; the point stands without one, because no other candidate category of that size exists.

**So the 7.5x spread is not a disagreement about women's health technology. It is the difference between a hospital women's-health device market and the entire female consumer hygiene aisle.** That is the finding of this report.

### Test 2 — what the digital tier, which gives the category its name, actually discloses

Nothing. **We found no public subscriber count, unit figure or revenue disclosure for any femtech application or platform company in this research pass** — not for Flo, not for Clue, not for Natural Cycles, not for any menopause or pelvic-health platform. All are private. There is no listed femtech pure-play of scale anywhere in the category.

That absence is worth stating as a result rather than as a gap:

```
Disclosed revenue in the DIGITAL femtech tier, 2025                       none found
Mordor's software share of its own 2025 market                            not stated as a figure
Mordor's software growth rate to 2031                                     16.12%  (the third fastest segment)
```

**The segment growing fastest in the published forecasts is the one with no public revenue disclosure of any kind.** A reader cannot check it, and neither can we. The report says so.

### Test 3 — the growth rates, disclosed against published

This is the sharpest line in the file.

```
Published femtech CAGRs                                 13.52%  to  17.9%
Our published series (Mordor)                            14.20%

Actual FY2025 revenue growth, the two largest disclosed women's health franchises:

  Hologic, Breast Health                                 -2.5%
  Hologic, GYN Surgical                                  +6.0%
  Hologic, Breast Health + GYN Surgical combined         -0.01%   ($2,163.9M vs $2,164.2M)
  Organon, Women's Health                                 -1% reported, -2% ex-FX
    of which NuvaRing                                   -23% ex-FX
    of which Nexplanon                                   -4% ex-FX  (US -9%)
  Organon, Women's Health, Q4 2025 alone                 -16% ex-FX

  Combined, 2025 vs 2024:  $3,915.9M vs ~$3,934M         -0.5%
```

**The two largest disclosed women's health franchises in the world shrank by about half a percent in 2025, against published femtech growth rates of 13.52% to 17.9%.** Organon's fourth quarter was down 16% ex-FX. Nexplanon's US sales fell 9%, which Organon attributes to **US policy-related access restrictions** and lower physician demand, partly offset by growth in Brazil and its institutional business.

(*The 2024 combined figure of about $3,934M is **our own back-calculation**: Hologic's FY2024 segment revenues are disclosed at $1,522.9M and $641.3M, but Organon's FY2024 Women's Health revenue was not read directly and is inferred from its stated -1% as-reported decline — **flagged, and the -0.5% combined figure carries that caveat wherever it appears.**)

There is a coherent story that partly reconciles this, and the report gives it: the growth in this category is in segments these two companies barely address — software at 16.12%, menopause and longevity at 16.18%, specialty women's health clinics at 16.73% on Mordor's own projections — and those are precisely the segments with no public revenue. But a published CAGR of 14.2% applied to a market that is 51.62% devices, in which the largest disclosed device franchises are flat to shrinking, is a forecast that the undisclosed minority carries the whole market. That is a far stronger claim than any of these pages makes explicitly.

### Test 4 — and the largest disclosure is about to disappear

On **21 October 2025** Hologic announced a definitive agreement to be acquired by funds managed by **Blackstone and TPG**, and it is **not providing annual or quarterly financial guidance for fiscal 2026** as a result.

**The single largest public disclosure of women's health device revenue anywhere is being taken private.** This is the same event that already removed the second disclosure from veterinary diagnostics when Mars bought Heska, pointed at a different market: after this closes, the public record on women's health devices consists of Organon's pharmaceutical franchise and very little else. Any femtech market size published after that date is less checkable than this one, and the report says so.

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope and plausibility tests. They establish that two disclosed women's health franchises sum to $3,915.9M for 2025, which is 45.7% of the narrowest published femtech market and 5.3% of the widest; that the narrow reading is therefore internally coherent as a women's health device and pharmaceutical market while the wide reading requires a $69.5bn remainder for which consumer menstrual care is the only candidate of that size; that the digital tier which gives the category its name discloses no revenue, subscriber or unit figure we could find; that the two largest disclosed franchises shrank about 0.5% in 2025 against published CAGRs of 13.52% to 17.9%; and that the largest of those disclosures is being taken private. They do **not** establish the correct market size for any scope, and no market size of our own is asserted. Hologic's fiscal year ends in September and is not coterminous with Organon's calendar year; Hologic's Diagnostics segment contains cervical cytology and HPV testing which are women's health but are **excluded** from our sum because no split is disclosed, making the sum a floor; Organon's FY2024 Women's Health revenue is our back-calculation from a stated percentage; no fertility revenue total is disclosed by Organon despite an +8% growth figure; and dividing company revenue by a third party's market total compares differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $9.78B (2026) → $21.69B (2032) at 14.20% — the narrowest sourced reading in the set, published as such.**

Source: Mordor Intelligence, *Femtech Market*, page read from the publisher's own site on **16 October 2026**, which publishes **$8.56B for 2025, $9.78B for 2026, $18.98B for 2031 and a 14.20% CAGR for 2026–2031** over a 2020–2031 study period covering 17 countries.

The 2026 base is **Mordor's own published 2026 figure**, not a derivation. The 2032 endpoint **continues Mordor's own stated rate one year past its own horizon**: 9.78 × 1.1420^6 = **21.69**. Internal check: interpolating forward lands 2031 on **19.00** against Mordor's published **18.98**, and the implied rate between our endpoints is **14.20%**, identical to the stated rate.

Chosen because: (a) it is the **narrowest sourced reading**, which is the correct default in a category with no defensible boundary; (b) it is the **only source in the set read directly from the publisher's own page** — Global Market Insights, Report Prime and the Fortune-attributed figures all came through search summaries or Notion mirror pages, and DelveInsight's own page leaves its dollar figures as literal placeholders; (c) it is the only one that publishes a **definition, a country count, an application taxonomy and segment shares**, which is what makes it identifiable at all; and (d) at $8.56B for 2025 the two disclosed women's health franchises are 45.7% of it, a coherent concentration, where at $73.4B they are 5.3% and the remainder has to be the consumer hygiene aisle.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Medical Devices.

**What this reading excludes, named explicitly as the brief requires.** On its own segment shares, Mordor's market is 51.62% devices and 46.10% hospital and maternal centre revenue. It therefore **substantially excludes**: consumer menstrual care products (pads, tampons, cups, period underwear); the retail breast pump and consumer wearable market; direct-to-consumer subscription platforms outside a clinical setting; and general women's wellness and mental health services. Readers who want a figure that includes those should expect something in the **$56B to $73B** range, should know that no publisher in that range states a scope, and should know that it would make two of the largest women's health businesses in the world about 5% of their own market.

The report states plainly in `executive_summary`, `market_context`, `forecast_analysis` and `research_methodology` that femtech has no defensible boundary and that this report publishes the narrowest sourced reading; that Hologic's Breast Health and GYN Surgical plus Organon's Women's Health come to $3.92 billion and are 45.7% of that reading and 5.3% of the widest one; that the digital tier discloses nothing; that those two franchises shrank about 0.5% in 2025 against published CAGRs of 13.52% to 17.9%; and that Hologic is being taken private by Blackstone and TPG and has withdrawn fiscal 2026 guidance.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Two companies disclose women's health revenue lines; neither discloses a share. The 45.7%, 40.0%, 50.1%, 6.9% and 5.3% ratios are **our own division** of disclosed revenue lines by third parties' published totals, stated in prose with both inputs named. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **A global menstrual care products market size.** It is the only candidate for the $69.5bn remainder in the wide readings and we say so, but **we obtained no sourced figure for it in this pass and publish none.** Procter & Gamble, Kimberly-Clark and Essity are named as the companies that sell it; none is sized.
- **Any revenue or subscriber figure for a femtech application or platform company.** We found none for Flo, Clue, Natural Cycles or any menopause or pelvic-health platform. All are private. **The absence is reported as a result; no estimate is substituted.**
- **An Organon fertility revenue total.** Organon disclosed **+8% ex-FX fertility growth** and the individual products Follistim AQ ($264M) and Ganirelix Acetate ($101M), but **no fertility total**. The products are reported; no total is summed or inferred, because the franchise contains more than those two.
- **Hologic's Diagnostics revenue as femtech.** $1,827.2M contains cervical cytology and HPV molecular testing, which is women's health, alongside respiratory assays and Biotheranostics oncology lab testing, with no split disclosed. **Excluded from the sum, and the exclusion is stated**, which makes our $3,915.9M a floor. Including it would give $5,743.1M and 67.1% of Mordor's 2025 market.
- **The -0.5% combined 2025 growth figure as a clean disclosure.** Hologic's FY2024 segment revenues are disclosed; **Organon's FY2024 Women's Health revenue is our back-calculation from its stated -1% decline.** Flagged wherever the figure appears. The component figures — Hologic's combined -0.01%, Organon's -1% reported and -2% ex-FX, Q4 -16% ex-FX, NuvaRing -23%, Nexplanon -4% with US -9% — are all disclosed and are used in preference.
- **Mordor's segment shares as segment percentages.** Devices 51.62%, pregnancy and nursing care 31.05%, hospitals and maternal centres 46.10% and the 16.12%/16.18%/16.73% growth rates are **quoted in prose with the publisher named**, because they are the evidence that identifies what the narrow reading measures. They are **not** reproduced in `market_segmentation`, which goes out as plain strings. One source, one page, no stated method.
- **Mordor's North America 41.95% of 2025.** A single house's apportionment of a category with no agreed boundary, with no method stated. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`. `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **DelveInsight's market size.** The publisher's own page leaves the dollar figures as "USD XX Billion". Its **13.52% rate is recorded in the estimates table** and nothing else is taken from it.
- **The Fortune Business Insights attribution.** Read from Notion mirror pages that are secondary copies of the report rather than from the publisher. **Heavily flagged**, in the table for range only.
- **Research and Markets' $60.7B by 2031.** Published 11 July 2022, four years stale. Range only.
- **Global Market Insights' $73.4B and Report Prime's $56.44B as the femtech market.** Both appear in the estimates table and the report describes them as readings that necessarily include consumer menstrual care and wellness, which is what Test 1 shows they must. Neither is used for the headline, and the report says why in terms a reader can check.
- **Any statement that the wide readings are wrong.** They are unscoped, not disproven. The report says what they would have to contain and lets the reader decide whether that is the market they meant.
- **A policy-impact figure for the Nexplanon US decline.** Organon attributes a 9% US decline to **policy-related access restrictions** and lower physician demand. That is the company's own characterisation and is reported as such; **no quantification of the policy effect is attempted**, because none is disclosed.
