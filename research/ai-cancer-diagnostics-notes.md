Allied and GMI are 2–7x higher because they use different scopes and base years —
GMI explicitly covers all of AI in oncology, not diagnostics alone. The divergence
is itself the analytical point: scope definition, not growth disagreement, drives
the spread. A report that states this plainly is more useful than one that asserts
a single number.

## Regulatory base rates (FDA AI-enabled device list)
- **1,524** authorized AI-enabled devices through Q1 2026; **1,164 (76%)** radiology.
- **92** authorized in Q1 2026 alone.
- 2025 full year: ~**295** clearances from 221 manufacturers (one analysis), vs
  **331** in another using a different base — counting methods differ.
- Trend: 221 (2023) -> 253 (2024) -> ~295 (2025).
- **Important caveat to quote:** the FDA states the list is "not a comprehensive
  resource of AI-enabled medical devices"; entries are identified by AI-related
  terms in authorization summaries and mix standalone software with hardware
  containing embedded AI.
- No oncology-specific count is published. Deriving one requires filtering the FDA
  list by specialty — a genuinely original contribution this report could make.

## The angle worth taking
Most published reports on this topic assert a single market size. This one should:
1. Present the range and explain *why* estimates differ by up to 7x (scope).
2. Ground growth in FDA authorization base rates, which are countable and public,
   rather than in an unverifiable top-down number.
3. Note that radiology dominates authorizations at 76%, so "AI in cancer
   diagnostics" is in practice largely imaging — which has implications for where
   value accrues.
