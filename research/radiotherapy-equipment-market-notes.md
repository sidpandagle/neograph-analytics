# Radiotherapy equipment — research notes
**Slug:** radiotherapy-equipment-market   **Drafted:** 2026-10-17

Scope drafted here: **external-beam radiotherapy equipment and its service** —
linear accelerators, cobalt-60 teletherapy, stereotactic and robotic radiosurgery
platforms, brachytherapy afterloaders, treatment planning and verification
software, and the manufacturer service contracts attached to the installed base.
Not the clinical service revenue of delivering radiotherapy, and not oncology
drugs or radiopharmaceuticals.

## The spread

**$4.13B to $13.95B on a common 2026 base — a 3.38x spread.** The CAGRs run
**6.0% to 12.5%, a 2.08x spread.**

But the interesting result here is the opposite of what this corpus usually
finds, and it only appears once the two internally incoherent estimates are set
aside.

**The two that do not survive their own arithmetic:**

- **Verified Market Research, $3.60B (2024) -> $5.93B (2032).** The endpoints
  imply `(5.93/3.60)^(1/8) - 1 = 6.43%`, not the 7.10% stated, and the stated
  rate is labelled 2026-2032 while the base is 2024 — the publisher does not say
  what happens in the two intervening years. Its application list (cardiology,
  urology, gynaecology, neurology) and its segmentation axis ("technological
  innovation") do not describe a radiotherapy equipment market. Grounding
  division 3 below rules the level out independently.
- **Precision Business Insights, $12.40B (2025) -> $28.20B (2032).** The
  endpoints do reconcile at the stated 12.5% (`12.40 x 1.125^7 = 28.28`), but
  the page gives 2024 as the base in one section and 2025 in another, 2031 in
  one place and 2032 in another, and its own FAQ states a different CAGR of 5.2%
  for 2023-2029. A 12.5% rate is 1.6x the next highest in the set.

**With those two removed, ten estimates span $7.21B to $10.01B — a 1.39x
spread.** The CAGRs across the same ten span 6.0% to 9.0% — **a 1.50x spread.**

So the growth rates disagree *proportionally more* than the sizes do. That is
unusual, and it is the finding. Radiotherapy equipment is a market where
publishers broadly agree what the thing is and roughly how big it is, and
disagree about how fast it grows. The reason is structural: the denominator is a
countable, registered fleet of machines in a known number of named centres (see
below), the suppliers number three of any size, and two of the three publish
audited revenue. There is very little room to disagree about the level. What is
genuinely uncertain is the **replacement cycle**: how fast an installed base of
roughly 17,000 machines turns over, and how much of the enormous unmet need in
low- and middle-income countries converts into orders rather than remaining a
need. A 6.0% reading and a 9.0% reading of the same fleet are two different
answers to that question, not two different definitions of the market.

The one definition gap that does matter is visible in the table: the entries
labelled "equipment" cluster at $8.14-9.14B and the entries labelled
"radiotherapy" broadly reach $10.01B. The difference, about $0.9-1.9B, is
roughly what you would expect from adding intraoperative radiotherapy, third-
party treatment planning software and brachytherapy sources to an
equipment-and-service scope. It is a 1.23x gap, not a 6x one.

## Grounding arithmetic

Radiotherapy is one of the best-registered markets in medical devices. The IAEA
has counted the world's radiotherapy centres and teletherapy machines since 1959
and publishes the directory (DIRAC); the installed base is therefore a real
denominator rather than an estimate. Two of the three suppliers of any scale
publish audited revenue, and one publishes its own installed-base count. Five
independent divisions follow.

### FX basis

Varian reports inside Siemens Healthineers in euro; Elekta reports in Swedish
krona. Both are converted at the **ECB 2025 annual average reference rates as
published by the Bank of Finland [13]: EUR 1 = USD 1.1300 and EUR 1 = SEK
11.0671.** The USD/SEK cross is derived from those two, and the derivation is
printed because it is not itself a published rate:

```
11.0671 SEK/EUR / 1.1300 USD/EUR = 9.7940 SEK per USD
```

Two caveats, stated rather than buried. Siemens Healthineers' fiscal 2025 ended
30 September 2025, so a calendar-2025 average is a close but not exact match.
Elekta's fiscal 2025/26 ran May 2025 to April 2026, so a calendar-2025 average
overlaps eight of its twelve months. Elekta itself reports that net sales fell
**7% in SEK but rose 1% at constant exchange rates** [14], which is the measure
of how much currency is doing here: about eight points. Every conversion below
should be read as accurate to roughly that tolerance, and no conclusion in this
file turns on less than that.

### The register

IAEA DIRAC, as described by the IAEA in 2026 [15]: *"The database includes
nearly 8700 radiotherapy centres, over 17 000 teletherapy machines and more than
3000 brachytherapy units worldwide."* The DIRAC front page separately shows
"8500+ centres", "20000+ units" and "150+ countries" [16] — the "units" figure
is not broken down by equipment type on that page, so the IAEA's own
**17,000 teletherapy machines and 8,700 centres** are used throughout and the
20,000 figure is not.

### 1. Dollars per installed teletherapy unit — the point of the exercise

```
$8,140M / 17,000 teletherapy machines = $478,824 per installed unit per year
```

Bracketing with the coherent cluster's endpoints:

```
$7,210M / 17,000 = $424,118   (The Business Research Company, lowest coherent)
$10,010M / 17,000 = $588,824  (Research and Markets, radiotherapy broad)
```

And the two rejected readings, for contrast:

```
$4,130M / 17,000 = $242,941   (Verified Market Research)
$13,950M / 17,000 = $820,588  (Precision Business Insights)
```

Now build the same quantity from the bottom up, from two sources that have
nothing to do with each other.

**Service, from Elekta's own books.** Elekta disclosed FY2025/26 service sales of
SEK 7,393M against an installed base of approximately 7,500 devices at 30 April
2026 [14]:

```
SEK 7,393M / 7,500 devices = SEK 985,733 per device per year
SEK 985,733 / 9.7940       = $100,647 per installed device per year, service only
```

**Capital, from the Lancet Oncology linac-requirements study.** Moraes et al.
(*The Lancet Oncology*, February 2025) analysed DIRAC and GLOBOCAN 2022 across
181 countries with a 15 September 2024 data cutoff, and costed the requirement
at **$123.9B of investment for an estimated 30,470 linacs needed by 2045,
including replacement of obsolete devices** [17][18]:

```
$123,900M / 30,470 units = $4.0663M per unit, new-build and replacement
                           (inclusive of centre costs)
```

Amortised over a ten-year replacement cycle and added to the measured service
line:

```
$4,066,300 / 10 years = $406,630 capital per unit per year
+ $100,647 service    = $507,277 per installed unit per year
```

Against the published figure of $478,824:

```
$507,277 / $478,824 = 1.059
```

**Two constructions that share no input land 5.9% apart.** On a twelve-year
cycle instead, capital is $338,858 and the total is $439,505, which is 8.2%
*below* the published figure. So the published $8.14B is exactly consistent with
a world fleet of 17,000 machines, each carrying about $100,000 a year of
manufacturer service and being replaced every ten to twelve years at roughly
$4.07M a time. That is what a radiotherapy equipment market should look like,
and it is the strongest corroboration in this file.

**What it rules out.** The $4.13B reading implies $242,941 per unit per year —
less than the measured Elekta service line plus any replacement cycle longer
than zero; it cannot fund the fleet it would have to fund. The $13.95B reading
implies $820,588 per unit per year, which on the Lancet's own unit cost would
require every machine in the world to be replaced every 5.6 years. Neither is a
plausible description of a 17,000-unit installed base.

### 2. Dollars per radiotherapy centre, and what the market is not

```
$8,140M / 8,700 centres = $935,632 per radiotherapy centre per year
$10,010M / 8,700        = $1,150,575 per centre per year
17,000 / 8,700          = 1.95 teletherapy machines per centre
```

Under a million dollars a year per centre. That single number settles the scope
question that the published literature leaves open, because a radiotherapy
centre running two machines does not bill under a million dollars a year for the
treatments it delivers — it spends about that much on the machines and their
upkeep.

Push it one step further using the IAEA's own capacity benchmark. The IAEA, and
the IAEA-led Lancet Oncology Commission on Radiotherapy and Theranostics, use a
target of **one radiotherapy machine per 500 cancer patients** [19]:

```
17,000 machines x 500 patients = 8,500,000 patients of annual fleet capacity
$8,140M / 8,500,000            = $958 per patient of annual capacity
```

**$958 per patient treated.** Whatever a course of external-beam radiotherapy is
worth clinically, it is not $958 in any reimbursed health system — a single
planning CT costs more than that. The published figure is therefore the
equipment-and-service cost of the *capacity*, amortised across the throughput
the capacity supports. It is not, and cannot be read as, what hospitals bill for
radiotherapy. Anyone quoting an $8-10B "radiotherapy market" as the size of the
radiation oncology business is understating that business by at least an order of
magnitude.

This division rules in the equipment reading and rules out the services reading,
using only two sourced registers and no price assumption.

### 3. The disclosed floor — what the three suppliers actually book

|---|---|---|---|
| Varian, a Siemens Healthineers business | FY2025 segment revenue (FY ended 30 Sep 2025) [20] | EUR 4,081M | **$4,611.5M** |
| Elekta AB | FY2025/26 net sales (May 2025-Apr 2026) [14] | SEK 16,718M | **$1,706.9M** |
| Accuray Incorporated | FY2026 total net revenue (FY ended 30 Jun 2026) [21] | $401.9M | **$401.9M** |
| | | **Three-company total** | **$6,720.3M** |

```
EUR 4,081M x 1.1300    = $4,611.5M
SEK 16,718M / 9.7940   = $1,706.9M
+ $401.9M              = $6,720.3M
```

Against each published reading:

- $6,720.3M / $8,140M equipment = **82.6%**
- $6,720.3M / $9,140M Mordor = 73.5%
- $6,720.3M / $10,010M radiotherapy broad = 67.1%
- $6,720.3M / $7,210M devices = 93.2%
- $6,720.3M / $13,950M Precision Business Insights = 48.2%
- Varian alone / $8,140M = **56.7%**

**The decisive one.** Verified Market Research's series forecasts a $5.93B market
in **2032**. The three suppliers disclosed $6.72B in their most recent completed
fiscal years:

```
$6,720.3M / $5,930M = 1.13x
```

Three companies already out-earn, today, the entire market that series projects
for 2032. Varian alone, at $4.61B, is 1.28x that series' $3.60B 2024 base. The
$4.13B normalised reading is not an estimate of this market; it is an estimate of
something smaller that is not named.

**And the other end.** Precision Business Insights' $13.95B leaves $7.23B for
everyone outside Varian, Elekta and Accuray. The remaining suppliers are IBA,
Hitachi, Sumitomo Heavy Industries, Mevion, ProTom, Brainlab, Eckert & Ziegler
BEBIG and the Chinese entrants — several of which are proton-only, and none of
which approaches Elekta's scale. A $7.23B tail in this market does not exist.

**What 82.6% means, said plainly.** Three suppliers accounting for 83% of a
published market is the kind of ratio that normally indicates the market figure
is too small. Here it indicates the opposite: radiotherapy equipment really is
that concentrated. The ratio is tight, and two things loosen it in the honest
direction. Varian's EUR 4,081M segment includes proton therapy, interventional
oncology and oncology software, so the share of it that is external-beam
radiotherapy equipment is less than the whole. And Accuray's fiscal 2026 was a
bad year — revenue fell 12%, product revenue 27%, with roughly $58M of the
decline attributed to China [21][22] — so $401.9M is a trough, not a run rate.
Both corrections move the ratio below 82.6%. Neither moves it anywhere near the
48% that Precision Business Insights would require.

### 4. Installed base by count against revenue by value — why you cannot scale one vendor

Elekta is the only supplier that publishes an installed-base count. Two
registers, divided:

```
7,500 Elekta devices / 17,000 DIRAC teletherapy machines = 44.1% of the world
                                                           fleet by count
$1,706.9M Elekta / $8,140M published market               = 21.0% of revenue
```

**44.1% of the machines, 21.0% of the money — a 2.10x gap.** This is a ratio of
counts against a ratio of values, not a market share, and it is not entered as
one anywhere in the JSON.

The gap is the structure of the market. Elekta's base skews toward its
lower-priced Harmony and Synergy class and toward health systems where service
is priced below U.S. and Western European levels; Varian's revenue per installed
machine is roughly double. It also invalidates the obvious shortcut:

```
$1,706.9M / 7,500 devices = $227,587 per Elekta device per year
17,000 x $227,587         = $3,869M
$3,869M / $8,140M         = 47.5%
```

Scaling Elekta's unit economics across the DIRAC fleet yields $3.87B — which is
**below the $6.72B three companies actually disclose**, and therefore wrong by
construction. The fleet does not earn uniformly. This is worth stating because
the method works in the adjacent proton therapy report, where IBA is a listed
pure-play whose per-site and per-room economics do scale. Elekta is not a pure
play on the average machine, and the same arithmetic fails here. A grounding
method is not transferable between markets just because the register is.

### 5. The capital need against the capital flow

Deriving the equipment half of the market needs a service share, and the two
suppliers who disclose one disagree:

```
Elekta:  SEK 7,393M / SEK 16,718M = 44.2% service
Accuray: $229.2M / $401.9M        = 57.0% service
```

That bracket is published rather than averaged away. Applying it to $8.14B:

```
$8,140M x (1 - 0.570) = $3,500M equipment flow (high service share)
$8,140M x (1 - 0.442) = $4,542M equipment flow (low service share)
```

Against the Lancet Oncology requirement, annualised across the study's own
horizon:

```
$123,900M / 20 years (2025-2045) = $6,195M of capital needed per year
$3,500M / $6,195M                = 56.5%
$4,542M / $6,195M                = 73.3%
```

**The world is buying radiotherapy machines at 56-73% of the annual rate needed
to close a 30% global capacity gap by 2045** — and spending nearly all of it in
the countries that already have machines. The IAEA's own distribution figures
make that concrete: one teletherapy machine serves about **140,000 people in
high-income countries and over 15 million in low-income countries, a 107x
ratio**, only **21% of the world's countries met the IAEA's minimum resource
requirements for radiotherapy in 2022**, and more than 20 African Union members
had no radiotherapy machine at all [19][23]. A 2025 geospatial study in
*Radiotherapy and Oncology* put the share of population within 120 minutes of a
radiotherapy facility at **17.1% in low-income countries against 93.1% in
high-income countries** [24].

The commercial reading: the shortfall in this market is not a shortfall of
demand, and the 6.0%-to-9.0% disagreement among forecasters is a disagreement
about whether that need converts to orders. The low forecasts assume replacement
cycles in equipped countries; the high ones assume the gap starts closing.

### What the five divisions rule in and out

- **Ruled out: $4.13B (Verified Market Research).** Three suppliers out-earn its
  2032 forecast today; Varian alone exceeds its 2024 base; and $242,941 per
  installed unit per year cannot fund the measured service line plus any
  replacement cycle.
- **Ruled out: $13.95B (Precision Business Insights).** Requires a $7.23B
  supplier tail that is not there, and a 5.6-year fleet replacement cycle on the
  Lancet's own unit cost. The page is also internally inconsistent about its own
  base year, end year and CAGR.
- **Ruled out: the services reading.** $935,632 per centre per year and $958 per
  patient of fleet capacity are equipment-and-service magnitudes, not
  treatment-billing magnitudes.
- **Ruled out: scaling any one vendor's unit economics across the DIRAC fleet.**
  Elekta's rate gives $3.87B, below the disclosed floor.
- **Ruled in: $8.14B (2026) -> $12.70B (2032), equipment scope.** It sits inside
  the coherent $7.21-10.01B cluster, its per-unit figure reconciles to within
  5.9% of a two-source bottom-up build, and the three-supplier disclosed floor of
  $6.72B is 82.6% of it — tight, consistent with a three-supplier market, and
  loosened in the honest direction by Varian's non-radiotherapy segment content
  and Accuray's trough year.
- **Ruled in, as a count ratio only:** Elekta's 7,500 devices are 44.1% of
  DIRAC's 17,000 teletherapy machines. Both terms are registers. It is a share of
  *machines*, not of revenue, and it appears nowhere as a vendor market share.

## What we publish

**Scope: global external-beam radiotherapy equipment and service.** Base 2026
**$8.14B**, forecast 2032 **$12.70B**.

**CAGR: 7.69%**, not the 7.63% the publisher states. The publisher's own
endpoints imply:

```
(12.70 / 8.14)^(1/6) - 1 = 1.5601966^(0.1666667) - 1 = 7.6937%
```

7.63% is its rounding. The 0.06-point difference is stated here rather than
silently absorbed, and 7.69% is published so that the endpoints, the headline
rate and the seven forecast points reconcile exactly.

`market_forecast` interpolates 2026-2032 at 7.69% from exactly $8.14B:
8.14, 8.77, 9.44, 10.17, 10.95, 11.79, 12.70. The chart is labelled as modelled
between published endpoints, because the intermediate years are arithmetic, not
observation.

### How this relates to the published proton therapy report

`proton-therapy-market` is already in the corpus at **$1.82B (2026) -> $3.36B
(2032) at 10.8%**, grounded on the PTCOG register of roughly 125 particle
therapy centres in clinical operation and on IBA's disclosed per-site and
per-room economics. The two reports are deliberately nested, not overlapping
restatements, and the boundary is a countable one:

```
125 particle therapy centres / 8,700 DIRAC radiotherapy centres = 1.4%
$1.82B proton / $8.14B radiotherapy equipment                   = 22.4%
```

**1.4% of the centres, 22.4% of the equipment spend.** That ratio — a 16x
difference between proton's share of the world's radiotherapy infrastructure by
count and its share by value — is the whole economic distinction between the two
reports, and it is why they cannot be merged. Proton therapy is a building
project with an accelerator in it, at $33-160M a facility on a decades-long
asset. Photon radiotherapy is a $4.07M machine in a bunker on a ten-to-twelve
year cycle, deployed 17,000 times. The first is a small number of very large
transactions; the second is a large fleet of recurring ones, and its growth is
governed by replacement timing rather than by new-build decisions.

Two consequences for this report's scope, both honoured:

1. The published series we take ($8.14B, Research and Markets) states proton
   among its radiation types, so proton is **inside** this report's number. The
   proton report is therefore a component report, and the $1.82B/$8.14B = 22.4%
   relationship is published in `market_context` so a reader cannot add the two
   headlines together.
2. The grounding method differs because the supplier structure differs. IBA's
   pure-play disclosure scales across the proton fleet; Elekta's does not scale
   across the photon fleet (division 4). The proton report's central arithmetic
   is $13.1M per operating centre per year; this report's is $478,824 per
   installed teletherapy unit per year and $935,632 per radiotherapy centre per
   year. They are measurements of different things and neither substitutes for
   the other.

`oncology-information-systems-market` is adjacent on the third side: the
treatment planning and verification software that Mordor explicitly includes in
its radiotherapy definition is the same software that an OIS report counts. That
overlap is named in `market_context` rather than netted out, because no source
in this file gives the software line separately.

## Do not publish

- **No vendor market share.** `competitive_landscape[].market_share` absent
  throughout. An undated fund-manager blog describes Varian as holding "almost
  70% of the US market"; it is undated, secondary and unverifiable, and is
  excluded. The 44.1% Elekta installed-base-to-DIRAC-fleet ratio in division 4 is
  a count ratio between two registers and is labelled as such every time it
  appears.
- **`regional_analysis: []`.** Several publishers give regional figures and they
  contradict each other, so none is safe to carry: Precedence puts North America
  at 37% in 2024; no other publisher in the set corroborates it; and no publisher
  gives a regional split of the specific equipment scope we publish. The IAEA's
  access figures (140,000 vs 15 million people per machine; 17.1% vs 93.1%
  population within 120 minutes) are about *machines and people*, not revenue,
  and belong in prose where their derivation is visible.
- **`country_analysis: []`.** Same reason, and one more. **DIRAC country unit
  counts are a register of machines, not of revenue.** No country's unit count is
  converted into a country revenue figure anywhere in this file or the JSON. The
  per-unit dollar figures in divisions 1 and 2 are global averages over a global
  numerator, and multiplying them by a national machine count would manufacture a
  national market size that no source gives. That operation is not performed.
- **No segment percentages.** The published external-beam share of radiotherapy
  revenue spans **48.7% (Coherent Market Insights), 58.6% (Fact.MR), 82.11%
  (Mordor) and 96.0% (MarketsandMarkets)** — a 1.97x spread on a single
  segment, wider in proportion than the 1.39x spread of the coherent size
  cluster. The publishers disagree about segmentation far more than about size.
  Segmentation is published as plain strings with no shares attached, and the
  disagreement itself is reported.
- **No Varian installed-base count.** Siemens Healthineers does not disclose one.
  A residual (DIRAC 17,000 minus Elekta 7,500 minus others) would be an inference
  presented as a register figure, so no Varian unit count and no Varian
  revenue-per-unit figure is published.
- **No linac list price.** The $4.0663M per unit used in division 1 is the
  Lancet study's own new-build-and-replacement investment per linac, inclusive of
  centre costs, and is labelled that way wherever it appears. It is not a machine
  price and is never described as one.
- **No price per course of external-beam radiotherapy.** The $958 per patient of
  fleet capacity in division 2 is a capacity cost, not a treatment price. No
  source in this file gives a photon radiotherapy course price, so none is
  published and the comparison is made by magnitude only.
- **No "half of all cancer patients need radiotherapy" figure.** It is widely
  repeated and is not sourced in this file. GLOBOCAN 2022's ~20.0 million new
  cases are not used as a denominator anywhere; the IAEA's own
  one-machine-per-500-patients benchmark is used instead, because the IAEA
  publishes it.
- **The 96% coincidence is rejected.** An earlier pass derived the equipment half
  of the market as $8.14B minus (17,000 x $100,647 of service) = $6.43B, which is
  96% of the Lancet's $6.195B annual need — a striking match. It is discarded,
  because it assumes every machine in the DIRAC fleet earns Elekta-like service
  revenue, and division 4 disproves exactly that assumption. The published
  version uses the 44.2-57.0% service-share bracket and reports 56-73% instead.
  The tighter number was the more tempting one and is the one that is wrong.
- **No single market-wide service share.** Elekta says 44.2% of its own sales,
  Accuray 57.0% of its own; Varian does not disclose one. The bracket is
  published; the midpoint is not.
- **No Elekta full-year order intake.** Sources conflict — a table in Elekta's
  Q1 2026/27 report shows SEK 17,441M for FY2025/26 while the annual report's
  narrative describes order intake differently — and Elekta's Q2 reinterpretation
  of its order criteria produced a **SEK 2,197M cancellation** that makes
  year-on-year order comparisons unsound. Book-to-bill of 1.04 (from 1.09) is
  cited instead, because Elekta states it directly.
- **Not the DIRAC "20,000+ units" figure.** The DIRAC front page shows it without
  saying which equipment types it covers. The IAEA's own stated breakdown —
  17,000 teletherapy machines, 3,000+ brachytherapy units — is used, and the
  per-unit divisions use the teletherapy count only.
- **No current global linac count from the Lancet study.** The paper reports a
  median Linear Accelerator Shortage Index of 130 (IQR 96-319) and a requirement
  of 30,470 units by 2045 including replacements; it does not publish a current
  worldwide total, and one is not back-calculated from the shortage index.
- **Not Accuray's $402M as a run rate.** It is a trough: fiscal 2026 revenue fell
  12% and product revenue 27%, with roughly $58M of the full-year decline
  attributed to China. It is used as a disclosed floor, labelled with its fiscal
  year, and never annualised forward.
