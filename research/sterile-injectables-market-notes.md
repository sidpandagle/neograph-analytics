## The spread

**At 2025, $46.43B to $632.46B — a 13.6x spread.** This is the widest spread in the batch by a
factor of three, and for once it is not a disagreement at all. It is two different things
wearing the same name.

**The same publisher holds both ends.** Coherent Market Insights publishes a sterile
injectables market of $586.1B for 2024 and a generic sterile injectables market of $46.43B for
2025. Those two numbers are internally consistent: generic sterile injectables are
7.9% of all sterile injectable drug revenue, which is roughly what you would expect in a
category whose highest-revenue products — checkpoint inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists,
biologics for autoimmune disease — are all on patent and all injectable.

So the $591B/$632B figures are **the revenue of injectable medicines at list price.** They
include Keytruda, Ozempic, Dupixent and every other branded injectable. They are not a measure
of the sterile injectable supply business — the plants, the lines, the fill-finish, the
shortage-prone generics — which is what the phrase "sterile injectables market" means to
everyone who works in it.

The arithmetic below shows why that distinction is not a quibble. It is the difference between
a market where two of the world's largest generic injectable suppliers hold 12% and one where
they hold less than 1%.

## Grounding arithmetic

Two of the three largest generic sterile injectable suppliers in the world disclose segment
revenue outright. That makes a direct test possible against both readings of the market.

### Anchor 1 — sum what the suppliers disclose

Converted at the **IRS yearly average currency exchange rates for 2025**, retrieved
16 October 2026: **EUR 0.886** per US dollar.

| Company | Disclosed FY2025 segment revenue | USD |
|---|---|---|
| Fresenius Kabi — Pharma (IV Drugs & Fluids) | €3,735M (2024: €3,835M; decline on FX, organic growth 2%) | 3,735 / 0.886 = **$4,215M** |
| Hikma Pharmaceuticals — Injectables (core revenue) | $1,423M (2024: $1,324M), +7% | **$1,423M** |
| | **Sum of two** | **$5,638M** |

Set that against the two readings of the market for the same year:

```
5,638 / 46,430   = 12.1%   of Coherent's generic sterile injectables market
5,638 / 591,400  =  0.95%  of Persistence's sterile injectable drugs market
```

**Two of the world's largest generic sterile injectable suppliers are under one percent of the
$591 billion figure.** Add Pfizer's hospital/injectables business, Baxter's injectables,
Sandoz's injectables unit, Eugia, Amneal and Gland and the generic sterile injectable supply
industry still does not approach 5% of it. Whatever the $591B measures, it is not this
industry. Against the generic-only reading, the same two companies are 12.1% — a plausible
figure for the number two and number four suppliers in a category with roughly a dozen
meaningful players.

**That is the finding: the $591B and $632B series are the injectable drug market and should
not be used to size the sterile injectables supply business.** We publish the generic reading
and say so explicitly in the report.

*Caveat stated plainly:* Fresenius Kabi's Pharma division reports as "IV Drugs & Fluids",
which includes large-volume parenterals, clinical nutrition and infusion solutions as well as
generic injectable drugs, so €3,735M is **wider** than generic sterile injectables. Hikma's
$1,423M of Injectables core revenue includes the Xellia portfolio acquired during the period
and some non-sterile injectable activity. The sum is a scale check, not an apportionment.

### Anchor 2 — the shortage register, and why the two public counts disagree

Generic sterile injectables are the shortage category. That is the structural restraint on
this market, and the registers that track it are countable — but they disagree with each other
by 2.4x, which is itself worth recording.

```
FDA Drug Shortages Database, ongoing shortages at 31 Dec 2025            93
USP, year-end 2025 count                                                 75   (down 23% from 98 in 2024)
ASHP / University of Utah Drug Information Service, active national
  shortages, Q2 2026                                                    227   (third consecutive quarterly rise)
```

```
227 / 93 = 2.44x
```

The gap is a definitional one, and the explanation is in ASHP's own framing: ASHP counts every
drug that is short from a pharmacist's point of view, while the FDA counts shortages against
its own statutory definition, which can exclude a product where some presentations remain
available. **A reader who takes "93 shortages" as the size of the problem and a reader who
takes "227" are reading two different registers, and no published forecast of this market says
which it priced.**

The dosage-form concentration is the load-bearing figure:

```
Sterile injectables as a share of all active US shortages, end-2025      71%
Injectables as a share of the 2025 Vulnerable Medicine List              63%
Of the five longest-running US shortages, all over a decade old,
  the number that are injectable medicines                              5 of 5
```

*All four counts in these two blocks are flagged: they were read from a 2026 compilation
(intuitionlabs) and secondary updates rather than from the FDA Drug Shortages Database, USP or
ASHP pages directly. The FDA's own live database and ASHP's quarterly statistics are the
primary sources and should be consulted before the figures are relied upon.* The 71% figure is
attributed by that compilation to usp.org, which we did not check.

### Anchor 3 — the disclosed margin, which is the mechanism

The reason generic sterile injectables go short is visible in one supplier's own disclosure.
Hikma reported Injectables revenue up about 12% in the first half of 2025 while the segment's
**core operating margin fell from 36.3% to 30.0%**, which it attributed to product and
geographic mix and to euro appreciation raising costs. By the full year, the chief executive's
own statement acknowledged that the Injectables business "has experienced some challenges",
and the 2026 guidance assumed *increased investment* in Injectables alongside momentum
elsewhere.

That is the economics in one line: **volume growth in generic sterile injectables does not
carry margin, because price is set by group-purchasing-organisation tender and the cost base is
an aseptic facility that cannot be cheapened.** A supplier facing a six-point margin decline
on growing volume rationalises presentations, and a rationalised presentation is a shortage.
No published forecast of this market prices that, which is why the 7.5%–10.5% CAGRs on offer
are forecasts that the tender economics hold.

## What we publish

**Headline: $51.30B (2026) → $93.39B (2032) at 10.5%, scoped explicitly to generic sterile
injectables.**

Source: Coherent Market Insights, generic sterile injectables market, published 3 October 2025
(via BioSpace press release), which states $46.43B for 2025, $93.39B for 2032 and a 10.5% CAGR
over 2025–2032.

The 2026 base is **derived by advancing that house's own published 2025 figure one year at its
own stated rate**: 46.43 × 1.105 = 51.30. The 2032 endpoint is that house's own published
figure, reproduced to the arithmetic: 51.30 × 1.105^6 = 93.39 against its published 93.39.

Internal check: Coherent's own series closes exactly. 46.43 × 1.105^7 = 93.39 against its
published $93.39B — an implied 10.50% against a stated 10.5%. This is the only series in the
batch whose published base, endpoint and rate reconcile to two decimal places.

Chosen because: (a) it is the only series in the set whose scope matches what the phrase
"sterile injectables market" means operationally — the supply business, not the drug revenue;
(b) the disclosed revenue of Fresenius Kabi's Pharma division and Hikma's Injectables segment
sums to 12.1% of it, which is a plausible figure for two of its largest suppliers, against
0.95% of the $591B reading; and (c) its arithmetic is internally exact.

**We state the alternative reading in the report rather than hiding it.** The whole-injectable-
drug market is a real quantity of roughly $591–632 billion for 2025, and the report says so,
labelled as the drug revenue rather than the supply business. A reader who wanted that figure
should not have to find it elsewhere.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Pharmaceuticals.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Fresenius Kabi and Hikma disclose segment revenue; neither
  discloses a share, and Pfizer, Baxter and Sandoz do not break out injectables at all. The
  12.1% figure is our own division of two disclosed revenue lines by a third party's published
  total, presented as prose with both inputs named.
  `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **Coherent's "North America ~40% of 2025."** The press release's own wording is "nearly
  two-fifths", which is a characterisation, not a measurement, and no second source agrees or
  disagrees. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Country splits.** No named source gives any. `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Coherent's "monoclonal antibodies 22.2% of 2025."** One source, one press release, no
  method — and a figure that sits oddly in a *generic* market, since most monoclonal
  antibodies on the market are not generic. Recorded here as a reason to doubt the
  segmentation, not reproduced. Segmentation goes out as plain strings.
- **The $591.4B / $632.46B / $586.1B series as the sterile injectables market.** They appear
  in the estimates table and are described in the report as the injectable drug market, which
  is what the supplier arithmetic shows them to be. They are not used for the headline.
- **The "US$616.66 million" base-year figure.** A units error. Recorded only as evidence that
  the distributor listings in this category are not proofread.
- **A single authoritative count of US drug shortages.** Three registers give 75, 93 and 227
  for overlapping periods. All three are reported, with the definitional reason for the gap,
  and none is adopted as the count.
- **Any figure for the economic cost of shortages, or lives affected.** Widely quoted in
  policy literature, not publicly registered at a checkable level.
- **Hikma's full-year Injectables operating margin.** The half-year figures (36.3% falling to
  30.0%) are disclosed and used; the full-year segment margin did not appear in the material
  we could read, and the results presentation PDF could not be decoded. Not invented.
