## The spread

**This market is the opposite case to most of the corpus, and that is the
finding.** The published estimates very nearly agree:

- **Base year: $4.4B (2023) against $4.5B (2024) — a 1.02x spread.** Two
  independent houses, one year apart, essentially the same number. That is
  almost unheard of in this literature.
- **2030 endpoints within the broad scope: $6.1B against $7.3B — 1.20x.**
- **Broad scope against narrow scope at 2030: $7.3B (AMR diagnostics) against
  $4.736B (AST only) — 1.54x.** This is the only material scope gap, and it is
  explicable: AST is the susceptibility half; AMR diagnostics also counts
  resistance-gene detection, organism identification tied to resistance, and
  surveillance assays.
- **CAGR: 5.1% to 8.51% — 1.67x.**

So the spread to report as the analytical spine is **1.54x, broad versus
narrow definition**, with a 1.67x spread on growth rate and a remarkable 1.02x
agreement on the base year.

**The story is not the disagreement. It is what the agreement reveals: this
market is small and grows at 5–6% while antimicrobial resistance is described by
the WHO as a global emergency.** A $4.4–4.5 billion market growing at
mid-single digits is not the market of a crisis being solved. It is the market
of an installed base of microbiology instruments being maintained.

## Grounding arithmetic

### Test 1 — one vendor against the whole published market

**Register used:** bioMérieux's disclosed financial reporting. bioMérieux is the
acknowledged leader in clinical microbiology and antimicrobial susceptibility
testing (the VITEK and BACT/ALERT ranges), which makes its disclosed revenue the
sharpest available test of whether a published market size is coherent.

Disclosed figures:
- Group sales FY2025: **€4,070 million**, +6.2% organic (FY2025 results release).
- Microbiology, Q2 2025: **€320.4 million**, against €324.2 million in Q2 2024
  (−1% reported, +3% at constant exchange rates).
- Microbiology, FY2025: **+4% organic** growth.
- Excluding China, VITEK and BACT/ALERT grew **+9% in Q2 2025** on both
  instruments and reagents; microbiology sales in China fell by mid-teens.

Annualise the disclosed quarterly microbiology figure and convert:

```
€320.4M x 4 = €1,281.6M
€1,281.6M x $1.09/€ = $1.397B  (~$1.40B)
```

Divide by the published whole-market base-year sizes:

```
$1.40B / $4.45B (mean of GMI's $4.4B and GIA's $4.5B) = 31.5%
```

**One vendor's microbiology franchise is roughly a third of the entire published
global AMR diagnostics market** — and that franchise includes blood culture and
organism identification as well as susceptibility, so the susceptibility-only
portion is smaller than 31.5% while the market it is being compared against is
broader than susceptibility. The ratio is therefore an upper bound on
bioMérieux's AMR share and still a third.

**What this rules in:** the $4.4–4.5 billion base-year cluster, as a coherent
whole-market figure for a genuinely concentrated category. A market in which one
participant can plausibly hold around a third is consistent with the 5–6% growth
rates published, because concentrated instrument-and-reagent installed bases grow
at the rate of their reagent pull-through.

**What this rules out:** any reading in which AMR diagnostics is a large,
fragmented market with many billion-dollar participants. It also rules out
treating the 8.51% CAGR as a whole-market rate: for the market to grow at 8.51%,
bioMérieux's microbiology franchise — a third of it, growing at 4% organically —
would have to be offset by the remaining two-thirds growing at roughly 11%, and
no public filing evidences a participant of that size doing so.

### Test 2 — the market against the surveillance system it is meant to serve

**Register used:** WHO Global Antimicrobial Resistance and Use Surveillance
System (GLASS), 2025 report, published 13 October 2025.

Disclosed figures:
- **1 in 6** laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections worldwide were resistant
  to antibiotic treatment in **2023**.
- Resistance rose in **over 40%** of monitored pathogen–antibiotic combinations
  between 2018 and 2023, at **5–15% average annual increase**.
- Highest in the South-East Asia and Eastern Mediterranean regions (**1 in 3**
  reported infections resistant) and the African Region (**1 in 5**).
- GLASS participation rose from **25 countries in 2016 to 104 in 2023**.
- **48% of countries did not report data to GLASS in 2023**, and about half of
  the reporting countries still lacked the systems to generate reliable data.

```
$4.45B global market / 104 reporting countries = $42.8M per reporting country per year
```

**$42.8 million a year is the entire global diagnostics spend attributable to
each GLASS-reporting country** — and that includes the United States, which
Global Market Insights alone sizes at $3.1 billion by 2032. The distribution is
therefore extremely skewed, and the arithmetic makes the skew concrete: the
countries with 1-in-3 resistance rates are not the countries buying the
diagnostics. That is the structural fact behind a 5–6% growth rate in a market
addressing a condition whose incidence is rising 5–15% a year.

**Both calculations are plausibility tests, not market estimates.** The
bioMérieux division uses an annualised single quarter and a single
euro/dollar rate; the GLASS division treats countries as equivalent units, which
they plainly are not. Neither establishes the correct market size.

## What we publish

**Headline:** $5.28B (2026) → $7.60B (2032) at 6.26%.

Source: Global Market Insights (November 2024), which publishes $4.4B for 2023
and $7.6B for 2032. Those endpoints imply 6.26% compound over nine years
((7.6/4.4)^(1/9) − 1 = 6.26%), consistent with GMI's own stated "over 6.3%". The
2026 base is the published 2023 value advanced three years at that implied rate
(4.4 × 1.0626³ = 5.28). The 2032 endpoint is **GMI's own published figure**,
unmodified. $5.28B → $7.60B over six years reconciles at 6.26%.

Chosen because it is the only series with both an explicit base-year value and an
explicit endpoint inside our forecast window, it is corroborated to within 1.02x
at the base year by an independent house, and it passes both grounding tests.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Life Sciences.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** bioMérieux, Becton Dickinson, Thermo Fisher, Danaher
  (Cepheid), Roche and Beckman Coulter do not disclose AMR-diagnostics-specific
  revenue. The 31.5% figure above is **our own upper-bound ratio from an
  annualised quarter**, used as a plausibility test; it must never be rendered as
  a share. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted.
- **Regional splits.** GMI publishes a US figure ($3.1B by 2032 at 6.2%), which
  is a country forecast endpoint, not a regional apportionment of our base year,
  and no house publishes a full regional table.  `regional_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Country splits.** The single US endpoint above is quoted in prose with its
  source and year; it is not a country table. `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Segment shares.** GMI's kits & reagents at $2.8B and hospitals & clinics at
  $1.8B are 2023 segment values from one house with no visible methodology, and
  the *C. difficile* $2.3B-by-2032 figure is a forecast endpoint. Segmentation
  published as plain strings with no percentages.
- **Any attribution of the 1-in-6 WHO figure to a market size.** The GLASS
  resistance rate is an epidemiological measurement. Converting it into
  addressable test volume would require a global count of bacterial infections
  that nobody publishes.
- **The 8.51% CAGR as our rate.** Recorded as the top of the published range and
  shown to be hard to reconcile with the leader's disclosed 4% organic growth.
- **A global AST test volume.** Not published anywhere.
