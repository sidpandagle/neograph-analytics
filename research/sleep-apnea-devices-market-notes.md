### Arithmetic check on the source we use

MarketsandMarkets states **10.9% for 2025–2032**. Its own endpoints:

```
$6.93B (2025) → $11.61B (2032), seven years
Implied CAGR = (11.61 / 6.93)^(1/7) - 1 = 7.65%

$6.93B compounded at the stated 10.9% for seven years = $14.4B, not $11.61B
```

**The stated rate and the stated endpoints are irreconcilable, by $2.8 billion at the horizon.** The page offers no reconciliation. We take the endpoints — which are the figures the report is actually built on and which appear consistently across reprints — and publish the rate they imply. This is recorded here so a reader comparing our 7.6% to the publisher's 10.9% knows exactly why they differ.

## The spread

**At 2025, $5.6B (KSI) to $9.13B (Arizton, extended at its own rate) — a 1.63x spread.** At 2024, $6.47B to $8.52B — 1.32x. At the 2030–2033 horizon, $7.3B to $14.4B — 1.97x. Published CAGRs run 5.2% to 10.9%, a 2.10x spread.

By this corpus's standards a 1.63x spread is narrow, and as with proteomics that is the wrong thing to be reassured by. The publishers broadly agree because they are broadly doing the same sum: PAP flow generators, masks, oral appliances, and sleep testing hardware. The scope decisions that remain visible are two: **whether masks are counted** (Arizton makes "facial interfaces" a top-level segment; MarketsandMarkets folds masks into therapeutic; KSI's much lower total looks like flow generators and diagnostics without a full mask count) and **whether diagnostics are counted** (MarketsandMarkets says yes, explicitly including polysomnography, home sleep testing and oximeters).

Masks are not a rounding error. ResMed's FY2025 disclosure splits its own Sleep and Breathing Health revenue into **$2,665.2M of devices and $1,839.7M of masks and other** — masks are 41% of that segment. A definition that omits them removes nearly two-fifths of the largest vendor's relevant revenue, which is roughly the size of the whole published spread.

**But the spread is not where this market fails. The level is.**

### Test 1 — three companies against the whole published market

```
ResMed, Sleep and Breathing Health (FY2025)        $4,504.9M
Inspire Medical Systems (FY2025)                     $912.0M
Fisher & Paykel, Homecare group (FY2025, converted)  ~$437M   (upper bound; includes non-OSA)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Three-company total                                $5,853.9M
```

Against the published 2025 totals:

```
÷ KSI's        $5.60B  =  104.5%   ← three companies EXCEED the entire published market
÷ MarketsandMarkets' $6.93B  =   84.5%
÷ Arizton's implied $9.13B   =   64.1%
```

**Three disclosures exceed one publisher's entire global sleep apnoea device market and consume 85% of another's.** And the list of what is *not* in that $5,853.9M is long, and every item on it is explicitly inside MarketsandMarkets' stated scope:

- **Philips Respironics** — barred from new sleep and ventilator device sales in the United States under the April 2024 consent decree, but still supplying new devices outside the United States, and still servicing the United States installed base and selling accessories, consumables and replacement parts there. UBS analysts estimated the decree as an approximately €400M sales headwind, which implies the business was substantially larger than that.
- **Löwenstein Medical, BMC Medical, React Health** and the other PAP flow-generator manufacturers.
- **SomnoMed, ProSomnus, Vivos Therapeutics** and the oral appliance industry — a segment MarketsandMarkets names explicitly.
- **Nyxoah** — hypoglossal nerve stimulation, Inspire's direct competitor.
- **The entire diagnostics half of the stated scope**: polysomnography systems, home sleep testing devices and oximeters, from Natus, Nihon Kohden, Itamar/ZOLL, Compumedics and others.

Add Philips' ex-United States sleep business and the United States consumables stream alone and the total passes MarketsandMarkets' $6.93B. Add the diagnostics segment that MarketsandMarkets says it is counting, and the published figure is too small to contain the market it describes.

**ResMed alone is 65.0% of MarketsandMarkets' 2025 total** ($4,504.9M ÷ $6,930M) **and 80.4% of KSI's.** A single vendor at two-thirds of a global market is possible — ResMed is the clear leader — but it is a fact the published reports do not state, and a reader who assumes a normal competitive distribution underneath a $6.93B headline is misreading the market completely.

### Test 2 — the prevalence denominator, which is the real finding

Benjafield et al., "Estimation of the global prevalence and burden of obstructive sleep apnoea: a literature-based analysis", *The Lancet Respiratory Medicine*, 2019 (PubMed 31300334):

- **936 million adults aged 30–69** with mild-to-severe OSA (95% CI 903–970 million)
- **425 million adults aged 30–69** with moderate-to-severe OSA (95% CI 399–450 million)
- Highest absolute numbers in China, then the USA, Brazil and India
- Method: PubMed and Embase search for objective-testing prevalence studies, with a conversion algorithm for studies not using AASM 2012 criteria; extrapolated to other countries by population and BMI-linked prevalence
- **Only 16 countries, from 17 studies, provided reliable prevalence data**
- **Funded by ResMed**, with 12 academic co-authors. **Flagged**: the denominator every forecast in this market leans on was paid for by the market's largest vendor, and it replaced a WHO 2007 estimate of "more than 100 million" with a figure nearly ten times larger

```
$6.93B (MarketsandMarkets, 2025) ÷ 936 million prevalent adults  = $7.40 per person per year
$6.93B                           ÷ 425 million moderate-to-severe = $16.31 per person per year
```

A PAP flow generator plus a year of masks and consumables is roughly **$700–$1,200** at manufacturer prices (consistent with ResMed's own split: $2,665.2M of devices and $1,839.7M of masks across its base).

```
$16.31 per moderate-to-severe adult per year ÷ ~$1,000 of annual device-and-mask value
= roughly 1.6% of the treatment-indicated population served
```

**The entire global sleep apnoea device industry reaches on the order of 1.6% of the population it says needs treatment.** Cross-check from the vendor side: ResMed's chief executive stated on the FY2025 earnings call that the company has sold **"30 million 100% cloud-connectable medical devices" into 140 countries**. (A spoken remark on an earnings call, not a figure in the 10-K — **flagged**.)

```
30 million ResMed devices ever sold ÷ 425 million moderate-to-severe adults = 7.1%
```

Seven percent of the indicated population has, across the market leader's entire history, ever received one of its devices. Reporting separately described more than 154 million people worldwide as ResMed customers against a stated goal of 500 million patients by 2030 — **an outlet's characterisation, not a filing figure; flagged** — and even taken at face value that is 36% of the moderate-to-severe population as a *cumulative customer* count, not an annual treated count.

### Test 3 — what the forecast is actually a claim about

```
$11.61B (MarketsandMarkets, 2032) ÷ 425 million moderate-to-severe adults = $27.32 per person per year
```

So the published forecast is, in substance, a claim that penetration of the treatment-indicated population moves from roughly **1.6% to roughly 2.7%** between 2025 and 2032 (assuming the prevalence base and per-patient device value hold). That is the whole content of the 7.65% rate, stated in terms a clinician can argue with. It is a modest, defensible claim. It is also not what "a market growing at 10.9% to $11.6 billion" sounds like.

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope and plausibility tests. They establish that the published totals are too small to contain the vendors their own scope statements name, that ResMed alone is roughly two-thirds of the mid-range published figure, that the industry reaches on the order of 1.6% of its stated indicated population, and that the forecast is a penetration claim of 1.6% to 2.7%. They do not establish the correct figure. The Fisher & Paykel conversion uses our own approximate exchange rate and its Homecare group includes non-OSA products, so that line is an upper bound; the ResMed 30-million device count is a spoken earnings-call remark; the prevalence base was funded by ResMed and rests on reliable data from only 16 countries; the $700–$1,200 per-patient device value is our own characterisation from ResMed's device-to-mask revenue split rather than a published price; and dividing company revenue into a published market total compares differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $7.46B (2026) → $11.61B (2032) at 7.6%.**

Source: MarketsandMarkets, *Sleep Apnea Devices Market*, report MD 1181, published June 2025, which states $6.47B for 2024, **$6.93B for 2025** and **$11.61B for 2032**. The 2026 base is **derived by advancing MarketsandMarkets' own published 2025 figure one year at the rate its own endpoints imply** (6.93 × 1.0765 = 7.46). The 2032 endpoint is MarketsandMarkets' own published figure, unaltered. The rate is **the one its endpoints imply (7.65%, published as 7.6%), not the 10.9% its page states** — because $6.93B compounded at 10.9% for seven years reaches $14.4B, not the $11.61B the same page publishes. The discrepancy and our choice are documented above and restated in the report.

Chosen because: (a) it publishes **by far the most explicit scope** in the set, naming both halves and every product class inside them — therapeutic (CPAP, APAP, BPAP, oral appliances, masks) and diagnostic (polysomnography, home sleep testing, oximeters) — which is what made the containment test possible; (b) it carries a **stated publication date** (June 2025) and a dated 2024 base and 2025 estimate, where most of the set carries neither; (c) it uniquely states that it reports **volume as well as value**, which is the right instinct for this market; and (d) its endpoint-implied 7.65% sits between Arizton's 7.20% and Emergen's 6.9% on one side and KSI's 5.2% on the other, so the arithmetic-corrected rate is corroborated rather than idiosyncratic.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Medical Devices.

The report states plainly in `market_context`, `forecast_analysis` and `research_methodology` that the published total is too small to contain its own named vendors, that ResMed alone is roughly 65% of it, that the industry reaches on the order of 1.6% of the treatment-indicated population, and that the publisher's own stated CAGR fails its own arithmetic.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** None is disclosed. ResMed's Sleep and Breathing Health revenue divided by a published market total is *our* division of a reported figure by a third-party estimate, stated in prose with both inputs named, and never published as a share. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **MarketsandMarkets' 10.9% CAGR.** It fails its own endpoints by $2.8 billion at the horizon. We publish the implied 7.6% and document why.
- **Regional splits.** MarketsandMarkets names North America as largest and Asia Pacific as fastest at 11.8% but publishes no regional dollar figures, and no other source gives a 2026 split. ResMed's own geographic split ($2,997.5M in U.S./Canada/Latin America against $1,507.4M in combined Europe, Asia and other markets) is one company's revenue, not a market split, and is reported as such in the competitive section. `regional_analysis` and `country_analysis` stay `[]`.
- **MarketsandMarkets' demographic segments** (40–60 age group largest and 11.8% CAGR, males larger share, home care largest end user at 11.8%). One source, one page, no dollar values, no method. Segmentation goes out as plain strings.
- **A market size of our own.** Our containment test shows the published figures are too small; it does not produce a replacement. We publish the published figure, labelled, and the arithmetic showing what it omits.
- **A Fisher & Paykel OSA revenue figure.** NZ$739.9M is the whole Homecare group, which includes humidification and nasal high flow. Used only as a labelled upper bound, with our own approximate exchange rate flagged.
- **Philips Respironics' sleep revenue.** Philips does not disclose it. UBS's approximately €400M headwind estimate is an analyst figure about the United States consent decree's impact, not a revenue line, and is cited as such.
- **A global treated-patient count.** Nobody publishes one. ResMed's 30 million cumulative cloud-connectable devices is a spoken earnings-call figure, used only in the flagged penetration calculation.
- **An annual device replacement cycle or per-patient price.** The $700–$1,200 range is our characterisation from ResMed's device-to-mask revenue split, not a published price, and appears only inside the flagged penetration arithmetic.
- **Arizton's, KSI's, IMARC's, Emergen's, Technavio's or Data Bridge's series as anything but spread.** All six were read from search summaries or mirrored pages.
