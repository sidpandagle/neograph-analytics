# Patient Monitoring Devices — research notes
**Slug:** patient-monitoring-devices-market   **Drafted:** 2026-10-18

Pipeline row 49, round 6A. Industry: Medical Devices.

Unlike the other round 6 markets, **the price of a patient monitor is not
published anywhere** — no fee schedule, no statutory tariff, no reference price,
no public tender register that covers the category globally. Everything below
that looks like a unit economic is a division of a published market figure by a
countable denominator, and it can only bound a scope. It cannot price a device.

### Where the remote / wearable boundary actually sits

Established from each publisher's own page, not assumed:

- **Explicitly in, as a named segment:** TBRC (standalone RPM category with
  telemedicine, wearable and home-health subsegments), both Data Bridge reports
  ("Remote Patient" product segment), iData (RPM a sized segment, incl. cardiac
  implantable electronic devices), Grand View (RPM and wearables are the *type*
  axis), Towards Healthcare (states home-care monitoring *is* RPM).
- **Wearables in, RPM partly out:** GMI sells "smart wearable ECG patches" inside
  cardiac monitoring but never states the RPM boundary.
- **Split by editorial decision:** Fortune includes blood glucose (CGM + SMBG)
  as a product type and homecare as an end user, but routes wearable sensors and
  RPM devices to *separate* reports. So two ~USD 52 bn figures (Fortune and GMI)
  that look like agreement are not measuring the same thing.
- **Not stated at all:** BCC Research, Next Move.

The conclusion that matters: **remote and wearable monitoring is inside most of
these boundaries, and so is self-monitoring of blood glucose.** That inclusion,
not growth, is what moves the number.

## The spread

Across the nine global estimates, all device-category readings (i.e. excluding
Grand View's "digital patient monitoring", which is a different category — see
below):

- **Base-year spread: 4.93x.** USD 63.94 bn (Towards Healthcare, 2025) ÷
  USD 12.98 bn (Data Bridge device/system/equipment, 2024) = **4.9260**.
- Restricted to the five estimates that share a **2025** base year:
  USD 63.94 bn ÷ USD 26.08 bn (TBRC) = **2.4517x**.
- **CAGR spread: 1.36x.** 10.50% (Data Bridge systems) ÷ 7.73% (Towards
  Healthcare) = **1.3583**.

Including Grand View's digital reading: base-year spread USD 178.1 bn ÷
USD 12.98 bn = **13.7211x**, CAGR spread 25.2% ÷ 7.73% = **3.2600x**.

**Verdict: scope disagreement, decisively.** The base year spreads 4.93x while
the growth rate spreads 1.36x. Everyone is modelling roughly the same expansion
and starting from wildly different places.

The strongest single piece of evidence is not a comparison between publishers —
it is **one publisher disagreeing with itself**. Data Bridge publishes two
reports whose product lists, end-use lists and forecast periods are *identical
as printed*: "Patient Monitoring Device/System/Equipment" at USD 12.98 bn for
2024, and "Patient Monitoring Systems" at USD 56.65 bn for 2024. That is
**4.3644x on the same base year from the same house with the same published
segmentation**, which rules out any reading in which the gap is a methodological
disagreement about growth, demand or demographics. It is a boundary decision
that the published segmentation does not disclose.

### The tiers

- **Tier 1 — acute-care monitoring equipment.** Bedside multiparameter monitors,
  telemetry, central stations, fetal/neonatal, neuro, capnography, cardiac
  output. Data Bridge "device/system/equipment" at USD 12.98 bn (2024) sits
  here. iData's US-only USD 10 bn+ (2025) is consistent with a global tier-1
  figure in the teens of billions, since the US is the largest single
  geography — but see the caution on that inference below.
- **Tier 2 — tier 1 plus home / remote monitoring plus self-monitoring of blood
  pressure and blood glucose.** TBRC USD 26.08 bn and BCC USD 36.7 bn (both
  2025).
- **Tier 3 — tier 2 plus continuous glucose monitoring, wearables and connected
  consumer devices.** GMI USD 51.7 bn, Fortune USD 52.74 bn, Towards Healthcare
  USD 63.94 bn (2025); Data Bridge "systems" USD 56.65 bn (2024).
- **Tier 4 — not a device market at all.** Grand View's "digital patient
  monitoring devices" has mHealth and telehealth on its *type* axis: it is
  counting platforms and services. USD 178.1 bn in 2024 at 25.2% should never be
  compared with any of the above, and this report does not.

The step from tier 2 to tier 3 is roughly USD 26 bn, and continuous glucose
monitoring alone plausibly accounts for most of it: **Dexcom's full-year 2025
revenue was USD 4.662 bn** (up 16% reported) and **Abbott's full-year 2025
Diabetes Care sales were USD 7,998 m** (US USD 3,181 m, international
USD 4,817 m), of which Q4 2025 CGM sales alone were about USD 2.0 bn. Two
companies that no one would describe as patient-monitoring vendors carry over
USD 12 bn of revenue that tier-3 definitions pull in and tier-1 definitions do
not. Abbott's **full-year** CGM figure is not disclosed in the Q4 release, so no
full-year CGM total is published anywhere in this report.

## Grounding arithmetic

### 1. Dollars per staffed bed (the primary division)

**Denominator — AHA Annual Survey, countable and published.** AHA *Fast Facts on
U.S. Hospitals*, from the **2024 AHA Annual Survey** (sampled into *AHA Hospital
Statistics*, 2026 edition):

- All US hospitals: **6,100**
- **Staffed beds, all US hospitals: 907,216**
- Community hospitals: 5,121; **staffed beds, community hospitals: 775,297**
- Admissions, all US hospitals: 35,658,583

**Numerator — the only US-scoped published estimate.** iData Research, published
05/25/2026: the US patient monitoring equipment market is **over USD 10 billion
in 2025**, reaching over USD 13 billion by 2032 at 3.7%.

```
USD 10,000,000,000 ÷ 907,216 staffed beds  = USD 11,022.73 per staffed bed/year
USD 10,000,000,000 ÷ 775,297 community staffed beds = USD 12,898.28 per staffed bed/year
```

**What this division can establish.** That a US patient-monitoring figure of
~USD 10 bn is dimensionally sane for a scope that is *more* than bedside
monitors. A multiparameter bedside monitor is a five-figure capital purchase on a
replacement cycle usually quoted in years, so recognised equipment revenue per
bed per year from monitors alone should sit well below USD 11,023. The gap is
absorbed by disposable sensors and cuffs, central-station and software licences,
service contracts, and — in iData's published scope — remote patient monitoring
and personal/home blood-pressure monitoring, which have no hospital bed behind
them at all. The figure is therefore consistent with iData's stated scope and
would *not* be consistent with a monitors-only reading.

**What this division cannot establish.** Not the market size, and above all not a
price. **There is no published price for a patient monitor.** No CMS fee
schedule, no statutory tariff, no national reference-price list and no global
tender register covers the category, so unlike the statutory-price markets
elsewhere in round 6 there is nothing to multiply a unit count against. A
per-bed figure of USD 11,023 is a plausibility band on a published estimate; it
is not a bottom-up build, it is not offered as one, and no part of this report's
headline derives from it.

**Denominator cross-check.** CMS's own hospital counts, tabulated by KFF from
CMS (Office of Enterprise Data and Analytics), give **5,928 Medicare-certified
hospitals and 917,505 hospital beds for 2023**. Against AHA's 907,216 staffed
beds the two registers agree to **1.13%** despite counting different things
(certified beds vs staffed beds) in different years, so the denominator is
robust to the choice of register. Note honestly: the **CMS Provider of Services
file itself could not be downloaded in this pass** — data.cms.gov and cms.gov
returned HTTP 403 to every request — so the CMS figure used here is CMS's
published derivative as tabulated by KFF, not a count this pass made from the POS
file. A later pass that can reach the file should recount it.

### 2. Vendor segment revenue against the published market (no FX needed)

Two of the three vendors are reported in dollars, so these two can be summed
without an exchange-rate assumption:

- **GE HealthCare, Patient Care Solutions (PCS)**, FY2025 Form 10-K
  (gehc-20251231): segment revenues **USD 3,086 m** in 2025, USD 3,125 m in
  2024, against total company revenues of USD 20,625 m (2025) and USD 19,672 m
  (2024). The 10-K describes PCS as serving "care teams and healthcare systems
  across multiple patient care needs… including Monitoring Solutions and Life
  Support Solutions", where "Monitoring Solutions includes Patient Monitoring and
  Diagnostic Cardiology" and "Life Support Solutions includes Maternal Infant
  Care and Anesthesia". PCS revenue has declined three years running
  (3,142 → 3,125 → 3,086 on the sequence reported for 2023–2025).
- **Baxter, Front Line Care** (the former Hillrom division), FY2025 Form 10-K
  (bax-20251231): net sales **USD 1,160 m** in 2025, up 2% from USD 1,137 m in
  2024, growth attributed mainly to cardiology products and partly offset by exit
  from a respiratory health product line. Front Line Care is one of two divisions
  in Healthcare Systems & Technologies (total USD 3,071 m; Care and Connectivity
  Solutions USD 1,911 m).

```
USD 3,086 m (GE PCS) + USD 1,160 m (Baxter FLC) = USD 4,246 m
USD 4,246 m ÷ USD 26,080 m (TBRC 2025 base)  = 16.28%
USD 4,246 m ÷ USD 52,740 m (Fortune 2025 base) =  8.05%
```

Two vendors that every one of these publishers names among the leaders hold
**8.05%** of the tier-3 market as published. That is not a leadership structure;
it is evidence that the tier-3 figure counts a product population these vendors
do not sell into.

### 3. Philips' own addressable market, against the publishers'

From Philips' Capital Markets Day deck of 10 February 2026 (Connected Care,
Julia Strandberg) — read directly:

- Connected Care **2025 sales: EUR 5.1 billion**; adjusted EBITA margin 10.7%.
  Split: **Monitoring ~59% of Connected Care**, Enterprise Informatics ~22%,
  Sleep & Respiratory Care ~19% (footnote: "Including Emergency Care. Divestment
  completed in Q4 2025").
- **"EUR 23 billion addressable market in 2025", "~4% CAGR (2025–2028)"** — for
  the *whole* of Connected Care, i.e. monitoring **plus** enterprise informatics
  **plus** sleep and respiratory care.
- Monitoring claims: "1 out of every 2 patient monitors, globally", "#1 patient
  monitoring device player", "**~54% market share** and growing", footnoted
  "Philips **estimated** equipment market share based on the last twelve months
  for Patient Monitors; **data on file**".

```
Monitoring sales   ≈ 0.59 × EUR 5.1 bn = EUR 3.009 bn  (≈ EUR 3.0 bn)
If that is ~54% of patient-monitor equipment:
                     EUR 3.009 bn ÷ 0.54 = EUR 5.572 bn  (≈ EUR 5.6 bn)
```

EUR 5.6 bn is an **upper bound**, not an estimate, because the EUR 3.0 bn
includes consumables, software and services while the 54% is an *equipment*
share — so the true implied equipment market is smaller still.

And the TAM comparison, stated so that it survives any plausible exchange rate:

```
Fortune 2025 base USD 52.74 bn ÷ Philips TAM EUR 23 bn at 1.00 USD/EUR = 2.2930x
Fortune 2025 base USD 52.74 bn ÷ Philips TAM EUR 23 bn at 1.20 USD/EUR = 1.9109x
```

At any rate between 1.00 and 1.20, **the publishers' tier-3 figure for patient
monitoring devices alone is 1.9x to 2.3x the market leader's own addressable
market for a strictly larger product set** — and the leader puts that market's
growth at ~4% a year, against the publishers' 7.7–10.5%. Both the level and the
rate disagree with the vendor that claims half the monitors in the world.

### 4. FDA 510(k) register: the clearance pipeline is mature, not compounding

Counted from the openFDA 510(k) endpoint on 2026-10-18. Product code **MWI** is
21 CFR **870.2300**, class **II**, "Monitor, Physiological, Patient (Without
Arrhythmia Detection Or Alarms)"; **MHX** is the counterpart with arrhythmia
detection or alarms.

| Product code | What it is | 510(k) clearances, all time |
|---|---|---|
| MWI | Physiological patient monitor, without arrhythmia detection/alarms | 308 |
| MHX | Physiological patient monitor, with arrhythmia detection/alarms | 430 |
| MWI + MHX | combined | 738 |
| DQA | Oximeter | 727 |
| DQK | Apnea monitor (infant) | 439 |
| DRG | Non-invasive blood pressure monitor | 192 |

New clearances per year, MWI + MHX, by decision date:

| 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | 20 | 16 | 15 | 12 |

Twelve to twenty new physiological-monitor clearances a year, with **2025 the
lowest of the five**, is the regulatory signature of a mature, low-churn device
class. It is not the signature of a category compounding at 25.2%, or even at
10.5%. Whatever growth the high estimates describe is being cleared under other
product codes — CGM, wearables, software — which is exactly what the tier
analysis predicts. (Caveat: a 510(k) count measures regulatory activity, not
revenue, and does not distinguish a platform refresh from a new entrant.)

## Do not publish

Everything in this list is a figure that was available and tempting, and is not
defensible. Each must survive into the JSON as an **empty array** or an absence,
never a guess.

- **`regional_analysis`: `[]`.** No source surveyed publishes a regional split of
  this market that survives scrutiny, and the one regional datapoint in hand
  (iData's US-only USD 10 bn against Fortune's USD 52.74 bn global = 18.96%) is
  a comparison across two publishers with different scopes. Dividing one house's
  US number by another house's global number is not a regional share and will not
  be presented as one.
- **`country_analysis`: `[]`.** Same reason, with less data.
- **`recent_developments`: `[]`.** Nothing verified to a company, a year and an
  event in this pass. GE HealthCare's review of strategic options for PCS and
  Philips' Q4 2025 Emergency Care divestment were both seen, but only in a trade
  report and a deck footnote respectively — not firm enough to publish as dated
  events.
- **No `market_share` anywhere — not in `competitive_landscape`, not in
  `market_segmentation`, not in `regional_analysis`.** Vendor segment revenue
  *is* disclosed (GE PCS USD 3,086 m; Baxter Front Line Care USD 1,160 m;
  Philips Monitoring ≈ EUR 3.0 bn derived from a disclosed percentage of a
  disclosed total) — **and the segment boundaries differ between all three**. GE's
  PCS bundles anaesthesia and maternal-infant care with monitoring. Baxter's
  Front Line Care sits beside a separate Care and Connectivity Solutions division
  that also carries connected-care products. Philips' Monitoring includes
  Emergency Care and, per the footnote, a business divested in Q4 2025. Summing
  three different boundaries and dividing by a fourth party's market definition
  produces an implied share that is an artefact of four unrelated scope
  decisions. **No implied share is built from them.**
- **Philips' "~54%" and "1 out of every 2 patient monitors" are not published as
  share.** The deck footnotes them as a Philips *estimate* on *data on file*.
  They are reported in this report as what the company claims, attributed, and
  used only as the input to an upper-bound calculation that is labelled as such.
- **Abbott's full-year 2025 CGM revenue.** Not disclosed in the Q4 2025 release.
  A widely repeated "over USD 7.5 bn for the year" traces to an earnings-call
  paraphrase, not to a filing table, so it is not used. Diabetes Care segment
  sales (USD 7,998 m) are used instead, labelled as the segment and not as CGM.
- **MarketsandMarkets and Future Data Stats figures.** Seen only in search
  summaries; the publisher pages could not be confirmed. Not used.
- **BCC Research's base year.** Its own page says USD 33.9 bn for 2024 in the
  synopsis and for 2023 in the FAQ. Only the 2025 estimate (USD 36.7 bn) is used,
  and the contradiction is disclosed rather than silently resolved.
- **Any per-unit price for a patient monitor.** None is published. No
  price-times-volume build appears anywhere in this report.
- **A count of hospitals or beds made from the CMS Provider of Services file.**
  CMS endpoints returned HTTP 403 throughout this pass. The CMS-derived figure
  used is KFF's tabulation of CMS data, labelled as such.
