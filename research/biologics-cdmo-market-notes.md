## The spread

**At 2025, $18.8B to $25.32B — only 1.35x.** That is the narrowest base-year spread in this
batch, and it is misleading, because the disagreement has moved into the growth rate.

**Published CAGRs run 7.14% to 15.5% — a 2.17x spread in the rate itself.** That is the wide
dimension here, and it is unusual: most markets in this corpus disagree about the base and
roughly agree about growth. Biologics CDMO does the opposite. Three houses sit at 7.1–7.5%
(Mordor, Lucintel), three at 9.1–11.7% (Precedence, P&S, DelveInsight), and three at
14.4–15.5% (Research and Markets, The Business Research Company, Nova One Advisor).

Run the extremes forward from a common 2025 base of $23B and the forecasts diverge violently:

```
23.0 x 1.0714^9  = 23.0 x 1.863 = $42.8B  by 2034
23.0 x 1.1550^9  = 23.0 x 3.508 = $80.7B  by 2034
```

**A 1.9x gap by 2034 from the same starting point.** Nova One Advisor's $94.60B for 2034 sits
above even that, which requires both the high base and the high rate.

The scope decision that explains it is visible on the P&S page: it reports **drug substance at
roughly 80% of the 2024 market and commercial (not clinical) manufacturing at 75%.** A house
that counts only bulk drug substance manufacture gets a smaller, slower market. A house that
adds fill-finish, drug product, analytical and regulatory services, and biosimilar programme
work gets a larger, faster one, because those adjacent service lines are growing faster than
bioreactor hours. None of the pages say which they did. One industry commentary on this spread
says exactly that: the variation "reflects different definitions of what constitutes the
biologics CDMO market, whether analytical services, regulatory support, and fill-finish
operations are included, and whether biosimilar manufacturing services are fully captured
within scope." (*Read from a search-result summary of an imapac commentary dated 19 May 2026 —
flagged as a secondary characterisation, not a primary source.*)

## Grounding arithmetic

This market is unusually well served for a grounding test, because the three largest
independent biologics CDMOs are all listed and all report segment revenue, and one of them
publishes installed bioreactor capacity in litres.

### Anchor 1 — sum what the top three disclose, in USD

Converted at the **IRS yearly average currency exchange rates for 2025**, the table of annual
average rates the IRS publishes for converting foreign currency to US dollars, retrieved
16 October 2026: **CHF 0.831**, **KRW 1,421.779** and **CNY 7.129** per US dollar. (The IRS
page notes these rates do not apply to tax payments made to the IRS; they are used here only
as a named, dated conversion basis.)

| Company | Disclosed FY2025 segment revenue | ÷ 2025 IRS average rate | USD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lonza — Integrated Biologics | CHF 3.65bn (+26.6%; core EBITDA CHF 1.29bn, 35.3% margin) | 3,650 / 0.831 | **$4,392M** |
| Samsung Biologics — CDMO business, post-spin-off | KRW 4,557bn (+30.3% on KRW 3,497.1bn) | 4,557,000 / 1,421.779 | **$3,205M** |
| WuXi Biologics — group revenue | RMB 21.8bn (+16.7%) | 21,800 / 7.129 | **$3,058M** |
| | | **Sum of three** | **$10,655M** |

Set that against the published whole-market figures for 2025:

```
10,655 / 18,800  = 56.7%   of P&S Intelligence's $18.8B
10,655 / 23,000  = 46.3%   of DelveInsight's $23.00B
10,655 / 25,320  = 42.1%   of Mordor Intelligence's $25.32B
```

**Three companies are between 42% and 57% of the entire published market.** And these are not
the only large players: Fujifilm Diosynth Biotechnologies, Catalent (now held by Novo
Holdings), Thermo Fisher's Pharma Services division, Boehringer Ingelheim BioXcellence, AGC
Biologics and Siegfried all run commercial-scale biologics capacity and are not in the sum.
None of them disclose a biologics-CDMO-only revenue line, so they cannot be added without
guessing — but their existence means the real top-handful concentration is higher than 57%.

**P&S Intelligence's own page calls this market "fragmented."** Three companies at 57% of its
own total is not a fragmented market. Either the characterisation is wrong or the total is
too low. This is the finding, and it is a disagreement between two statements on the same page
rather than between two publishers.

*Caveat stated plainly:* Lonza's Integrated Biologics division includes bioconjugates and
mammalian and microbial drug substance and drug product; WuXi Biologics' RMB 21.8bn is group
revenue, which includes discovery and development services that a narrow drug-substance scope
would exclude; Samsung Biologics' KRW 4,557bn is the post-spin-off CDMO entity. All three are
therefore slightly **wider** than a pure drug-substance definition and slightly **narrower**
than a definition including fill-finish done by others. The sum is a scale check, not an
apportionment.

### Anchor 2 — revenue per litre of installed capacity

Samsung Biologics is the only one of the three that publishes installed bioreactor capacity in
litres, and it also stated which plants were fully utilised in 2025. That makes a direct
productivity calculation possible.

Disclosed in its fourth-quarter and fiscal-year 2025 results (21 January 2026) and the
accompanying capacity statements:

```
Songdo Plants 1-5 installed capacity        785,000 L
  of which Plant 5 (Bio Campus II)          180,000 L  (ramping, not full-year revenue)
  Plants 1-4, stated at full utilisation    605,000 L
Rockville, Maryland                          60,000 L  (added; global total ~845,000 L)
```

Plants 1 through 3 ran at full utilisation through 2025 and **Plant 4 reached full utilisation
in Q3 2025**, which is the reason the company gave for 30.3% revenue growth. So 605,000 litres
is close to the capacity that actually earned the KRW 4,557bn.

```
$3,205,000,000 / 605,000 L = $5,298 per litre of installed capacity per year
```

Now invert it. To support the published market sizes entirely out of mammalian drug-substance
manufacture at Samsung's revenue density, the world would need:

| Published 2025 market | ÷ $5,298/L | Implied fully-utilised third-party capacity |
|---|---|---|
| P&S, $18.8B | 18,800,000,000 / 5,298 | **3.55 million litres** |
| DelveInsight, $23.0B | 23,000,000,000 / 5,298 | **4.34 million litres** |
| Mordor, $25.32B | 25,320,000,000 / 5,298 | **4.78 million litres** |

Samsung Biologics — the single largest installed CDMO capacity in the world — holds about
845,000 litres of that. **Mordor's figure requires 5.7 Samsung Biologics' worth of
fully-utilised third-party capacity.** Lonza, WuXi Biologics, Fujifilm Diosynth, Boehringer
and the rest together plausibly reach a few million litres of nameplate capacity, but
nameplate is not utilisation: the industry's persistent complaint through 2024–2025 was
*under*-utilisation outside the top tier, and Lonza's own disclosure of CHF 1.3bn of capex at
19.6% of sales is the behaviour of a company still building, not one running full.

**What this rules in and out.** It rules the $18.8B end in as a drug-substance-weighted
reading. It rules the $25B+ bases out as pure bioreactor revenue — they are only reachable if
drug product, fill-finish, analytical, regulatory and development service revenue is inside
the scope, which is exactly the scope ambiguity the estimates table shows. It does **not**
establish a correct market size.

*Caveat stated plainly:* $5,298 per litre is one company's revenue density, for a
high-titre monoclonal-antibody-weighted book at full utilisation, in a year of 30% growth. A
CDMO running viral vector, microbial or early-clinical work earns a different figure per
litre, often a much higher one per litre on small volumes. This is a plausibility test, not a
market estimate.

## What we publish

**Headline: $20.87B (2026) → $39.03B (2032) at 11.0%.**

Source: P&S Intelligence, *Biologics CDMO Market Size & Share Analysis*, report 12310, 270
pages, published August 2025, which states $17.1B for 2024, $18.8B for 2025, $39.0B for 2032
and an 11.0% CAGR over 2025–2032.

The 2026 base is **derived by advancing that house's own published 2025 figure one year at its
own stated rate**: 18.8 × 1.11 = 20.87. The 2032 endpoint is that house's own published
figure, reproduced to the arithmetic: 20.87 × 1.11^6 = 39.03 against its published 39.0.
Labelled as interpolation in the report.

Internal check: P&S's own series closes. 18.8 × 1.11^7 = 39.04 against its published $39.0B.
It is the only series in the set whose stated base, stated endpoint and stated rate reconcile
to within a rounding unit.

Chosen because: (a) its forecast window is exactly 2025–2032, so the endpoint is published
rather than extrapolated to our window; (b) it carries the **lowest base in the set**, which
the revenue-per-litre test above is the only one of the published bases to survive as a
drug-substance reading; (c) its scope is disclosed in enough detail — drug substance 80%,
commercial 75%, mammalian 65% — that a reader can see what the number counts; and (d) its
11.0% CAGR sits in the middle of the 7.14%–15.5% published range, which is the honest place to
sit when the growth rate is the contested dimension.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Pharmaceuticals.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Lonza, Samsung Biologics and WuXi Biologics disclose revenue; none
  discloses a market share, and the denominator is exactly what is in dispute. The 42–57%
  range above is our own division of their disclosed revenue by other people's published
  totals and is presented as prose, with both inputs named.
  `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **P&S's "North America 40% of 2024."** One unexplained percentage, one page, no method, and
  no second source agrees or disagrees. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`. This is a market where
  the geography genuinely matters — Samsung Biologics is Korean, WuXi Biologics is Chinese,
  Lonza is Swiss with large US capacity — and a 40% North America figure in a market whose
  three largest suppliers are not North American would need explaining, not reproducing.
- **Country splits.** No named source gives any. `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **P&S's segment shares** (drug substance 80%, mammalian 65%, commercial 75%, Asia-Pacific
  11.5% CAGR). One source, one page, no method. Segmentation goes out as plain strings. They
  are discussed in the notes above because they are the clue to the scope gap, not because
  they are measurements we stand behind.
- **A global total of installed third-party bioreactor capacity.** Nobody publishes one. The
  3.55–4.78 million litre figures are our own division of published market totals by one
  company's revenue density and are labelled as such.
- **Nova One Advisor's $94.60B and The Business Research Company's $50.29B as anything but
  spread.** The first requires both the highest base and the highest rate in the set; the
  second was read from a syndicated newspaper reprint of a press release rather than the
  primary release. Both appear in the estimates table, flagged, and are used for nothing else.
- **Mordor Intelligence's figures as publisher-confirmed.** The direct page URL returned 404
  on two spellings; the $25.32B / $38.29B / 7.14% values were read from a search-result
  summary. They are recorded as the low-growth end of the spread and flagged.
- **A biosimilar-manufacturing sub-market.** Several pages assert biosimilar CDMO work is
  inside or outside their scope; none quantifies it.
- **Utilisation rates.** Samsung Biologics states which plants are full; Lonza, WuXi Biologics
  and the private CDMOs do not publish utilisation, so no industry utilisation figure is
  derivable.
