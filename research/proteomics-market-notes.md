## The spread

**At 2025, $31.0B (BCC) to $41.97B — a 1.35x spread.** At 2032, $96.81B to $126.2B — 1.30x.
Published CAGRs run 12.0% to 16.55%, a 1.38x spread.

By the standards of this corpus that is remarkable agreement, and it is the wrong thing to
be reassured by. The houses agree because they are all doing the same thing: adding up
protein-related *spend* across instruments, every consumable that touches a protein, and
services. What none of them does is size the thing the sector's investment narrative is
about — next-generation, high-plex proteomic platforms. The definitional work is visible in
BCC's own exclusion note: it leaves out "protein structural analysis and protein-expression
products", and that choice alone separates its $31.0B from GMI's $41.2B for the same year.

GMI's segmentation makes the breadth explicit. Its consumables-and-reagents category, 69.4%
of the 2025 total, is defined to include "protein chips, assay kits, buffers, chromatography
columns and electrophoresis gels". Buffers and gels are laboratory supplies. Counting them
makes the number large; it also makes the number a measure of laboratory activity rather
than of an addressable platform market.

**So the disagreement in this market is not between publishers. It is between every
publisher and the companies.**

## Grounding arithmetic

**Registers used:** FY2025 audited and reported revenue for Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bruker,
Quanterix, Standard BioTools and Seer; and the announced consideration for Illumina's
acquisition of SomaLogic.

### Test 1 — the instrument line against the largest instrument company

GMI puts **proteomics instruments alone at $8.0B in 2025.**

Thermo Fisher Scientific's **entire Analytical Instruments segment** — chromatography and mass
spectrometry, electron and ion-beam microscopy, and chemical analysis, sold into pharma,
academia, semiconductors, forensics, food and environmental testing — was **$7.55B in FY2025**
(up 1%, flat organically, from $7.46B).

```
$8.0B  (GMI's proteomics instruments, 2025)
÷ $7.55B (Thermo Fisher's whole Analytical Instruments segment, FY2025)
= 1.06x
```

Proteomics instruments, on GMI's count, are worth 6% *more* than the whole instrument
business of the world's largest analytical-instrument company — a business in which
proteomics is a minority application of one product line.

### Test 2 — the total against the two largest suppliers

```
Thermo Fisher Analytical Instruments, FY2025   $7.55B
Bruker Corporation, FY2025 total revenue       $3.44B
--------------------------------------------------------
                                              $10.99B

GMI total proteomics market, 2025:             $41.2B
$41.2B ÷ $10.99B = 3.75x
```

And against Thermo Fisher's **whole company** — all of life sciences, diagnostics,
laboratory products and pharma services — FY2025 revenue of **$44.56B**:
41.2 / 44.56 = **92.5%**. A "proteomics market" nine-tenths the size of Thermo Fisher entire
is not a market anyone can sell into; it is a measure of how much protein work the world does.

### Test 3 — the published total against the disclosed pure-play sector

Every company whose business is next-generation proteomics and which reports publicly,
FY2025:

| Company | FY2025 revenue | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Quanterix Corporation | $138.9M | Includes $21.9M of Akoya (spatial) product revenue from close date |
| SomaLogic | ~$99.7M | **Derived**: Standard BioTools' preliminary combined-company revenue of ~$185M less ~$85.3M continuing operations. Labelled as our derivation |
| Standard BioTools Inc. (continuing) | $85.3M | Microfluidics and mass cytometry |
| Seer, Inc. | $16.6M | Product $11.2M, service $4.9M; installed base 82 instruments |
| **Sum** | **$340.5M** | |

```
$340.5M ÷ $41.2B = 0.83%
$41.2B ÷ $340.5M = 121x
```

**The entire disclosed revenue of the next-generation proteomics platform sector is 0.83% of
the published proteomics market.** Olink is inside Thermo Fisher's Life Sciences Solutions
segment and not separately disclosed; Nautilus Biotechnology is pre-commercial; Bruker's
timsTOF proteomics revenue is inside BSI CALID ($1.21B, +11%) and not broken out. So $340.5M
is a floor for the pure-play sector — but it would have to be off by two orders of magnitude
for the published totals to be describing the same thing.

A price check says the same. **Illumina agreed to buy SomaLogic for $350M in cash plus up to
$75M in near-term milestones and royalties** (announced 23 June 2025, completed 30 January
2026). The cash consideration for one of the two leading affinity-proteomics platforms is
roughly equal to the *entire disclosed revenue* of the pure-play sector, and 0.85% of the
published market.

**Conclusion of the test.** The published $31–42B figures are not wrong so much as they are
answering a different question. They measure protein-related laboratory and service spend,
including buffers, gels and columns, across pharma, academia and diagnostics. They do not
measure a purchasable proteomics-platform market, which is measurable at a few hundred
million dollars of disclosed revenue. **A reader who takes a $41B headline as the addressable
market for a proteomics platform company is out by roughly 100x.** That gap is this report's
finding.

**This is a scope and plausibility test, not a market estimate.** It does not establish the
correct figure for either reading. The $99.7M SomaLogic line is our own subtraction from two
of Standard BioTools' own disclosures; Olink, Nautilus, Bruker's proteomics line and all
private vendors are outside the $340.5M sum; and dividing one company's segment revenue into
another company's market estimate compares two differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $35.03B (2026) → $72.93B (2032) at 13.0%.**

Source: BCC Research, *Proteomics: Technologies and Global Markets*, report BIO034G,
published May 2025, which states $27.6B for 2024, $31.0B for 2025, $57.2B for 2030 and a
13.0% CAGR over 2025–2030. The 2026 base is **derived by advancing BCC's own published 2025
figure one year at BCC's own stated rate** (31.0 × 1.13 = 35.03) and the 2032 endpoint by
continuing that rate (35.03 × 1.13^6 = 72.93). Labelled as interpolation in the report.

Internal check: the modelled 2030 point of our series is $57.12B against BCC's own published
2030 figure of $57.2B — a 0.14% difference, which confirms the interpolation is faithful.

Chosen because: (a) BCC publishes the **narrowest stated scope** in the set and is the only
house to publish an explicit exclusion list, which makes it the most checkable; (b) it carries
the **lowest base** among the dated full-scope series, so it is the least inflated reading of
a definition we have shown to be very wide; and (c) 13.0% sits at the bottom of the published
CAGR range.

The report states plainly, in `market_context` and `forecast_analysis`, that this figure is a
measure of protein-related tools and consumables spend under a wide consumables definition,
and that the disclosed revenue of the next-generation proteomics platform sector is $340.5M —
roughly 1% of it.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Life Sciences.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** GMI's page asserts a combined top-five share of 54.4% in one
  section and 54.5% in another, with no method and no company list. We publish no share for
  anyone. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **GMI's regional figures** ($19.9B North America and $13.4B Europe for 2025, 12.2% North
  America CAGR). They are splits of a total our arithmetic shows to be a different kind of
  measurement, from a single source with no visible method. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Country splits.** No named source gives any. `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **GMI's segment percentages** (consumables 69.4%, clinical research 53.8%, pharma end-use
  50%, advanced techniques $21.6B). One source, one page, no method. Segmentation goes out as
  plain strings.
- **Olink's, Nautilus's or Bruker's proteomics revenue.** Olink is unsplit inside Thermo
  Fisher's Life Sciences Solutions ($10.37B); Bruker's timsTOF line is unsplit inside BSI
  CALID ($1.21B); Nautilus is pre-commercial. Named, not sized.
- **An "addressable next-generation proteomics market" figure.** We publish the $340.5M
  disclosed sum as disclosed revenue, explicitly a floor, and do not convert it into a market
  size.
- **The SkyQuest and Notion-hosted series as anything but spread.** One was read from a
  third-party summary; the other has no identified publisher.
- **Total global proteomic sample or assay volume.** Nobody publishes it. Seer's 82 installed
  instruments is a disclosed count, but no vendor discloses pull-through per instrument, so it
  divides into nothing.
