## The spread

On a common 2026 base the published figures run from **$20.29B to $134.90B — a
6.65x spread.**

It is a scope spread, not a growth disagreement, and the four tiers are legible:

| Tier | 2026 value | What it counts |
|---|---|---|
| Machines | $20.29B | The haemodialysis machine as a capital good |
| Equipment | $30.01B | Machines plus dialysers, bloodlines, concentrates |
| Services | $83.97B | The clinic business — the treatment, not the hardware |
| Undifferentiated | $130.4-134.9B | Services plus products plus dialysis drugs |

The CAGRs, by contrast, agree closely: **4.60% to 8.30%, a 1.80x spread** —
narrow next to the 6.65x disagreement about the size of the thing being grown.
Read together, the publishers broadly concur that dialysis grows at mid-single
digits and disagree by a factor of six-and-a-half about what dialysis is. The
machines reading and the undifferentiated reading are not two estimates of one
market; they are two different markets, and only one of the two is ever named in
the headline.

## Grounding arithmetic

Dialysis is unusually well served by public registers: a near-complete census of
facilities and patients, a published per-treatment payment rate, and two listed
operators who together see a large minority of the world's patients. Four
independent divisions follow, and they reconcile.

**FX basis.** Fresenius Medical Care reports in euro. All euro figures below are
converted at the **ECB 2025 annual average reference rate of 1.1300 USD/EUR**
(Bank of Finland, from the ECB daily fixings). The rate is stated rather than
implied because the conversion moves the headline by over $2B.

### 1. The disclosed floor — what the three largest operators actually book

| Operator | FY2025 disclosure | USD |
|---|---|---|
| Fresenius Medical Care, total revenue | EUR 19,628M | **$22.180B** |
| — of which Care Delivery | EUR 13,736M | $15.522B |
| — of which Care Delivery U.S. | EUR 11,507M | $13.003B |
| — of which Care Delivery International | EUR 2,229M | $2.519B |
| — of which Care Enablement (products) | EUR 5,476M | $6.188B |
| DaVita Inc., total consolidated revenues | $13.643B | **$13.643B** |
| Vantive Health (ex-Baxter Kidney Care) | $4.5B (2023, last public) | **$4.5B** |

- FMC + DaVita = **$35.823B.**
- With Vantive's last-disclosed revenue, the three = **$40.323B.**

Against the published readings:

- $35.823B / $20.29B machines = **1.77x.** The two listed operators' revenue is
  nearly twice the entire published "dialysis machines" market. The machines
  figure is a component and cannot be quoted as the market.
- $40.323B vs $30.01B equipment = **1.34x**, same conclusion one tier up.
- $35.823B / $83.97B services = **42.7%**; the three together = **48.0%.**
- $40.323B / $134.90B undifferentiated = **29.9%.**

The 48.0% ratio is the one that passes. It leaves the other half of the services
market to every other provider on earth — US Renal Care, Satellite Healthcare,
Nipro's clinic network, and the hospital-based and public-system units that carry
most of China's, Japan's, India's and Europe's patients. That is a credible
remainder. The $130-135B readings require the non-FMC, non-DaVita, non-Vantive
world to supply **$90-95B, or 2.3x the three largest disclosed operators
combined** — a claim about what is counted, not about who treats patients.

### 2. Revenue per patient-year, cross-checked two ways

DaVita's 10-K for FY2025 states U.S. dialysis is approximately 86% of
consolidated revenue, across approximately 200,500 U.S. patients:

```
0.86 x $13,643M = $11,733M
$11,733M / 200,500 patients = $58,519 per patient-year
```

Fresenius, blended across all geographies, 291,902 patients at 31 Dec 2025:

```
$15,522M Care Delivery / 291,902 patients = $53,176 per patient-year
```

The two agree to within **10%**, despite FMC's figure being diluted by
lower-reimbursement international business. A dialysis operator books roughly
**$53,000-58,500 per patient per year.** That number is the lever for everything
below.

### 3. The Medicare bundle rate — an independent check on #2

CMS finalised the CY2026 ESRD PPS base rate at **$281.71 per treatment**
(up from $273.82 in CY2025; final rule issued 20 Nov 2025, published in the
Federal Register 24 Nov 2025). Standard in-centre haemodialysis is thrice
weekly:

```
$281.71 x 3 x 52 = $43,947 per patient-year at the Medicare bundle rate
```

Against DaVita's realised $58,519: a ratio of **1.33x**. That is precisely the
magnitude of the commercial-and-Medicare-Advantage uplift the operators describe
qualitatively and never quantify. Two figures derived from entirely separate
sources — a company's own revenue over its own patient count, and a published
regulatory payment rate times the clinical treatment frequency — land 33% apart
in the direction theory predicts. This is the strongest single corroboration in
the file.

### 4. The U.S. ceiling, and what Medicare actually pays of it

Registers, all countable:

- **516,837** patients on dialysis in the U.S. and territories
- **7,556** dialysis centres in the U.S. and territories
  (both: ESRD Networks national census, as of 31 March 2025)
- **831,192** people treated for ESRD at end-2023 and **273,041** living with a
  functioning kidney transplant at end-2024 (USRDS 2025 Annual Data Report) —
  transplant recipients are not dialysis patients, which is why the dialysis
  census is the right denominator and the ESRD total is not

Scaling DaVita's realised rate across the whole U.S. dialysis population gives a
ceiling for U.S. dialysis **services** revenue:

```
516,837 patients x $58,519 = $30.245B
```

At the Medicare bundle rate instead, the same population gives:

```
516,837 patients x $43,947 = $22.713B
```

So U.S. dialysis services is a **$22.7-30.2B** market in 2026 — and the U.S. is
the highest-reimbursed dialysis market in the world. Per centre:

```
$30,245M / 7,556 centres = $4.003M per centre per year
```

Cross-check against the one operator who discloses both numerator and
denominator: DaVita, $11,733M over 2,657 U.S. centres = **$4.416M per centre**.
The derived all-U.S. figure sits 9% below the figure for the largest operator's
own centres, which is the right direction — the census includes small
hospital-based and rural units. Patients per centre agrees too: 516,837/7,556 =
**68.4** across the census, against 200,500/2,657 = **75.5** at DaVita and
291,902/3,601 = **81.1** at Fresenius.

**And then the payer.** MedPAC reports that fee-for-service Medicare and its
beneficiaries spent **$7.6B on outpatient dialysis in 2024** (down from $8.1B in
2023), and CMS's CY2026 final rule projects approximately **$8.2B** of Medicare
payments to ESRD facilities in CY2026. Set that against the ceiling above:

- $7.6B / $30.245B U.S. services ceiling = **25.1%**
- $7.6B / $22.713B at the bundle rate = **33.5%**
- $7.6B / $83.97B published global services market = **9.1%**
- $7.6B / $134.90B undifferentiated reading = **5.6%**

The programme that created the modern dialysis industry, and still underwrites
it, accounts for a quarter of U.S. dialysis services revenue and **under a tenth
of the lowest credible global figure.** The rest is Medicare Advantage,
commercial payers, Medicaid, and the ex-U.S. world. Anyone citing a $130B
dialysis market alongside Medicare's ESRD programme as its driver is describing
two things sixteen times apart in size.

### What the divisions rule in and out

- **Ruled out:** quoting $20.29B or $30.01B as "the dialysis market". Two
  companies out-earn the first; three out-earn the second.
- **Ruled out:** the $130-135B readings as a services market. They are only
  coherent as services + products + dialysis drugs, and they are not labelled
  that way.
- **Ruled in:** $83.97B (2026) -> $118.79B (2032) for dialysis services. It
  survives the 48.0% disclosed-revenue ratio, and its implied U.S. share — if
  U.S. services is $30.2B, the U.S. is 36% of global services revenue on 13% of
  a roughly 4M global dialysis population — is consistent with U.S.
  reimbursement being multiples of the rest of the world's.
- **Ruled in, as a patient-count ratio only:** DaVita treats
  200,500 / 516,837 = **38.8%** of U.S. dialysis patients. Both terms are
  public registers. This is a share of *patients*, not of revenue, and it is not
  entered as a vendor market share anywhere in the JSON.

## What we publish

**Scope: dialysis services, global.** Base 2026 **$83.97B**, forecast 2032
**$118.79B**.

**CAGR: 5.95%**, not the 5.93% the publisher states. The publisher's own
endpoints imply (118.79/83.97)^(1/6) - 1 = 5.9523%; 5.93% is its rounding. The
0.02-point difference is stated here rather than silently absorbed, and 5.95% is
used so that the endpoints, the CAGR and the seven forecast points reconcile
exactly rather than to within a rounding error.

`market_forecast` interpolates 2026-2032 at 5.95% from exactly $83.97B. The
chart is labelled as modelled between published endpoints, because that is what
it is — the intermediate years are arithmetic, not observation.

## Do not publish

- **No vendor market share.** `competitive_landscape[].market_share` absent
  throughout. DaVita's 10-K was reported in one secondary summary as claiming
  "approximately 36% of the U.S. dialysis market"; a direct read of the filing
  did not surface that sentence, so the claim is treated as unverified and
  excluded. The 38.8% patient-count ratio above is a different quantity and is
  labelled as such wherever it appears.
- **`regional_analysis: []`.** No publisher gave a regional split of the services
  scope we publish. The $30.2B U.S. ceiling is our own derivation, not a
  published regional figure, and it belongs in prose where its derivation is
  visible — not in a regional table where it would read as sourced.
- **`country_analysis: []`.** Same reason.
- **No global dialysis patient count as a hard figure.** "Roughly 4 million" is
  widely repeated and is used above only to test an order of magnitude, never as
  a denominator for a published number. The U.S. census is exact; the global one
  is not, and the difference is not blurred.
- **No treatment-volume total.** DaVita disclosed 21,469,460 U.S. treatments for
  the nine months to 30 Sept 2025; the full-year figure was not found in its
  own releases, and annualising a nine-month count across a season the company
  itself attributes to flu mortality would manufacture precision.
- **No Vantive 2025 revenue.** It went private on 31 Jan 2025. $4.5B is its 2023
  figure and is labelled with that year every time it is used.
- **No segment percentages** for haemodialysis against peritoneal dialysis, or
  in-centre against home. Segmentation is published as plain strings.
- **No home-dialysis penetration rate.** The ETC Model makes this a live policy
  question and a tempting number; no source in this file gives it.
