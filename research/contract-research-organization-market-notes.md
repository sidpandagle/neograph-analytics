## The spread

On a common 2026 base the nine CRO readings run from **$48.50B to $99.87B — a
2.06x spread.** Dropping [9], which labels itself a pharma-only subset and is
therefore not a competing estimate of the same object, the eight undifferentiated
readings run **$61.20B to $99.87B, a 1.63x spread.**

The CAGRs run **6.5% to 9.0% — a 1.38x spread** (1.55x if [10]'s 10.1% is
admitted).

**This is the opposite of the usual pattern, and that is the finding.** In most
healthcare markets the published sizes disagree by several times while the growth
rates agree closely, which identifies a definition dispute. Here the size
disagreement (2.06x) is only 1.5x larger than the growth disagreement (1.38x).
The publishers broadly concur about what a CRO *is* — a vendor that sells
outsourced drug-development work — and are genuinely uncertain about how fast it
grows. Two consequences:

1. **The residual definition question is narrow and nameable.** It is whether
   discovery and pre-clinical work, central-laboratory work, consulting, and
   commercialisation services sit inside the market, and whether device,
   diagnostics and academic sponsors are counted. [9] against [5] — $48.50B
   against $99.87B — is the whole of that gap, and both publishers say in their
   own scope lines which of those they include.
2. **The forecast spread widens rather than closing.** Carrying each 2026 base
   forward six years at its own stated rate: [7] reaches $61.2B x 1.065^6 =
   **$89.3B** by 2032, while [5] reaches $99.87B x 1.09^6 = **$167.5B**. The
   2032 spread is **1.88x**, up from 1.63x in 2026. The disagreement about growth
   compounds, so the further out a CRO forecast runs, the less of it is an
   estimate of the market and the more of it is an estimate of the rate.

**The scope lever that actually moves the number is manufacturing, and one
publisher prices it.** Fortune Business Insights publishes both a CRO services
market [5] and a CRDMO market [11] on the same base year, from the same house,
updated the same day. Its CRO reading is $99.87B for 2026; its CRDMO reading,
which adds API manufacturing, finished dosage and packaging to the same CRO
definition, is $281.16B. **Contract manufacturing is therefore 2.815x the size of
contract research**, measured inside one publisher's own consistent accounting.
Any figure in the $250-550B range that is presented as a "CRO market" has had
manufacturing folded into it.

## Grounding arithmetic

The CRO sector is unusually well instrumented for a services market: eight of its
largest participants disclose a revenue line that is specifically contract
research, and the work itself is enumerated in a public register, study by study.
Three independent divisions follow. They agree about which published readings are
too small, and they disagree with every publisher about the growth rate.

**FX basis.** Tigermed reports in renminbi. Its figures are converted at
**7.1875 CNY/USD**, the arithmetic mean of the 250 daily noon buying rates the
Federal Reserve published for 2025 on its H.10 release [23]. The rate is stated
because the conversion is material to the floor by about $130M depending on
whether a year-start (7.2994) or year-end (6.9931) rate is used.

or results release. No line is an estimate, and no company's non-CRO revenue is

Four deliberate exclusions keep this a floor and not an estimate:

- **IQVIA's reportable-segment figure is larger.** Its FY2025 reportable segment
  "Research & Development Solutions" is $9,570M, against $8,896M on the
  operational R&DS view; the $674M difference is Contract Sales & Medical
  Solutions revenue reallocated into the R&D segment in 2025 [12]. The smaller

  accounts*, which is a useful calibration of how much precision the published
  market sizes can possibly carry.
- **Thermo Fisher's "Pharma services" line, $7,142M in 2025 [14], is excluded.**
  It is contract development and manufacturing, not contract research. Including
  it would almost double Thermo's contribution and would silently convert this
  floor into a CRDMO floor — the exact error diagnosed in The spread above.
- **Charles River's Research Models and Services ($846.1M) and Manufacturing
  Solutions ($766.4M) segments are excluded** [18]. Only DSA is contract
  research; note that DSA combines Discovery with Safety Assessment and Charles
  River does not break Safety Assessment out separately.
- **WuXi AppTec is excluded entirely.** It sold its China clinical CRO (WuXi
  Clinical) and SMO (WuXi MedKey) to Hillhouse, completing 16 December 2025, and
  now describes itself as a CRDMO [20]. The divested units' combined revenue was
  RMB 1.16B for the first three quarters of 2025, about 3.5% of group revenue.

Now add the largest CRO that no longer files. Syneos Health's last full year as a
public company was FY2022; its Clinical Solutions segment booked **$4,070.6M**,
and the company was taken private for $7.1B in September 2023 [21]. That figure
is three years stale and is labelled as such every time it is used. Eight filers
plus Syneos's last disclosed clinical revenue = **$40.838B**:

- $40.838B / $45.3B [9] = **90.2%**
- $40.838B / $57.5B [7] = **71.0%**
- $40.838B / $59.6B [8] = **68.5%**
- $40.838B / $74.51B [1] = **54.8%**
- $40.838B / $92.27B [5] = **44.3%**

**What this rules out.** The $45.3B pharma-CRO reading is exhausted by nine
companies at 90.2%, which is only coherent because it declares itself a
pharma-sponsor subset — it is not a total CRO market and must never be quoted as
one. The $57.5B and $59.6B readings leave **$16.7-18.8B** for every CRO on earth
that is not one of those nine. That remainder has to accommodate Parexel,
Novotech, PSI, Worldwide Clinical Trials, Premier Research, Caidya, Veeda, every
niche and regional CRO, every Chinese and Indian CRO other than Tigermed, and the
CRO revenue sitting undisclosed inside large diversified filers. It is tight to
the point of implausibility.

**What this rules in.** The $74.5-92.3B band leaves 45-55% of the market outside
the nine largest disclosed books. For a sector with a documented long tail of
specialist and regional providers, that is a credible remainder, and it is the
band the second and third divisions also support.

### 2. Revenue per trial, against the register — two denominators, one of which is wrong

ClinicalTrials.gov is queryable study by study. Queried via its API v2 on
17 October 2026 [22]:

```
total registered studies                                              607,349
interventional studies, all sponsors                                  463,466
studies with an industry lead sponsor, all types                      133,027
industry-lead interventional studies with a 2025 start date             5,904
industry-lead studies of any type with a 2025 start date                6,937
industry-lead interventional studies currently open or under way      16,499
  (recruiting + active-not-recruiting + enrolling-by-invitation + not-yet-recruiting)
industry-lead interventional, first posted during 2025                  5,947
```

**Per trial start** — the obvious division, and the wrong one:

```
$36,767.7M floor / 5,904 industry interventional starts = $6.228M per start
$81,130M [1] / 5,904 starts                             = $13.74M per start
$99,870M [5] / 5,904 starts                             = $16.92M per start
```

Set those against the only rigorously derived public cost figure for a trial:
Moore et al. estimated the **median cost of a pivotal trial supporting an FDA
novel-drug approval at $19.0M** (IQR $12.2-33.1M; n = 138 trials behind 59
agents approved 2015-2016) [24]. Pivotal trials are the expensive end: of the
5,904 industry interventional studies that started in 2025, only **1,012 are
Phase 3**, while **2,120 are Phase 1** and **1,602 are Phase 2** [22]. A blended
per-trial cost should therefore sit well *below* $19.0M. Yet [5]'s $16.92M of CRO
revenue per start would have the CRO alone capturing nearly the entire estimated
cost of a *median pivotal* trial on an overwhelmingly early-phase trial mix — and
CRO revenue is only part of a trial's cost, which also carries sponsor internal
staff, investigator grants, study drug and site overheads. **The per-start
denominator is wrong**, and it is wrong in an instructive way: trials run for
several years, so the studies generating revenue in any one year vastly outnumber
the studies that started in it.

**Per ongoing trial-year** — the right division:

```
$36,767.7M floor / 16,499 ongoing industry interventional studies = $2.228M per trial-year
$61,200M [7]     / 16,499                                          = $3.709M per trial-year
$81,130M [1]     / 16,499                                          = $4.917M per trial-year
$99,870M [5]     / 16,499                                          = $6.053M per trial-year
```

Over a three-year trial, [1]'s figure implies **$14.75M of CRO revenue across a
trial's life**, which is **77.6% of the $19.0M median pivotal estimate** — below
it, as it must be, and of the right order for a sponsor that outsources most of
the execution. [5]'s figure gives $18.16M, or 95.6% of the median pivotal
estimate, which leaves essentially nothing for sponsor-side cost on a trial mix
dominated by Phase 1. [7]'s gives $11.13M, or 58.6%, which is comfortable but
requires the eight filers plus Syneos to be 71% of the whole market.

This division therefore does what the first one did, from entirely separate data:
it rules the low readings tight and the highest reading implausible, and lands on
the **$74-93B** band. It is a plausibility test on magnitude, not a market
estimate; the Moore figure is itself modelled from contract-research costing
software rather than read off sponsors' books [24], and it is a 2015-2016
vintage.

### 3. The growth rate, against what the sector's own books and the register did

This is the division that disagrees with every publisher in the table.

**What the disclosed books grew.** Each FY2025 line against the same company's
FY2024 line, with Tigermed held at constant FX so the comparison is not a
currency artefact:

| Company | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| IQVIA R&D Solutions | 8,529.2 | 8,896.0 | **+4.3%** |
| ICON plc (2024 as restated) | 8,189.0 | 8,251.3 | **+0.8%** |
| Thermo Fisher, Clinical research | 7,836.0 | 7,915.0 | **+1.0%** |
| Labcorp Biopharma Laboratory Services | 2,922.6 | 3,098.2 | **+6.0%** |
| Fortrea | 2,696.4 | 2,723.4 | **+1.0%** |
| Medpace | 2,109.1 | 2,530.2 | **+20.0%** |
| Charles River DSA | 2,451.3 | 2,402.9 | **-2.0%** |
| Tigermed (constant FX) | 918.7 | 950.7 | **+3.5%** |
| **Aggregate** | **35,652.3** | **36,767.7** | **+3.13%** |

The eight largest disclosed CRO books grew **3.13%** in 2025. Every forecast in
the table is between **2.1x and 2.9x** that rate. One company, Medpace, supplied
$421M of the aggregate's $1,115M of growth — **37.8% of it from 6.9% of the
base** — and without Medpace the remaining seven grew **1.99%**.

**What the register did.** Industry-lead interventional study starts by year [22]:

```
2021  6,399      2022  6,044      2023  5,726      2024  5,600      2025  5,904
```

That is a **-1.99% CAGR over 2021-2025**, a **-12.5% fall from the 2021 peak to
the 2024 trough**, and a partial 2025 recovery of +5.4% off that trough. The
count of industry trials being started has not grown for five years.

**What the two together mean.** A 6.5-9.0% revenue CAGR on a flat-to-declining
trial count is only achievable from price, trial complexity, scope per study and
outsourcing penetration — not from volume. That may well happen; it is the
sector's own stated thesis and the booking data is consistent with demand
recovering (IQVIA ended 2025 with $32.7B of contracted backlog, up 5.3%, and a
1.18x fourth-quarter book-to-bill [12]; ICON booked $9,033M net for a 1.09
book-to-bill [13]; Medpace held $3,027.2M of backlog [17]). But it is a claim
about pricing and share-of-wallet, not a claim about more medicine being tested,
and **no publisher in the table says so.** The forecast is a bet on revenue per
trial, and the register says so plainly.

### What the divisions rule in and out

- **Ruled out:** quoting $45.3B [9] as "the CRO market". Nine companies' disclosed
  revenue is 90.2% of it. It is a declared pharma-sponsor subset and is coherent
  only as that.
- **Ruled out, or very nearly:** $57.5B [7] and $59.6B [8] as totals. They leave
  $16.7-18.8B for the entire rest of the sector including Parexel, Novotech, PSI
  and Worldwide Clinical Trials.
- **Ruled out:** $99.87B [5] read together with the register. At 16,499 ongoing
  industry trials it implies CRO revenue equal to 95.6% of the median pivotal
  trial's whole estimated cost over a three-year life, on a trial mix in which
  Phase 1 outnumbers Phase 3 by better than two to one.
- **Ruled out:** any figure above roughly $250B described as a CRO market. One
  publisher's own paired reports price the difference: contract manufacturing is
  2.815x contract research on the same 2026 base [5] vs [11].
- **Ruled in:** **$81.13B (2026) -> $135.33B (2032)** [1]. It sits mid-band among
  nine readings, it leaves 49.3% of the market outside the eight disclosed books
  and 45.2% outside nine, and its per-ongoing-trial-year figure of $4.917M lands
  at 77.6% of the median pivotal cost estimate over a three-year trial — below
  it, where it must be.
- **Ruled in as the chief caveat, not as a figure:** the 8.90% rate is 2.8x the
  3.13% the sector's own largest books delivered in the base year, on a trial
  count that has fallen 12.5% from its 2021 peak. This belongs in
  `forecast_analysis`, prominently, and it does.

## What we publish

**Scope: the global contract research organization market, as defined in [1] —
outsourced clinical development, regulatory affairs, data management, safety,
laboratory and commercialisation services sold to pharmaceutical, biotechnology
and medical-device sponsors.** It excludes contract manufacturing.

Base 2026 **$81.13B**, forecast 2032 **$135.33B**.

**CAGR: 8.90%**, not the 8.89% [1] states. Its own endpoints imply
(135.33/81.13)^(1/6) - 1 = 8.9024%; 8.89% is its rounding. The 0.01-point
difference is stated here rather than silently absorbed, and 8.90% is published
so that the endpoints, the headline rate and the seven forecast points reconcile
exactly rather than to within a rounding error.

[1] is chosen over the eight alternatives for three reasons: it is the only
reading that states a 2026 base and a 2032 endpoint for the full CRO scope
without interpolation; it sits mid-band rather than at either extreme; and it is
the reading that survives all three grounding divisions.

`market_forecast` interpolates 2026-2032 at 8.90% from exactly $81.13B. The chart
is labelled as modelled between published endpoints, because the intermediate
years are arithmetic, not observation.

## Do not publish

- **No vendor market share.** `competitive_landscape[].market_share` absent
  throughout. Three of the ten largest CROs are private (Parexel, Syneos,
  Novotech), one more left the clinical business mid-2025 (WuXi AppTec), and the
  largest disclosed book — IQVIA's — differs by $674M depending on which of its
  own two segment views is used. Share is not publicly computable here, and no
  publisher's share claim was found that traced to a disclosure.
- **`regional_analysis: []`.** Four publishers do give named North America
  splits, and **they do not agree**: Mordor 38.92% (2025) [3], GVR Healthcare CRO
  40.2% (2025) [8], Global Market Insights $24.84B (2025, = 43.2% of its $57.5B)
  [7] and Fortune 50.08% / $46.21B (2025) [5]. The two absolute figures differ by
  **1.86x for the same region in the same year**. They also attach to four
  different scopes, none of which is the scope published here. Entering any one
  of them in a regional table would render as sourced when what it actually is,
  is one of four irreconcilable claims. The disagreement is reported in prose,
  where it can be seen for what it is.
- **`country_analysis: []`.** Only one country figure was found at all —
  Fortune's "the U.S. is expected to account for approximately 20.4% of global
  sales in 2026" [5], which cannot be reconciled with the same publisher's own
  50.08% North America share and is therefore not usable.
- **No Precision Business Insights figure in the spread** [10]. Its page carries
  no publication date, no base-year value and no base year; the only route to a
  2026 comparison is the back-cast shown above, which is our arithmetic on a
  stale page, not a published estimate.
- **No Research and Markets headline figures from the [2] press release.** That
  release's headline states $107.03B for 2025 growing to $180.56B by 2030, while
  its own body states $87.43B for 2026 growing to $133.54B by 2032 at 6.94%. The
  two sets are irreconcilable — the headline's 2025 figure exceeds the body's
  2026 figure — and the release does not explain the difference. The body figures
  are recorded; the headline figures are not used anywhere.
- **No Market Research Future figure beyond the one tabled** [6]. The same page
  gives $81.1B / $171.8B / 7.8% in its front matter and scope table and
  $89.88B / $170.63B / 6.62% in its market summary. The front-matter set is
  tabled because it is the set the scope table also carries; the conflict is
  noted and no value from that page is relied on in any division.
- **No Grand View pharmaceutical-CRO 2026 value to two decimals** [9]. Its own
  page gives $48.5B in the body and $48.4B in the Report Scope table. $48.5B is
  used and the 0.1 discrepancy is noted.
- **No global pharmaceutical R&D spend denominator.** An outsourcing-penetration
  ratio would have been a fourth division, but no hard register for total global
  biopharma R&D was found: the available figures (roughly $288B for 2024, roughly
  $300B for 2025) are themselves analyst estimates, and dividing one estimate by
  another is not a grounding check. Dropped rather than published.
- **No full-year 2025 WuXi Clinical revenue.** Only RMB 1.16B for the first three
  quarters of 2025 is disclosed [20], and annualising across the quarter in which
  the business was sold would manufacture precision.
- **No Syneos 2025 revenue.** It has not filed since FY2022. Its Clinical
  Solutions figure of $4,070.6M is labelled FY2022 every time it appears, and the
  divisions are reported both with and without it.
- **No Parexel or Novotech revenue.** Neither discloses. They are named in the
  competitive section in words, with no figure attached.
- **No segment percentages.** Mordor publishes clinical research services at
  61.45% of its market, oncology at 21.43%, pharma/biopharma end users at 55.34%
  and full-service CROs at 62.16%, all for 2025 [3]. Every one attaches to
  Mordor's $92.98B scope rather than ours, and none was corroborated by a second
  source. Segmentation is published as plain strings.
- **No outsourcing-penetration rate.** Widely quoted at "over 50% of development
  spend" and nowhere sourced in this file.
- **No claim that trial starts predict CRO revenue.** Division 3 establishes that
  the register's volume is flat and the forecast rate is not, which is a question
  the forecast must answer. It does not establish a coefficient between the two,
  and none is published.
