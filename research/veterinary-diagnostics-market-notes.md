## The spread

**At the 2025/2026 base, $3.68B (MarketsandMarkets, 2025) to $9.72B (Maximize, 2025) — a 2.64x spread.** At the far end, $5.36B (2030) to $19.63B (2032).

Published CAGRs run **7.8% to 10.56%, a 1.35x spread.** As in most of this corpus, the growth rates nearly agree and the base years do not. The disagreement is a scope decision.

The scope decision is identifiable, and for once two of the publishers state their boundaries clearly enough to name it:

- **Mordor** includes "software and services" as a product type and **reference laboratories** as an end user, and states that reference laboratories are 45% of its 2025 market. It excludes imaging.
- **Maximize** includes "analytical services" and **diagnostic imaging**, and puts laboratories at "nearly 49%".
- **Coherent** includes software and services **and** imaging — the widest of the four.
- **MarketsandMarkets** segments only by **technology** and by **product (consumables and instruments)**. There is no services line and no reference-laboratory end user anywhere in its published segmentation.

So the two readings are: **the whole testing business, including the reference laboratory that runs the sample**, versus **the devices and consumables sold into a practice**. If laboratory services are roughly 45–49% of the whole, then stripping them out of a $9.5B market leaves about $5B of products — still well above MarketsandMarkets' $3.68B, but the right order. The $3.68B series is a products-and-instruments reading, and it is the only one of the four whose published segmentation contains no services at all.

The arithmetic below settles which reading can be the market, because **one company discloses enough revenue to break the narrow one outright.**

## Grounding arithmetic

This is the best-instrumented market in the batch. One of the two largest suppliers is a US-listed pure-play that discloses its diagnostics revenue outright, separates recurring from instrument revenue, and splits it by geography. A second major supplier was listed until 2023 and its last full year is on the record. The industry's largest single buyer tier — US veterinary practices — has its total spending published annually by a trade association.

**Registers and disclosures used:** IDEXX Laboratories' Q4 and full-year 2025 results (8-K exhibit 99.1, filed with the SEC, 2 February 2026); Zoetis' Q4 and full-year 2025 results (11 February 2026) and 2025 Form 10-K; Heska Corporation's last disclosed full year before acquisition; the American Pet Products Association's 2026 State of the Industry figures for 2025; and the AVMA's reported US pet populations.

### Test 1 — one company against each published market

```
IDEXX CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue, FY2025                      $3,407M

÷ $8,730M  (Mordor, 2025, broad incl. reference labs)        =  39.0%
÷ $9,720M  (Maximize, 2025, broad incl. imaging)             =  35.1%
÷ $3,680M  (MarketsandMarkets, 2025, products/instruments)   =  92.6%
```

**One company's diagnostics revenue line is 92.6% of MarketsandMarkets' entire global veterinary diagnostics market.** That alone is close to disqualifying. The next line removes the doubt:

```
IDEXX total company revenue, FY2025                                  $4,304M
÷ $3,680M  (MarketsandMarkets, whole global market, 2025)    =  1.17x
```

**IDEXX's total revenue exceeds MarketsandMarkets' entire published global market by 17%** — and IDEXX is not the whole industry. Mars' Science and Diagnostics division (Antech plus Heska) is of comparable scale in reference laboratory work and discloses nothing; Zoetis grew its diagnostics line 13% in 2025 on a base it does not publish in dollars; and Virbac, Neogen, Thermo Fisher's animal health business, bioMérieux, Fujifilm Sonosite and a long tail of regional laboratories all sell into this market.

The narrow series is therefore not usable as a market size. It may be internally consistent as a measure of *instruments and consumables excluding services* — IDEXX's own non-recurring instrument revenue is a small fraction of its diagnostics total, so a products-only cut of IDEXX would be far smaller than $3.4B — but the page does not say that, and a reader who takes $3.68B as "the veterinary diagnostics market" is out by a factor of roughly 2.6 against the broad houses.

### Test 2 — sum what is disclosed against the broad reading

Only two of the industry's four largest diagnostics businesses have ever disclosed a diagnostics revenue figure.

| Company | Disclosure | Diagnostics revenue | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IDEXX Laboratories** | FY2025 results, 2 Feb 2026 | **$3,407M** (CAG Diagnostics recurring) | Disclosed outright |
| **Heska Corporation** | FY2022, its last full year as a listed company | **$257.3M** (total revenue, 2021: $253.7M) | Acquired by Mars for ~$1.3B, closed 13 June 2023; now inside Mars Science and Diagnostics. **No disclosure since** |
| **Antech Diagnostics (Mars)** | — | **Not disclosed** | Private. Mars does not publish divisional revenue |
| **Zoetis** | FY2025 results and 10-K | **Not disclosed in dollars.** Diagnostics reported as **+13% operational revenue growth** for 2025, with US companion animal diagnostics +14% and international +11% | Zoetis' published tables break out Companion Animal ($6,587M), Livestock ($2,764M) and Contract Manufacturing & Human Health ($116M) against total revenue of $9,467M — **not diagnostics** |

```
$3,407M (IDEXX) + $257M (Heska's last disclosed year, 2022)  = $3,664M
$3,664M ÷ $8,730M  (Mordor, 2025)   = 42.0%
$3,664M ÷ $9,720M  (Maximize, 2025) = 37.7%
```

**Two companies — one of them measured four years stale — are between 38% and 42% of the broad published market.** That is a plausible concentration for a two-firm industry and it is the right answer to the question the brief poses: *if one company is most of a market, say so with both numbers.* IDEXX alone is 39.0% of the broad reading and 92.6% of the narrow one. Against the broad reading, IDEXX is the largest single supplier of a concentrated market. Against the narrow reading, IDEXX **is** the market, which cannot be true.

*Caveat stated plainly:* IDEXX's CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue includes reference laboratory services, consumables, rapid assay kits and the recurring element of its instrument business — it is wider than "diagnostic products" and narrower than IDEXX's total company revenue, which also carries water testing, livestock testing, veterinary software and imaging hardware. Heska's $257.3M is its whole 2022 company revenue, not a 2025 diagnostics figure, and is used only as a floor on what Mars absorbed. Dividing one company's revenue by a third party's market total compares differently constructed numbers.

### Test 3 — against the money US practices actually take in

The American Pet Products Association's 2026 State of the Industry figures give the 2025 US actuals:

| US pet industry, 2025 (APPA) | Value | Share of total |
|---|---|---|
| Total pet industry expenditure | **$158.0B** (+3.7%) | — |
| Food and treats | $68.3B | 43.2% |
| **Veterinary care and product sales** | **$41.0B** (+3% on 2024) | **25.9%** |
| Supplies, live animals, OTC medicine | $34.4B | — |
| Other services (grooming, insurance, boarding) | $14.3B | — |
| 2026 projection, total | $165B | — |

Now the only geographic figure in this whole exercise that comes from a primary disclosure rather than an apportionment:

```
IDEXX, US CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue, FY2025                  $2,215M

$2,215M ÷ $41,000M (APPA, US veterinary care and product sales, 2025) = 5.4%
```

**One supplier takes 5.4 cents of every dollar US pet owners spend on veterinary care.** Diagnostics — in-house chemistry and haematology, rapid assays, and send-out reference laboratory work — is conventionally a high-single-digit to low-double-digit share of a companion animal practice's gross revenue. If diagnostics is of the order of a tenth of US veterinary spend, the US diagnostics market is roughly **$4.1B**, which on its own exceeds MarketsandMarkets' **$3.68B global** market by 11%. Pushed the other way, MarketsandMarkets' global figure would require US diagnostics to be under 9% of a $41B spend *worldwide*, with the rest of the world contributing almost nothing — and IDEXX's own international CAG growth of 12% organic in Q4 2025 says otherwise.

And the final line, which is the one to remember:

```
IDEXX's UNITED STATES diagnostics recurring revenue alone  $2,215M
÷ MarketsandMarkets' entire GLOBAL market, 2025            $3,680M   = 60.2%
```

### Test 4 — per pet, as a scale check

The AVMA's 2025 Sourcebook figures, as reported by the pet trade press: **87.3 million dogs** and **76.3 million cats** in the United States in 2025 — **163.6 million companion animals** — in **77.5 million pet-owning households**, 58.6% of all US households. (**All read from trade-press summaries of the AVMA Sourcebook, which is a paid publication we did not buy — flagged.**)

```
$2,215M (IDEXX US diagnostics recurring) ÷ 163.6M dogs and cats = $13.54 per animal per year
```

$13.54 per dog or cat per year, from one supplier, against an AVMA-reported average of roughly $1,700 of annual pet spending per owner of which veterinary care is a third. That is a small, sane number — which is the point of the test. It confirms that the broad market readings describe a real recurring spend of a few tens of dollars per animal per year, and it confirms that nothing in the arithmetic above depends on an implausible per-unit price.

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope and plausibility tests. They establish that one company's disclosed diagnostics revenue is 92.6% of one published global market and 39.0% of another, that the same company's total revenue exceeds the smaller published market outright, that its US-only diagnostics revenue is 60% of that market, and that 5.4% of all US veterinary spending passes through that one supplier's diagnostics line. They do not establish the correct market size for either scope. Heska's figure is four years stale; Antech's is not published at all; Zoetis' diagnostics base is not published in dollars and only its growth rate is on the record; APPA's $41.0B is US veterinary care *and product sales*, which is wider than veterinary services; the AVMA pet populations were read from trade-press summaries rather than the Sourcebook; and the "diagnostics is about a tenth of practice revenue" step in Test 3 is a stated industry convention, not a measured figure, and is labelled as such wherever it appears.

## What we publish

**Headline: $9.53B (2026) → $16.49B (2032) at 9.57%.**

Source: Mordor Intelligence, *Veterinary Diagnostics Market*, data stated current as of January 2026, page last updated 11 September 2026, which publishes **$8.73B for 2025, $9.53B for 2026, $15.05B for 2031 and a 9.57% CAGR for 2026–2031.**

The 2026 base is **Mordor's own published 2026 figure**, not a derivation. The 2032 endpoint is **derived by continuing Mordor's own stated rate one year past its own horizon**: 9.53 × 1.0957^6 = 16.49. Internal check: the implied rate between our endpoints is 9.57%, identical to Mordor's stated rate, and the interpolated 2031 point lands on **15.05**, which is Mordor's published 2031 value to the cent. This is the only series of the four whose published base, mid-points and rate reconcile exactly.

Chosen because: (a) it is the **only one of the four with a fully stated scope** — it names what is in (instruments, kits and reagents, software and services; hospitals, clinics, reference laboratories, mobile practices, research institutes) and what is out (medicines, vaccines, treatment, clinic supplies, imaging), which is the single thing a reader of this category most needs; (b) it publishes **both** a base and a mid-horizon value, so its trajectory is checkable rather than asserted; (c) it was read **directly from the publisher's own page**, where the Coherent and Maximize figures came through a distributor listing and a newswire reproduction; and (d) its $8.73B for 2025 makes IDEXX 39.0% of the market, a concentration consistent with a two-firm industry, where the narrow reading makes IDEXX 92.6% of it, which is not possible.

Coherent's $9.68B for 2026 is within 1.6% of Mordor's $9.53B despite including imaging, which Mordor excludes. Two independent houses landing that close on the broad definition is the strongest corroboration available in this market, and the report says so.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Life Sciences.

The report states plainly in `executive_summary`, `market_context`, `forecast_analysis` and `research_methodology` that IDEXX's disclosed CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue of $3.407 billion is 39.0% of the published market we adopt and 92.6% of the narrow one; that IDEXX's total revenue of $4.304 billion exceeds the narrow published market outright; that IDEXX's US-only diagnostics revenue of $2.215 billion is 5.4% of all US veterinary care and product spending; and that Antech, Heska and Zoetis' diagnostics lines are not disclosed, so no share can be computed for the rest of the industry.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** The 39.0% and 92.6% ratios are **our own division** of one company's disclosed revenue line by third parties' published totals, and are stated in prose with both inputs named and both constructions described. They are not IDEXX's market share: IDEXX has never published one, and no filing contains one. One aggregator page asserts "100,000+ veterinary clinics" and "~70% market share" for IDEXX with no sourcing; it is unusable and is recorded here only so that nobody repeats it. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **Mordor's "reference laboratories 45% of 2025" and Maximize's "laboratories nearly 49%".** Two houses, two different numbers for overlapping definitions of the same segment, neither with a method. They are quoted in the notes and in the report's prose as the evidence that the services tier is roughly half the market — which is the scope finding — and are **not** reproduced as segment shares. `market_segmentation` goes out as plain strings.
- **Regional splits.** Maximize describes North America as leading and APAC as emerging; no publisher in the set gives a numeric regional share we could read from a primary page. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Country splits.** None published. `country_analysis` stays `[]`. The only country-level figure we have is IDEXX's own US revenue, which is a company disclosure, not a market split, and is used as such.
- **A US veterinary diagnostics market size.** Our ~$4.1B figure in Test 3 rests on the convention that diagnostics is about a tenth of practice revenue. That convention is not a published measurement, so the figure is a labelled plausibility step inside the notes and is never published as a market size.
- **A Zoetis diagnostics revenue figure.** Zoetis disclosed only growth rates for 2025 (+13% operational; US companion animal +14%, international +11%). Its published revenue tables stop at Companion Animal, Livestock and Contract Manufacturing & Human Health. Its 10-K does carry a revenue-by-product-category table, but we could not reach it: the filing runs to roughly 300,000 characters and the table did not appear in the three passes we were able to read. **Flagged, and nothing is inferred.** The +13% growth rate was read from a transcript aggregator's account of the earnings call rather than from Zoetis' own transcript — **flagged**.
- **An Antech or Mars Science and Diagnostics revenue figure.** Mars is private and publishes no divisional revenue. We searched and found none. Antech is named as a major supplier and explicitly not sized.
- **Heska's current revenue.** $257.3M is its **2022** total company revenue, its last as a listed company, used strictly as a floor on what Mars absorbed in June 2023. It is not a 2025 diagnostics figure.
- **The $1.3B Heska acquisition value as a market signal.** Sources differ ($1.2B and $1.3B, with premium measured against two different baselines). The deal is reported as an event; no multiple or implied market size is derived from it.
- **MarketsandMarkets' $3.68B as the veterinary diagnostics market.** It appears in the estimates table, and the report describes it as a products-and-instruments reading that excludes laboratory services, which is what Test 1 shows it must be. It is not used for the headline, and the report says why in terms a reader can check.
- **MarketsandMarkets' US 9.1% CAGR.** No base value was read alongside it. Rate recorded in the notes only.
- **The AVMA pet populations as primary figures.** 87.3M dogs, 76.3M cats, 77.5M households at 58.6% of all households were read from trade-press summaries of the AVMA 2025 Sourcebook, a paid publication we did not purchase. Used for the per-animal scale check only, labelled.
- **A US veterinary practice count.** Figures in circulation include ~34,296 "veterinary practices" attributed to the US Census Bureau for 2023 via a market-research summary, ~34,000 "establishments" attributed to Census via the AVMA for 2022, and "roughly 30% corporate ownership". An establishment count is not a clinic count, the vintages differ, and all of it came through third parties. **No per-practice figure is published**; the per-animal figure in Test 4 is used instead, because the denominator there is at least a count of the thing being tested.
- **A veterinarian headcount.** Two aggregators give 133,475 (AVMA registry, including retired and non-practising) and ~127,000 (practising). Different scopes, both third-hand. Not used.
