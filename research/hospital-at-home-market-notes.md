## The spread

- **Base-year values for 2025: $5.62B (Global Reports Store, technology only) to
  $37.18B (The Insight Partners) — a 6.62x spread.** Excluding the explicitly
  narrower technology sub-scope, $8.0B to $37.18B is still **4.65x**.
- **Published CAGRs: 7.8% to 18.10% — a 2.32x spread.**

This is the batch's only market where the houses **disagree about growth as
sharply as they disagree about size.** The Insight Partners has the largest base
and the slowest growth; Astute Analytica has the smallest comparable base and
one of the fastest. Those are not two views of one market; they are a mature
services market and an emerging technology market wearing the same name.

The scope decision is identifiable, and for once one house states it in the
negative. Astute Analytica explicitly **excludes routine home health, hospice
and standard outpatient telehealth**. Nobody else excludes anything. The
$27–37 billion figures are only reachable by letting post-acute care, chronic
disease management at home and remote patient monitoring inside the boundary —
and Meticulous's own segmentation does exactly that, listing "post-acute care"
and "chronic care" as service types alongside acute care.

## Grounding arithmetic

Hospital-at-home has the best denominator in this batch, because the United
States runs the programme as an explicit waiver and counts both the sites and
the patients.

**Register — the CMS Acute Hospital Care at Home (AHCAH) initiative.**

| Measure | Value | As of |
|---|---|---|
| Hospitals/programmes approved | **373** across **140** health systems in **37** states | January 2026 (Chartis) |
| Hospitals/programmes approved | **366** across **139** health systems in **37** states | March 2026 (AMA) |
| Hospitals that have **participated** | **366** | October 2024 (CMS blog, 17 December 2024) |
| Patients served in home settings | **over 31,000** | October 2024 (same CMS blog) |
| CMS study population | **332** participating hospitals across **38** states, November 2020 – July 2024 | CMS Report on the Study of the AHCAH Initiative, 30 September 2024 |

**Episode-value denominator.** CMS national health expenditures put hospital care
at **$1,634.7 billion** in 2024; the AHA Annual Survey for the same year records
**35,658,583** admissions.

```
$1,634.7B / 35,658,583 admissions = $45,843 per admission
```

That is an **upper bound** on the average revenue of an inpatient episode,
because NHE hospital care spending also includes hospital outpatient activity
while the denominator counts only admissions. The true per-admission figure is
lower, which makes every test below more severe, not less.

### Test 1 — how many acute-at-home episodes each market size implies

```
$9,270,000,000 (Astute Analytica, advanced to 2026) / $45,843 = 202,211 episodes/year
$27,300,000,000 (Meticulous Research, 2026)        / $45,843 = 595,509 episodes/year
$37,180,000,000 (The Insight Partners, 2025)       / $45,843 = 811,027 episodes/year
```

The entire US AHCAH programme served **just over 31,000 patients across four
years** (November 2020 to October 2024). Even the smallest comparable published
figure requires **202,000 acute-at-home episodes worldwide every year — about
6.5 times the whole cumulative US programme experience, annually.** The largest
requires 811,000 a year, roughly **26 times** the cumulative US total.

Programmes outside the United States are real and in places older — Johns
Hopkins adopted the model in the mid-1990s, and Spain, Catalonia, Australia and
the UK run established services — so a global figure above the US count is
expected. A figure twenty-six times the entire US four-year experience, every
year, is not.

### Test 2 — revenue per approved programme

```
$9,270M (Astute, 2026)             / 373 approved programmes = $24,852,547
$27,300M (Meticulous, 2026)        / 373 approved programmes = $73,190,349
$37,180M (Insight Partners, 2025)  / 373 approved programmes = $99,678,284
```

These place global numerators over the US-only approved-programme count, so
again they are **upper bounds**. For scale: Mass General Brigham runs one of the
largest programmes in the country and treats **50 to 60 patients a day** across
five hospitals, having treated **over 7,000 patients since January 2022**; an
earlier snapshot recorded roughly **1,000 admissions in a year** at a capacity of
**33 concurrent patients**, with a plan to reach 45. At $45,843 per episode, a
1,000-admission programme bills about **$46 million a year**. A $99.7 million
average across all 373 approved programmes would mean every approved programme
in the country is more than twice the size of one of the largest.

### What the tests establish

Only the narrowest published scope — the one that says in writing that it
excludes routine home health, hospice and outpatient telehealth — produces
per-programme and per-episode numbers that the countable register can support.
The larger figures are coherent only as home-care markets with a
hospital-at-home label.

**These are plausibility tests, not market estimates.**

## The restraint every forecast glosses over — and its 2026 resolution

The brief for this report assumed the AHCAH waiver was approaching statutory
expiry. **It is not, as of October 2026, and that is a material correction.**

- The waiver was created by CMS in **November 2020**.
- It was extended by the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023 and ran under a
  series of short-term extensions tied to government-funding deadlines. A March
  2025 extension carried it only to **30 September 2025**, and the waivers
  **lapsed** during the subsequent partial government shutdown.
- **Section 6210 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026** extended the
  AHCAH waivers and flexibilities to **30 September 2030**. The spending package
  passed on **3 February 2026** and was signed into law in early February 2026
  (sources differ between 3 and 4 February). CMS confirmed the extension in a
  fact sheet dated **17 March 2026**.
- The extension **decoupled** the programme's statutory authority from
  government-funding deadlines, ending the cycle of short-term extensions.

So the restraint is no longer imminent expiry. It is that **this programme has
already lapsed once**, within the last eighteen months, for reasons that had
nothing to do with its clinical performance — and that its authority is a
five-year statutory window rather than a permanent benefit category. Every
forecast in the table above runs past 30 September 2030. None of them discloses
what happens on 1 October 2030. A health system building capital capacity on a
seventeen-percent compound forecast is building it on a waiver with an end date
inside the forecast period.

CMS's own September 2024 study found lower mortality and lower post-discharge
spending for at-home acute care than for traditional inpatient care, with
positive patient and caregiver feedback — which is the argument for permanence,
not evidence of it.

## What we publish

**Headline:** $9.27B (2026) → $22.47B (2032) at 15.9%.

Source: Astute Analytica, which publishes $8.0B for 2025 and $35B for 2035 at a
stated 15.9% CAGR over 2026–2035. Those endpoints are internally consistent
($8.0B → $35B over ten years implies 15.91%). The 2026 base is the published
2025 value advanced one year at that rate ($8.0B × 1.159 = $9.27B) and the 2032
endpoint continues it ($8.0B × 1.159⁷ = $22.47B). Both are interpolation on a
published rate, not separately published observations, and are labelled as such.

Chosen because it is the **only** series in the set that defines its boundary by
saying what it excludes, and the only one whose implied episode counts and
per-programme revenues the CMS register can support. It is also the low end of
the comparable range, and the report must say so plainly: readers who have been
quoted $27–37 billion have been quoted a home-care market.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Healthcare IT.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Medically Home, Biofourmis/Careology, DispatchHealth,
  Contessa (Amedisys), Inbound Health and SENA Health are private or embedded in
  larger parents with no at-home segment disclosure. No numerator exists.
  `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted.
- **Regional splits.** No named source gives a defensible dollar split for this
  scope. We have a complete US waiver register and no comparable register
  anywhere else, which is enough to test a global figure and not enough to
  apportion one. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Country splits.** `country_analysis` stays `[]`. A US-only market size is
  especially tempting here and especially unavailable: no named source publishes
  one on this scope, and we will not derive one by apportioning a global figure.
- **The $45,843 per-admission figure as a hospital-at-home episode price.** It is
  an upper bound on an average *inpatient* episode derived from NHE hospital care
  spending over AHA admissions, and NHE hospital care includes outpatient
  activity. It is a denominator for a plausibility test, nothing else.
- **The 31,000-patient figure as a current programme volume.** It is cumulative
  from November 2020 to October 2024 and is the most recent CMS figure we could
  find. CMS has not, as far as we could establish, published a comparable
  cumulative count since.
- **The Coherent Market Insights $435.23B home healthcare series or the PA
  Consulting $390.4B "hospital to home" figure.** Different categories entirely;
  recorded only to prevent confusion.
- **GIR's component apportionments** (equipment and devices 64.55% of 2025).
  One house's unaudited split. `market_segmentation` carries plain strings.
- **Any claim that the 373-versus-366 discrepancy between the Chartis and AMA
  counts shows the programme shrinking.** They are different retrieval dates and
  very likely different counting rules (hospitals versus programmes). Both are
  reported; neither is a trend.
- **Mass General Brigham's census as a national average.** It is one of the
  largest programmes in the country and is cited as an upper reference point,
  not a typical one.
