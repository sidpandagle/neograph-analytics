# Ambulatory surgery centers — research notes
**Slug:** ambulatory-surgery-centers-market   **Drafted:** 2026-10-17

An ambulatory surgery centre is a licensed facility that performs same-day
surgery on patients who do not stay overnight. It bills a **facility fee**; the
surgeon bills separately under the physician fee schedule, and the equipment
makers sell into the building. Those are three different revenue pools, and the
published estimates for "the ambulatory surgery centers market" silently pick
different ones. Separating them is the whole exercise.

## The spread

On a common 2026 base the published global figures run from **$5.34B to
$165.61B — a 31.0x spread.** Drop the single order-of-magnitude outlier (Kings
Research, dealt with below) and the credible band is **$47.68B to $165.61B, a
3.47x spread.**

The CAGRs are **4.4% to 9.80% — a 2.23x spread**, taking Research and Markets at
the rate its own endpoints imply rather than the 9.67% it states.

This is the first place this market differs from most. In dialysis the sizes
disagreed 6.65x while the growth rates agreed to within 1.8x: a pure definition
problem. Here the growth rates disagree **almost as widely as the sizes do**
(2.23x against 3.47x). So there are *two* disagreements stacked on each other —
one about what an ASC market is, and a second, separate one about how fast the
thing grows. Both have to be resolved, and only the first is a labelling
question.

### Disagreement 1: what is being counted

Three distinguishable scopes appear under one name.

| Tier | 2026 value | What it appears to count |
|---|---|---|
| A sliver | $5.34B | Not the facility business at any reading (see below) |
| Facility services, conservative | $47.68B-$93.53B | ASC facility fees, global |
| Facility services, broad | $100.65B-$165.61B | Facility fees plus, on the broader readings, adjacent professional, diagnostic and ancillary revenue |

Two of the geographic labels are worse than the scope labels. **Grand View's
"U.S. & Europe" reading of $124.04B (2026) is larger than four separate
publishers' *global* readings** ($47.68B, $93.25B, $93.53B, $109.04B). And
**Fortune's United-States-only $48.75B (2026) is larger than Data Bridge's
entire global market of $47.68B.** A reader comparing headlines across two tabs
has no way to see either contradiction.

### The equipment trap, and Kings Research

This topic is unusually prone to conflating the ASC *services* market (what the
facility bills) with the ASC *equipment* market (what is sold into it). The
clearest case in this file is **Kings Research**, which sizes the "global
ambulatory surgical centers market" at **$4.76B in 2024** — between 9x and 35x
smaller than every other reading — while listing **General Electric, Philips and
Getinge** among its key players [1]. Those are equipment manufacturers, not
surgery-centre operators. A $4.76B global total is not reconcilable with the
facility business at any scope (grounding #1 below disposes of it in one line),
and the player list is the tell: this is an equipment- or
capital-goods-adjacent slice carrying a facility-market name. Nothing from it is
used.

The Insight Partners is the mirror case and the honest one: it is the only
publisher in this file that puts **"services"** in the title of the thing it is
sizing [12], and it also sits at the top of the range — the two facts belong
together, because a broad services definition sweeps in diagnostic and
preoperative revenue that a pure facility-fee definition does not.

### Disagreement 2: how fast it grows

The growth spread is a separate problem and the registers settle it; see
grounding #4.

## Grounding arithmetic

US ambulatory surgery is one of the better-instrumented corners of healthcare:
CMS certifies and counts every ASC that bills Medicare, MedPAC publishes the
count, the spending and the volume each March, the payment rate is a single
published number, and three of the four largest operators are listed companies.
What the registers do **not** cover is the rest of the world, Medicare Advantage
volume, and commercial payers — which is exactly why every derived figure below
stays in prose.

### The registers, as counted

From MedPAC's March 2026 Chapter 11, which is an analysis of the CMS Provider of
Services file and the physician/supplier standard analytic files [14]:

- **6,436** Medicare-certified ASCs in 2024, up 2.2% from 6,296 in 2023; 248
  opened and 108 closed or merged, a net +140. The count has grown at 2.2% a
  year since 2019 (5,760).
- **$7.5B** of FFS Medicare payments to ASCs in 2024 — **$6B** of programme
  spending and **$1.5B** of beneficiary cost sharing — against $6.6B in 2023 and
  $5.3B in 2019.
- **3.4 million** FFS Medicare beneficiaries treated in ASCs in 2024.
- **6.4 million** FFS Medicare ASC services in 2024, from **27.9 million** FFS
  Part B beneficiaries: **229.9 services per 1,000** beneficiaries.
- **$268** of FFS payments per FFS beneficiary, and **$2,211** per FFS
  beneficiary who actually used an ASC.
- The **ASC conversion factor is $56.32 in 2026**, against an OPPS conversion
  factor of **$90.97**; ASCs are paid an OPPS-derived relative weight times that
  factor. (ASCA's reading of the same final rule gives $56.322 and $91.415 [15];
  the two differ only in rounding and in which OPPS factor variant is quoted.)
- Over **95%** of ASCs are for profit; about **94%** are urban; **68%** of those
  that billed FFS Medicare in 2024 were single-specialty.
- Cataract removal with intraocular lens insertion alone was **18.0%** of FFS
  ASC volume in 2024; the top 20 procedures were **68%**; **12** procedures
  were 50% of FFS revenue.
- ASCs file **no Medicare cost reports**. The only margin figure that exists is
  Pennsylvania's state collection: a **29.5%** operating margin for
  Pennsylvania ASCs in 2024, against a 22.3-25.6% historical range for 2015-2023.

The count itself cross-checks: Fortune Business Insights cites the Ambulatory
Surgery Center Association for **6,394** Medicare-certified ASCs in 2024 [3],
against MedPAC's 6,436 from the CMS Provider of Services file — **0.7% apart**,
on two different extraction dates. The denominator is real.

### 1. Medicare FFS spending per certified ASC — and the end of Kings Research

```
$7,500M FFS Medicare ASC payments (2024) / 6,436 certified ASCs = $1.1653M per ASC per year
```

**$1.17M per certified ASC per year, from Medicare fee-for-service alone.** This
is the floor under every reading, and it disposes of the outlier immediately:

```
Tenet USPI $5,172M + Surgery Partners $3,200M = $8,372M
$8,372M / $5,340M (Kings Research, 2026 basis) = 1.57x
```

**Two US operators, between them 558 ASCs out of a US census of 6,436 — 8.7% of
the American count and nothing outside it — book 1.57x the entirety of Kings
Research's global ambulatory surgery centre market.** That is not an estimate of
the facility business. Ruled out, and with it the $5.34B end of the 31.0x
spread.

The same arithmetic pointed at the next tier up:

```
$8,372M / $47,680M (Data Bridge, 2026 basis) = 17.6%
```

Two US operators holding 8.7% of only the US facility count would be 17.6% of
the whole world's ASC revenue. That is already strained; it becomes incoherent
against Fortune's independent **United-States-only** $48.75B, which is *larger*
than Data Bridge's global total. A global figure cannot be smaller than a
credible single-country figure for its largest country. Data Bridge's $47.68B is
ruled out as a global reading.

### 2. Revenue per certified ASC per year — the central division

This is the test the headline has to survive. Take the two readings that give a
2026 North American or US figure directly, and divide each by the census.

Coherent Market Insights publishes North America at **43.70% of its global
market in 2026** [8]:

```
$109.04B x 0.4370 = $47.65B North America (2026)
$47,650M / 6,436 certified ASCs = $7.404M per ASC per year
```

Fortune Business Insights publishes a **United States** market, advanced one
year at its own 4.1%:

```
$46.83B x 1.041 = $48.75B United States (2026)
$48,750M / 6,436 certified ASCs = $7.575M per ASC per year
```

**$7.40M and $7.58M.** Two publishers, different scopes, different base years,
different stated growth rates, one built from a global total through a published
regional share and the other built as a country market from the start — and they
land **2.3% apart** on revenue per certified ASC. The US census overstates the
denominator for a North American numerator by Canada's and Mexico's centres,
which pushes the first figure slightly low; that is the right direction and
small.

Now cross-check that against the operators, who publish both numerator and
denominator:

```
Tenet USPI:        $5,172M / (401 consolidated ASCs + 8 consolidated surgical hospitals = 409) = $12.65M per facility
Surgery Partners:  $3,200M / 176 facilities (157 ASCs + 19 surgical hospitals)                 = $18.18M per facility
```

The derived census-wide **$7.40-7.58M per ASC is 59% of USPI's per-facility
revenue and 42% of Surgery Partners'.** That gap is not a discrepancy; it is the
expected one, and it runs the only way it can:

- Both operator figures are inflated by **surgical hospitals**, which are
  licensed differently, take higher-acuity cases and are materially larger than
  an ASC. Surgery Partners' 19 of them are the reason its figure is the highest
  here.
- **68% of Medicare-certified ASCs are single-specialty** [14], and the two most
  common single specialties are gastroenterology and ophthalmology — a
  two-room endoscopy or cataract centre, not a multi-specialty orthopaedic
  campus. The census is full of them; the chains are not.
- The chains concentrate on multi-specialty and orthopaedic assets, which is why
  MedPAC records total knee arthroplasty volume in ASCs rising **27.6%** and
  total hip **28.7%** in a single year [14].

So the census average must sit well below the chain average, and it does, by the
right factor. **A global 2026 reading of $109.04B is consistent with the US
register.**

Push the same division through the high readings and it fails. Taking each
publisher's own North American share (Insight Partners at the 45% midpoint of
its published 44-46% range, Precision Business Insights at the same midpoint for
want of its own, Grand View 41.2%):

```
The Insight Partners:        $165.61B x 0.45  = $74.52B / 6,436 = $11.58M per ASC
Grand View (global):         $160.43B x 0.412 = $66.10B / 6,436 = $10.27M per ASC
Precision Business Insights: $138.59B x 0.45  = $62.37B / 6,436 =  $9.69M per ASC
```

$9.69M to $11.58M per certified ASC requires the **average** Medicare-certified
centre — two-thirds of them single-specialty, 95% for-profit, many of them small
and rural-adjacent — to book **77% to 92% of what a facility inside the largest
multi-specialty chain in the country books**, with the chain's surgical hospitals
included in its figure. It cannot. Those three readings are ruled out as
facility-services markets; they are only coherent as facility revenue plus
professional, diagnostic and ancillary revenue, and none of them says so.

And the low end of the services band is squeezed from the other side:

```
The Business Research Company: $93.25B x 0.45 = $41.96B / 6,436 = $6.52M per ASC
```

$6.52M is below both independent US-facing readings. That is defensible — TBRC
publishes the tightest definition in the file and a tight definition should
produce a smaller number — but it means **$93-109B is the window**, with
everything above ruled out on per-centre arithmetic and everything below $48B
ruled out on operator disclosure.

### 3. Medicare's share of the US facility business, and per service

What proportion of the US ASC facility pool does the programme that created the
sector actually pay for?

```
$7.5B FFS Medicare (2024) / $47.65B Coherent North America (2026) = 15.7%
$7.5B FFS Medicare (2024) / $48.75B Fortune United States (2026) = 15.4%
```

**Fee-for-service Medicare is about a sixth of US ASC facility revenue.** The two
readings agree to 0.3 points. The remainder is Medicare Advantage, commercial
payers, Medicaid and self-pay — and MedPAC says plainly that MA volume is
excluded from its chapter for data reasons [14], so $7.5B is not Medicare's
total ASC spend, only its countable part.

The per-service figure, and an internal check on it:

```
$7,500M / 6.4M FFS ASC services (2024) = $1,172 average facility payment per service
```

Independently, from MedPAC's own two per-beneficiary figures:

```
6.4M services / 3.4M beneficiaries served             = 1.88 services per user
$2,211 payments per FFS beneficiary using ASCs / 1.88 = $1,176 per service
```

**$1,172 against $1,176 — 0.3% apart**, from two different MedPAC tables built
on different denominators. The register is internally consistent, and an average
ASC facility payment of roughly $1,175 is the right order for a volume mix in
which cataract removal with an intraocular lens is 18.0% of all services.

### 4. The growth decomposition — which CAGR the register supports

This is the division that settles the *second* disagreement, and it is the one no
published estimate in this file attempts.

MedPAC decomposes the 2024 increase itself: a **3.2%** rise in the ASC
conversion factor, a **0.8%** rise in per-capita volume, an **8.4%** rise in the
average relative weight of services provided, and **0.4%** from separately paid
drugs [14]. Multiply them:

```
1.032 x 1.008 x 1.084 x 1.004 = 1.1321  ->  13.2%
```

MedPAC reports total FFS payments rising **13.0%** in 2024. The decomposition
reproduces the published total to within 0.2 points. Then the denominator:

```
1.1321 / 0.975 (FFS Part B beneficiaries fell 2.5% in 2024) = 1.1611 -> 16.1%
```

against the **15.9%** rise in payments per FFS beneficiary that MedPAC reports.
Again 0.2 points. The whole table closes.

What that buys is the composition, and it is not what a 9.8% market CAGR
implies. Of the 13.2% growth in total Medicare ASC spending in its fastest
recorded year:

- **About 4 of the 13.2 points came from volume and price together** (0.8%
  per-capita volume, 3.2% conversion factor).
- **8.4 points — nearly two-thirds — came from case mix**: the same ASCs doing
  more expensive operations, as knees, hips and now shoulders migrate out of the
  hospital. That is a one-way, finite transfer, not a compounding growth rate.
- A further 2.7 points of the headline *per-beneficiary* figure is the
  denominator shrinking as beneficiaries leave fee-for-service for Medicare
  Advantage. **About a sixth of the most-quoted growth number in this sector is
  arithmetic on a shrinking denominator, not more surgery.**

Set that against the longer run, which MedPAC also publishes: facility count
**+2.2% a year** since 2019, services per FFS beneficiary **+1.3% a year**
(2019-2023) and +3.5% in 2024, total FFS payments **+5.6% a year** (2019-2023),
and a CY2026 conversion-factor update of **2.6%** (a 3.3% market basket less a
0.7-point productivity adjustment) [15].

A facility-services market cannot compound at **9.80%** for six years when its
facility count grows 2.2%, its per-beneficiary volume grows 1.3-3.5%, and its
regulated unit price is updated 2.6%. Research and Markets' $100.65B ->
$176.40B is ruled out on growth, independently of whether its level is right.
Equally, **4.4% is at the bottom of the register's own range** — below the 5.6%
a year that Medicare FFS ASC payments actually grew from 2019 to 2023. The
honest statement is that the register supports mid-single digits, that 4.4% is
the conservative end of it, and that the case-mix component which produced
2024's outlier is a migration with a finite endpoint rather than a rate.

### What the divisions rule in and out

- **Ruled out:** Kings Research's $5.34B. Two US operators out-earn the entire
  claimed global market by 1.57x. Its key-player list names equipment makers.
- **Ruled out:** Data Bridge's $47.68B as a global figure. It is smaller than an
  independent US-only reading of the same year.
- **Ruled out:** The Insight Partners ($165.61B), Grand View global ($160.43B)
  and Precision Business Insights ($138.59B) as facility-services markets. Each
  implies $9.69-11.58M of revenue per certified ASC, 77-92% of a large chain's
  per-facility revenue, for a census two-thirds of which is single-specialty.
- **Ruled out on growth:** Research and Markets' 9.67% stated / 9.80% implied.
  No component of the register grows at half that rate.
- **Ruled out as a geography:** Grand View's $124.04B "U.S. & Europe" reading
  cannot be set beside anyone's global figure, because it exceeds four of them.
- **Ruled in:** Coherent Market Insights' **$109.04B (2026)**. It is a published
  2026 value — our base year exactly, so no normalisation is needed — its stated
  4.4% reconciles with its own 2033 endpoint to three decimal places, and the
  North American share it publishes for the same year divides into the CMS
  census at $7.404M per ASC, within 2.3% of a wholly independent publisher's
  US-only reading.
- **Ruled in as corroboration only:** Technavio publishes no total, but values
  the single-specialty segment at **$51.19B in 2024** [13]. Grand View and Kings
  Research separately put single-specialty at 61.2% of the market. $51.19B /
  0.612 = $83.64B for 2024; advanced at Technavio's own 6.3% gives $94.53B for
  2026 — inside the $93-109B window, from a fourth direction. It is a
  cross-publisher derivation, not a published figure, and it is not published as
  a number anywhere in the JSON.

## What we publish

**Scope: the global ambulatory surgery centre facility-services market.** Base
2026 **$109.04B** (Coherent Market Insights, published for 2026 [8]), forecast
2032 **$141.19B**, CAGR **4.40%**.

The 2032 endpoint is not read off a page; it is **Coherent's own published 2033
figure of $147.39B discounted one year at Coherent's own stated 4.4%**:

```
$147.39B / 1.044 = $141.18B
```

and, compounded forward from the published base to check the other way:

```
$109.04B x 1.044^6 = $141.1851B
```

Published as **$141.19B**, and the implied rate is
(141.19/109.04)^(1/6) - 1 = **4.4006%**, published as 4.40%. One year of
arithmetic at the publisher's own rate, shown here so a reader can reverse it.
Coherent's own 2026-2033 endpoints imply 4.399%, so its stated 4.4% is accurate
to its own series and nothing has been bent to make the series close.

There is a second reason to be comfortable with that endpoint. **Persistence
Market Research independently publishes $141.19B for 2032** [6] — from a
different base year ($88.40B in 2025), a different stated CAGR (5.8%) and a
different segmentation. Two publishers who agree on nothing else converge to the
cent on 2032. Persistence's own stated 5.8% does *not* reconcile with its
endpoints, which imply (141.19/88.40)^(1/7) - 1 = **6.92%**, so its base and its
rate cannot both be right; its 2032 figure is the one that corroborates.

`market_forecast` interpolates 2026-2032 at 4.40% from exactly $109.04B: 109.04,
113.84, 118.85, 124.08, 129.54, 135.23, 141.19. The chart is labelled as
modelled between published endpoints, because the intermediate years are
arithmetic and not observation.

`industry` is **Healthcare IT**, per the corpus value assigned to this report.

## Do not publish

- **`regional_analysis: []`.** Four publishers do give a North American share —
  Coherent 43.70% (2026), Persistence 48.6% (2025), Insight Partners 44-46%
  (2025), Grand View 41.2% (2025) — and they span seven points on four
  different bases, which is itself the reason not to table one. More
  importantly, the $47.65B North American figure that the central grounding
  turns on is **our own multiplication** of one publisher's global total by that
  publisher's share. It belongs in prose where the multiplication is visible,
  not in a regional table where it would read as a sourced estimate. The share
  is attributed to Coherent in the prose and nowhere else.
- **`country_analysis: []`.** Every register in this file is United States only.
  The $48.75B US reading is Fortune's, normalised by us; the $7.40-7.58M per
  certified ASC is ours. Neither is a published country estimate of the global
  market we headline, and a US row in a country table would convert our own
  arithmetic into someone else's figure.
- **No vendor market share anywhere.** `competitive_landscape[].market_share` is
  absent throughout. SCA Health does not disclose segment revenue at all — it is
  consolidated inside Optum Health — and HCA does not break out ASC revenue, so
  even the numerator for a share calculation does not exist for two of the four
  largest operators. Facility *counts* are given where disclosed, and are
  labelled as counts.
- **No "two operators are X% of the market".** The $8,372M combined figure and
  the 558-of-6,436 facility count are used above only to rule a reading in or
  out. They are not a share: USPI's $5,172M includes 26 surgical hospitals and
  excludes revenue from the 132 ASCs it holds interests in but does not
  consolidate, and Surgery Partners' $3.2B includes 19 surgical hospitals.
- **No global ASC count.** CMS counts Medicare-certified US facilities. Nobody
  counts the world's, so no global per-centre figure is computed anywhere.
- **No grossed-up Medicare figure.** MedPAC excludes Medicare Advantage ASC
  volume for data reasons. Inflating $7.5B by an assumed MA share would
  manufacture the one number the register deliberately does not provide.
- **No ASC operating margin as a sector figure.** The only margin in existence is
  Pennsylvania's 29.5% for 2024, from one state's cost-containment council. It
  is quoted with that attribution and never generalised; MedPAC's standing
  recommendation that Congress require ASC cost reports exists precisely because
  the sector figure does not exist.
- **No segment percentages.** Single-specialty at 61.2%, freestanding at
  66.9-72%, physician-owned at 59.8-62.1% and orthopaedics at 26.4% are each
  published by one house and disagree across houses. Segmentation is published
