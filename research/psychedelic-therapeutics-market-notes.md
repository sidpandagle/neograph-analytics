## The spread

**At 2025, $3.12B to $7.4B — 2.37x.** CAGRs cluster tightly at 13.68% to 15.4%, a 1.13x spread, with
one dated outlier at 7.5%.

2.37x on the base with near-unanimity on the rate is an odd signature. It usually means the
publishers share a model and disagree about its starting level. **In this market it means something
worse: they share a definition of "psychedelic" that includes two substances that are not
psychedelics, and the arithmetic shows those two substances are more than the whole published
total.**

Both sources read directly from their own pages list the same four substance types:

> **gamma-hydroxybutyrate (GHB), ketamine, MDMA, psilocybin**

**GHB is sodium oxybate.** It is a CNS depressant marketed for narcolepsy and idiopathic
hypersomnia as Jazz Pharmaceuticals' Xyrem and Xywav. It is not a psychedelic by any
pharmacological definition — it is not a serotonergic 5-HT2A agonist, it does not produce the
perceptual effects the word describes, and nobody prescribes it to alter consciousness
therapeutically. Coherent Market Insights makes **GHB its largest substance segment at 34.2%** and
puts **Jazz Pharmaceuticals in its competitor list**, which removes any doubt about what is being
counted.

**Ketamine is the second.** Racemic ketamine is an anaesthetic used off-label in infusion clinics;
esketamine (Spravato) is an approved antidepressant. Neither is a classic psychedelic either,
though the case for including them is far stronger than for GHB.

**Meanwhile the substances a reader searching "psychedelic therapeutics" actually means — psilocybin
and MDMA — have no approved product anywhere.** FDA issued a complete response letter on
midomafetamine (MDMA) on **9 August 2024**. No psilocybin product holds a marketing authorisation.

So the spread is not the story here. **The composition is**, and it can be settled exactly, because
both non-psychedelic franchises are disclosed by listed companies.

### Test 1 — the two non-psychedelic franchises against the whole published market

```
Spravato (esketamine)         $1,696M
Jazz oxybate (GHB)            $1,846M
                              -------
                              $3,542M

$3,542M / $3,120M  (Coherent Market Insights, 2025)  =  113.5%
$3,542M / $3,190M  (Roots Analysis, 2025)            =  111.0%
$3,542M / $4,130M  (SNS Insider, 2025)               =   85.8%
$3,542M / $4,860M  (Maximize, 2025)                  =   72.9%
$3,542M / $5,820M  (Towards Healthcare, 2025)        =   60.9%
$3,542M / $7,400M  (Research and Markets, 2025)      =   47.9%
```

**Two disclosed franchises — one narcolepsy drug and one antidepressant, neither of them a
psychedelic — exceed the entire published market of the two publishers whose scope tables we read
directly.** They are 113.5% of Coherent Market Insights' 2025 total and 111.0% of Roots Analysis's.

That leaves the psilocybin and MDMA segments of those models with a **negative** implied value, in
a category named after them.

### Test 2 — Coherent Market Insights undercounts its own largest segment

CMI publishes **GHB as 34.2% of its own total**:

```
34.2% x $3,120M = $1,067M   <- CMI's implied GHB segment
Jazz's disclosed oxybate net sales             $1,846M
Ratio                                             1.73x
```

**One company's disclosed revenue for one substance is 1.73x the segment that substance is
supposed to occupy in the published model**, and Jazz is not the only oxybate seller — its own
disclosure references high-sodium oxybate competition and authorised-generic royalties.

This matters for how the figure should be read. **The published total is not too high for its own
definition. It is too low**, because the definition includes a $1.8B+ narcolepsy franchise that the
model sizes at $1.1B. That is a different and more awkward defect than the usual
over-estimation: these reports simultaneously **overstate** what psychedelics are worth (by
including GHB and ketamine) and **understate** the category they have defined (by mis-sizing GHB).

### Test 3 — can the residual be the state-regulated programmes?

If everything beyond esketamine is the legal, non-trial psilocybin economy, the Oregon register
prices it:

```
$3,120M (CMI total) - $1,696M (Spravato) = $1,424M of non-esketamine "psychedelic" revenue
$1,424M / 5,935 Oregon clients in 2025                  =  $239,900 per client
$1,424M / 5,375 Oregon administration sessions in 2025   =  $264,900 per session

$7,400M (highest base) - $1,696M = $5,704M
$5,704M / 5,935 Oregon clients                           =  $961,000 per client
```

Against measured session prices in the adjacent legal market — **a clinical ketamine session runs
roughly $275 to $1,300; an IV infusion $400 to $800, with a six-session induction course at $2,400
to $4,800; Spravato at about $900 to $1,300 per session self-pay including monitoring** — a
psilocybin administration session priced at **$264,900** is out by a factor of **200 to 960**.

**The state programmes cannot be the residual.** Oregon is the largest regulated non-clinical
psilocybin market in the United States and it served 5,935 people in a year. At even $2,000 all-in
per client — above the top of the measured clinical ketamine session range — the whole Oregon
programme is of the order of **$12 million**. It is a rounding error against any of these market
sizes, and that is the point.

### Test 4 — can the residual be the pipeline?

```
$1,424M (CMI residual) / 432 psilocybin + MDMA registered studies  =  $3.30M per study
$5,704M (highest residual) / 432 studies                            =  $8.93M per study
```

This is the same arithmetic that NeoGraph's **`phage-therapy-market`** report used to rule out the
high cluster in that market, and it reaches the same conclusion for the same reason. It **cannot be
product revenue**, because no psilocybin or MDMA product holds a marketing authorisation anywhere.
And it is not credible as sponsor R&D spend across a population in which only **102 of 307
psilocybin studies are recruiting and only 15 have ever been Phase 3**, with most of the rest
academic investigator-initiated work.

The sector's actual spend is visible in its most advanced programme. Compass Pathways' entire
pivotal effort for COMP360 is **two trials totalling 839 participants** — COMP005 (n=258, single
25 mg dose versus placebo) and COMP006 (n=581, fixed repeat dosing across 25 mg, 10 mg and 1 mg
arms). Two registrational trials of that size are a programme costing a few hundred million dollars
in total, not $1.4 billion of annual revenue.

### What the register says that the market sizes do not

**Psilocybin, the only classic psychedelic with a credible near-term approval:**
- FDA granted Compass Pathways **Breakthrough Therapy designation for COMP360 in treatment-resistant
  depression in 2018**.
- **COMP005** (n=258) met its primary endpoint, with a reported Week 6 MADRS difference of **3.6
  points** for 25 mg versus placebo.
- **COMP006** (n=581) met its primary endpoint, announced **17 February 2026**: 25 mg versus 1 mg
  gave a **highly statistically significant** MADRS reduction, **p<0.001**, difference **-3.8
  points** at Week 6.
- **7 July 2026**: 26-week data — **39% of the 25 mg arm achieved a ≥25% MADRS reduction by Week 6
  and maintained durable response at least through Week 26**, against 25% in COMP005 after a single
  dose. Serious adverse events were similar across arms (6.3% at 1 mg, 5.7% at 25 mg) over 26 weeks.
- FDA granted a **rolling NDA review**, ongoing; **in April 2026 FDA issued a National Priority
  Voucher** for COMP360 in TRD; one source puts the NDA submission target at **Q4 2026**. **We could
  not confirm whether the NDA has been completed — flagged.**

**MDMA, the programme that was closest and failed:**
- FDA's **Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee voted 10 to 1 against** recommending
  midomafetamine-assisted therapy for PTSD at its **June 2024** meeting, citing possible selection
  bias and **functional unblinding** — participants could tell whether they had received the drug.
- FDA issued a **complete response letter on 9 August 2024**, stating the application could not be
  approved on the data submitted and requesting **an additional Phase 3 trial** plus further safety
  and efficacy study. Lykos said the letter's issues echoed the advisory committee's; its CEO called
  the request for another Phase 3 "deeply disappointing" and argued much of it could be addressed
  with existing data, post-approval requirements or the literature. FDA said there were significant
  limitations preventing a conclusion of safety and efficacy.

**Esketamine, the one that worked:**
- Spravato is approved, discloses revenue, grew **57.4% to $1,696M in 2025**, and reached **$503M in
  the fourth quarter alone** — a run rate above $2 billion. It is also the single product that makes
  every published figure in this market unreadable, because whether a publisher counts it moves the
  total by more than half.

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope and plausibility tests. They establish that two
disclosed non-psychedelic franchises exceed the entire published market of both publishers read
directly; that one publisher's own largest substance segment is sized at 58% of one company's
disclosed revenue for that substance; that the residual cannot be the state-regulated programmes,
which Oregon's own register caps at 5,935 clients and 5,375 sessions in a year; and that the
residual works as neither product revenue nor research spend across 432 registered studies of which
15 have ever been Phase 3. They do **not** establish the correct size of anything. The Spravato
figures were reconciled by us from a mis-converted PDF table, with all four percentages checked
against their own numerators. Jazz's Xyrem figure came from trade press. Oregon's client count is a
state register but the centre and facilitator counts are December 2024 trade reporting, and no
Colorado figures were obtained at all. Session prices are from a clinic-pricing website whose own
clinic count is internally inconsistent — 376 in one place and 1,800 in another — and we use its
prices for order of magnitude only. And dividing company revenue into a published market total
compares differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $3.60B (2026) → $8.50B (2032) at 15.4%.**

Source: **Coherent Market Insights, *Psychedelic Drugs Market*, report CMI-185, 250+ pages,
published 10 April 2025**, which states **$3.12 billion for 2025 (estimated)**, $8.50 billion for
2032 and a **15.4% CAGR for 2025–2032** on a 2024 base year. The 2026 base is **derived by
advancing CMI's own published 2025 figure one year at CMI's own stated rate** (3.12 × 1.154 =
3.60). The 2032 endpoint is **CMI's own published 2032 value**. Internal check: the implied rate
between our endpoints is 15.39%, against CMI's stated 15.4%.

Chosen because: (a) it is one of only two series in the set **read directly from the publisher's own
page**, with a report ID, a page count, a stated publication date and — crucially — **a published
substance-type list that makes its scope auditable**; (b) its **2032 endpoint matches this report's
forecast horizon exactly**, so the terminal value is the publisher's own rather than our extension;
and (c) it is the **lowest base in the set**, which is the only one within reach of the disclosed
record, and it is still exceeded by two disclosed non-psychedelic franchises.

**It is published with a warning attached in every prose field of the report, and the warning is the
report.** This figure is not a measure of psychedelic therapeutics. It is a category that includes
sodium oxybate and ketamine, and two disclosed franchises in those substances — Jazz's oxybate at
roughly $1,846 million and J&J's Spravato at $1,696 million, totalling $3,542 million — are
**113.5% of its entire 2025 base**. On the publisher's own 34.2% GHB share, its GHB segment is
$1,067 million against Jazz's disclosed $1,846 million, so it **undercounts its own largest
segment by 1.73x**. Psilocybin and MDMA, the substances the category is named for, contribute
**approximately zero approved-product revenue**, because no psilocybin or MDMA product holds a
marketing authorisation and FDA issued a complete response letter on midomafetamine on 9 August
2024.

**The defensible statement about psilocybin and MDMA specifically is a register, not a market
size:** 307 psilocybin studies (102 recruiting, 15 ever Phase 3), 125 MDMA studies, 5,935 clients
in Oregon's regulated programme in 2025 across 5,375 administration sessions, two positive Phase 3
readouts for COMP360 totalling 839 participants, a rolling NDA review and an April 2026 National
Priority Voucher. The report publishes those numbers and tells the reader to track them instead of
the forecast.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Pharmaceuticals.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Nobody discloses it, and the two companies whose revenue dominates this
  category — Jazz and Johnson & Johnson — do not describe themselves as being in it.
  `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely. The ratios we do publish — $3,542M at
  113.5% of CMI's total, Jazz's oxybate at 1.73x CMI's own GHB segment, Spravato at 54.4% of CMI's
  total — are stated in prose with both inputs named.
- **CMI's origin and substance shares as a description of psychedelics** (natural 52.7%, GHB 34.2%).
  The GHB figure is used inside Test 2 specifically to show it is 1.73x too small for its own
  substance, and neither share is published. Segmentation goes out as plain strings.
- **Regional and country splits.** CMI publishes none; Roots Analysis names four regions without
  values. `regional_analysis` and `country_analysis` stay `[]`.
- **A psilocybin or MDMA market size.** There is none. No approved product exists in either, so
  approved-product revenue is approximately zero, and we say that rather than deriving a figure.
- **An Oregon programme revenue figure.** Our order-of-magnitude "roughly $12 million" is 5,935
  state-registered clients multiplied by a price above the top of a clinic-pricing website's
  measured ketamine session range. It is a labelled plausibility figure stated in prose and is never
  published as a market size.
- **A Colorado figure of any kind.** The programme exists and launched healing centres in 2024 under
  Proposition 122, covering more than psilocybin. **We found no client or centre count and publish
  none.** Recorded here as a gap in this research pass.
- **Any US ketamine clinic count.** The one source we found gives **376 in its own title and 1,800
  in its text** — a 4.8x internal contradiction. Recorded as a defect; no count published and no
  per-clinic arithmetic attempted.
- **Ketamine and Spravato session prices as market inputs.** They come from a clinic-pricing website
  that describes its own average as survey-based and contradicts itself on clinic count. Used for
  order of magnitude inside Test 3, labelled, and not published as prices.
- **CMI's competitor list as a competitive landscape.** It names Celon Pharma, iX Biopharma, Lykos,
  MindMed and Janssen as leaders and adds Jazz only in a separate section, and its own page is
  internally inconsistent about this. We name companies from the disclosed and regulatory record
  instead, and we say plainly which of them are pre-revenue.
- **Jazz's Xyrem figure as a primary disclosure.** It came from trade press. The Xywav figure and
  patient count are from Jazz's own materials.
- **Any claim that the COMP360 NDA has been submitted or accepted.** FDA granted a rolling review
  and issued a National Priority Voucher in April 2026, and one source gives a Q4 2026 submission
  target. We could not confirm completion and do not assert it.
- **The 7.5% BCC/TBRC rate.** Its base year is 2021 and its horizon ends in 2027. Recorded for range
  only.
