# Nuclear Medicine Radioisotope Market — research notes
**Slug:** nuclear-medicine-radioisotope-market   **Drafted:** 2026-10-16

## Scope boundary — read this first

This report covers the **isotope supply chain and generators**: irradiation of targets in research
reactors, processing of molybdenum-99, international shipment, and the Tc-99m generators that
hospitals elute at the bedside. It stops at the generator or bulk-isotope sale.

It does **not** cover finished radiopharmaceutical drug products — Pylarify, Definity, Pluvicto,
Illuccix, FDG doses, Lu-177 therapies. Those are the subject of the already-published NeoGraph
report **`radiopharmaceuticals-market`**, which puts that market at **$8.08 billion in 2026 rising
to $12.49 billion by 2032 at 7.53%**. That boundary is not a presentational nicety. **It is the
entire explanation for the spread measured below**, and the grounding arithmetic in this file
shows that several publishers selling a "nuclear medicine radioisotope market" report are in fact
sizing the drug-product market that the other report already covers.

## The spread

**At 2025–2026 the published figures run from about $707M to $11.77B — a 16.6x spread.** CAGRs run
3.4% to 19.15%, a 5.6x spread.

But 16.6x is the wrong way to read this table, because the estimates do not form a continuum.
**They form two clusters with an order of magnitude of empty space between them:**

```
ISOTOPE-TIER CLUSTER                      DRUG-PRODUCT-TIER CLUSTER
Grand Research Store   ~$707M (2025)      TMR                    $5.9B  (2024)
Report Prime           ~$709M (2025)      IMARC                  $8.4B  (2025)
Persistence (Mo-99)     $963.7M (2026)    Global Industry Analysts $10.6B (2024)
MarketIntelo (gens)     $1.8B  (2025)     MarketsandMarkets      $10.41B (2025)
                                          DelveInsight          ~$10.7B (2025)
--- nothing published between $1.8B and $5.9B ---
                                          Fortune Business Ins.  $11.77B (2025)
```

Within the lower cluster the spread is 2.5x; within the upper cluster 2.0x. **Between the
clusters it is 3.3x to 16.6x, and there is not a single published estimate in the gap.** A
bimodal distribution with an empty middle is the signature of two different things being
measured and given the same name — not of analysts disagreeing about growth.

The dividing line is the one this report's scope note draws. The lower cluster prices **the
isotope as supplied**: Mo-99 leaving a processor, a generator arriving at a hospital. The upper
cluster prices **the finished radiopharmaceutical**: a patient dose, a therapy vial, a PET
tracer. And the upper cluster's centre of gravity is unmistakable — **IMARC's $8.4B for 2025
against NeoGraph's own radiopharmaceuticals-market base of $8.08B for 2026. Those are the same
market.** One publisher's "nuclear medicine radioisotopes market" is, to within 4%, another
market's drug-product total.

The arithmetic below proves it three ways, and this is an unusually good market in which to try,
because the physical quantity is counted in a published unit by an intergovernmental body.

## Grounding arithmetic

**Registers used:** the OECD Nuclear Energy Agency's *Current Trends in the Supply and Utilisation
of Medical Radioisotopes* (NEA No. 7743, v1.1, **21 October 2025**), which publishes reactor
capacity in **6-day curies per week**; the NEA's full-cost-recovery methodology for Mo-99
irradiation; and company-disclosed revenue at Lantheus, GE HealthCare and Cardinal Health.

Currency conversions are at the **IRS yearly average exchange rates for 2025** — the source used
in round 3 of this corpus and reused here — **EUR 0.886 per US dollar**, retrieved 16 October 2026.

### The physical register

NEA No. 7743, Table 2, *Research reactors with significant Mo-99 production capacity*:

| Reactor | Location | Commissioned | Approximate capacity (6-day Ci/week) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BR2** | Mol, Belgium | 1961 | **8,600** |
| **SAFARI-1** | Pelindaba, South Africa | 1965 | **6,200** |
| **LVR-15** | Řež, Czechia | 1957 | **3,200** |
| **MARIA** | Świerk, Poland | 1974 | **3,000** |
| **HFR** | Petten, Netherlands | 1961 | **3,000** |
| **OPAL** | Lucas Heights, Australia | 2006 | **2,200** |
| | | **Total** | **26,200** |

NEA states the table shows only sources above 2,000 6-day Ci/week, and that **approximately 95% of
global Mo-99 supply comes from these reactors** (citing Ferruci et al., 2022). So:

```
Six listed reactors                      26,200  6-day Ci/week
Implied global capacity at 95% coverage  27,580  6-day Ci/week
Annualised at 52 weeks                1,434,160  6-day Ci/year
```

Three things make that a **ceiling, not an output figure**, and all three push the realised
price-per-curie *up*: these are nameplate capacities, not production; NEA explicitly discusses
**outage reserve capacity (ORC)** that is deliberately held idle against unplanned shutdowns; and
unplanned shutdowns happen — **the HFR was shut down in October 2024 for a structural issue
requiring immediate repair**, which combined with scheduled maintenance elsewhere to produce a
shortage of both Mo-99 and Tc-99m. Every division below therefore understates the per-curie price
of the isotope, which strengthens rather than weakens the conclusion.

NEA's procedure counts, with an internal inconsistency we flag:

```
Tc-99m share of nuclear medicine procedures          80-85%        (NEA No. 7743)
Tc-99m procedures per year, Chapter 1                "over 30 million"   (citing NEA, 2018)
Mo-99 procedures per year, Chapter 2                 "over 40 million"
Tc-99m "medical treatments" per year, Chapter 2      "more than 40 million"  (citing BWXT, 2023)
```

**The same report gives both "over 30 million" and "over 40 million" for what is substantially the
same quantity, in different chapters and from different citations. Flagged.** Both are used below
as a band rather than either being taken as the figure.

### Test 1 — dollars per 6-day curie, against the NEA's own cost stack

```
$963.7M  (Persistence, Mo-99, 2026)   / 1,434,160 6-day Ci  =    $672 per 6-day Ci
$1,800M  (MarketIntelo, generators)   / 1,434,160 6-day Ci  =  $1,255 per 6-day Ci
$8,400M  (IMARC, 2025)                / 1,434,160 6-day Ci  =  $5,858 per 6-day Ci
$11,770M (Fortune, 2025)              / 1,434,160 6-day Ci  =  $8,207 per 6-day Ci
```

Now the measured prices. The NEA's own pre-shortage levelised cost stack per 6-day curie, and the
required prices it derived (2011 NEA presentation to RRFM, in euros; USD at IRS 2025 average):

| Stage of the chain | Levelised cost | Required price | Required price, USD |
|---|---|---|---|
| At the reactor | €45 | €55–400 | $51 | $62–452 |
| At the processor | €315 | €415–855 | $356 | $468–965 |
| **At the generator** | **€375** | **€475–915** | **$423** | **$536–1,033** |
| At the radiopharmacy | €1,810 | €1,910–2,350 | $2,043 | $2,156–2,652 |

And an independent, older figure: a 2008 US study cited by the European Nuclear Society put Mo-99
production costs at **$125–325 per curie calibrated at 6 days** against a **selling price of about
$470 per curie**.

```
Persistence's Mo-99 market implies   $672 per 6-day Ci
NEA's required price at the generator stage   $536 - $1,033
2008 US study's observed selling price        ~$470
```

**$672 lands inside the NEA's own generator-stage price band and within 1.4x of the observed 2008
selling price.** The isotope-tier published figures are approximately right for the isotope tier.

```
$5,858 per 6-day Ci (IMARC)   /  $423 generator-stage levelised cost  =  13.8x
$5,858 per 6-day Ci (IMARC)   /  $2,043 radiopharmacy-stage cost      =   2.9x
$8,207 per 6-day Ci (Fortune) /  $2,043 radiopharmacy-stage cost      =   4.0x
```

**The upper cluster is not the isotope at any tier of the chain — not at the reactor, not at the
processor, not at the generator, and not even at the radiopharmacy.** It is 2.9x to 4.0x the full
radiopharmacy cost of a Tc-99m dose, which is what you get when therapeutic radioligands and PET
tracers priced in thousands of dollars per dose are added to the count.

### Test 2 — dollars per procedure, against the NEA's full-cost-recovery study

The NEA's 2012 full-cost-recovery methodology for Mo-99 irradiation states that **the cost of an
average patient dose at generator sale is around USD 21, of which only about USD 2 is associated
with the irradiation step**, and that reactors would need to raise prices a further **40%** to
achieve full cost recovery.

```
30 million Tc-99m procedures x $21 per dose at generator sale  =   $630M
40 million Tc-99m procedures x $21 per dose at generator sale  =   $840M
                                                                  -------
NEA-implied generator-sale value of the Tc-99m chain:          $630M - $840M
```

Against the published estimates:

```
Grand Research Store / Report Prime, ~$707-709M (2025)   inside the band
Persistence, Mo-99, $963.7M (2026)                       1.15x the top of the band
MarketIntelo, generators, $1,800M (2025)                 2.1x the top of the band
IMARC, $8,400M (2025)                                    10.0x the top of the band
Fortune, $11,770M (2025)                                 14.0x the top of the band
```

**Both "medical radioisotopes" series sit inside NEA's own band, and Persistence's Mo-99 figure
sits 15% above it** — which is what you would expect, since Mo-99 also feeds procedures outside
the 30–40 million Tc-99m count and NEA notes producers are below full cost recovery. Per
procedure, IMARC's figure is $210 and Fortune's is $294. **Those are finished-dose prices, not
isotope prices.**

```
$86.8M / $963.7M  (Persistence, Mo-99, 2026)   =  9.0%
```

**9.0% of the world Mo-99 market for one of a handful of generator suppliers is a credible
position. 0.7% is not** — not for a company whose generator product has supplied North American
hospitals for decades, in a chain NEA describes as dependent on six reactors and a handful of
processors.

### What the register says about the supply side, which the market sizes do not

NEA No. 7743 documents a supply chain under structural strain that no published CAGR reflects:

- **Marginal cost is far below average cost** for a non-storable good, so NEA observes that one
  way producers break even is **by restricting capacity until periodic supply interruptions
  generate price peaks** during which fixed capital costs are recovered. NEA says this means "even
  theoretical full cost recovery implies high price volatility and periodic price interruptions",
  and that the volatility then **induces producers to restrict capacity further**.
- **Producers "sometimes receive what could be characterised as state subsidies that obscure the
  actual cost of production"**, which NEA says deters new entrants.
- **NorthStar Medical Radioisotopes halted Mo-99 production** (active 2018–2023), attributing the
  withdrawal to rising production costs and competition from foreign government-subsidised
  producers. NEA calls this an illustration of how an inefficient market drives new suppliers out.
- **Demand growth is 5% annually in emerging markets and 0.5% in mature markets** (NEA, 2023).
  A weighted rate consistent with those two numbers is low single digits — which is where the
  isotope-tier CAGRs (3.4%, 4.2%, 4.3%) sit and nowhere near 15.1% or 19.15%.
- **Three of the six listed reactors were commissioned before 1962** and BR2 will use some HEU
  until a planned complete phase-out in 2026.
- New capacity is a decade out: **PALLAS** (Netherlands, 55 MW LEU, €2 billion of state aid, 300
  days a year) in the **early 2030s**; France's **Jules Horowitz Reactor** by **2032**, with
  capacity to meet 25–50% of Europe's annual Mo-99 demand; **SHINE Technologies' Chrysalis**
  accelerator in **2027**, which the company says could address more than a third of global demand;
  Argentina's **RA-10**; and Mo-99 production at **Darlington** via the Laurentis/BWXT Target
  Delivery System, pending FDA and Health Canada approval. Ontario's CANDU reactors already produce
  around **50% of the world's cobalt-60**.

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope and plausibility tests. They establish that
the published estimates are bimodal with an empty order of magnitude in the middle; that the lower
cluster prices at $672 per 6-day curie, inside the NEA's own generator-stage required-price band
and within 1.4x of an observed 1970s-to-2000s selling price; that the lower cluster sits inside or
just above NEA's own $630–840M per-dose arithmetic while the upper cluster is 10–14x it; that
inside Lantheus the drug-product line is 11.4x the generator line; and that two drug-product
disclosures are 4.6x the whole Mo-99 market and 53% of IMARC's. They do **not** establish the
correct size of either tier. The 1,434,160 6-day Ci/year denominator is **nameplate capacity
grossed up from a 95% coverage statement, not measured output**, and real output is lower, so
every per-curie figure here is a floor. NEA's cost stack is from 2011 and its euro figures are
converted by us at a 2025 average rate — a 14-year gap, and inflation alone would raise the
generator-stage band. The procedure count is internally inconsistent inside NEA's own report
("over 30 million" and "over 40 million"). Lantheus's TechneLite line is a US-centric product and
its ratio to a global market mixes scopes. And dividing company revenue into a published market
total compares differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $963.7M (2026) → $1,177.8M (2032) at 3.4%. Unit: USD Million.**

Source: **Persistence Market Research, *Molybdenum-99 Market*, report PMRREP38370, 195 pages,
page dated October 2026**, which states **$963.7 million for 2026**, $1,217.8 million for 2033 and
a **3.4% CAGR for 2026–2033**. The 2026 base is **the publisher's own published 2026 figure, with
no interpolation**. The 2032 endpoint is **derived by compounding the publisher's own stated rate
over six years** (963.7 × 1.034^6 = 1,177.8), which is a shortening of the publisher's horizon by
one year, not an extension of it. Internal check: the implied rate between our endpoints is 3.40%,
identical to Persistence's stated rate.

Chosen because: (a) it is **the only isotope-tier series read directly from a publisher's own
page**, with a report ID, a page count and a stated month — every competing isotope-tier figure
came through a search summary, and one of those listings has since returned 404; (b) it has a
**published 2026 base**, so the base year needs no interpolation at all; (c) its implied **$672 per
6-day curie sits inside the NEA's own generator-stage required-price band of $536–1,033**, and it
sits 15% above NEA's own $630–840M per-dose arithmetic, so it is the published figure best
supported by the primary register; (d) one of the handful of global generator suppliers discloses
**9.0% of it**, a credible position, against 0.7–1.0% of the upper-cluster figures; and (e) its
**3.4% rate is consistent with NEA's own demand observation** of 5% growth in emerging markets and
0.5% in mature ones, where the upper cluster's 15.1% and 19.15% are not.

**The scope limitation is stated plainly and repeatedly.** Persistence scoped its report to
**Mo-99**, the parent isotope behind the 80–85% of nuclear medicine procedures that use Tc-99m. It
is therefore the best-documented component of the isotope tier rather than the whole of it: I-131,
Lu-177 bulk isotope, F-18 and the rest are outside it. The two independent "medical radioisotopes"
series that attempt the whole isotope tier give **~$707M and ~$709M for 2025 — both *below*
Persistence's Mo-99-only figure**, which is incoherent and is why neither is published. The honest
statement, made in `market_overview`, `market_context` and `forecast_analysis`, is that the isotope
tier is somewhere in the **$0.7–1.8 billion** band, that Mo-99 is the part of it that can be
grounded against a public register, and that anything in the $5.9–11.8 billion range is the
drug-product tier covered by `radiopharmaceuticals-market`.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Curium is private. Cardinal Health's Nuclear and Precision Health
  Solutions is folded into an "Other" segment with two unrelated businesses. No producer publishes
  its share of Mo-99 supply, and NEA explicitly states that the contribution of each step to cost
  is proprietary. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely. The ratios we do publish
  — TechneLite at 9.0% of the Mo-99 market and 5.6% of its own parent's revenue, Pylarify at 11.4x
  TechneLite, two disclosures at 53% of IMARC's total — are stated in prose with both inputs named.
- **Persistence's regional and country shares** (North America ~38% of global, US ~90% of North
  America, Germany ~23% / France ~18% / UK ~15% of Europe, Japan ~38% / India ~9% / Southeast Asia
  ~8% of Asia Pacific). One source's apportionment of a single-isotope total, with no method
  stated. `regional_analysis` and `country_analysis` stay `[]`.
- **Persistence's segment shares** (fission ~39% of production method, cardiovascular ~34% of
  application, hospitals ~57% of end user). One source, one page. Segmentation goes out as plain
  strings. The fission figure is additionally odd: NEA describes fission from uranium targets as
  the dominant route by a wide margin, so 39% does not reconcile with the primary register, and we
  record that as a defect rather than publishing either number.
- **DelveInsight's "diagnostics ~77% of 2025".** Read from a republication of a release, and
  attached to an upper-cluster total this report assigns to a different market.
- **A per-curie or per-procedure market size.** Our $672 per 6-day curie and $630–840M per-dose
  band are labelled plausibility calculations, built on nameplate capacity grossed up from a
  coverage statement and on a 2012 cost study. They are stated in prose and never published as a
  price or a market size.
- **Any Curium revenue figure.** We found none. Named as one of the two largest processors and
  stated as not disclosing, rather than estimated.
- **Any Cardinal Health nuclear revenue figure.** The company reports it inside a $5,382M "Other"
  segment that also holds at-Home Solutions and OptiFreight Logistics. We publish the segment
  total as what it is — a segment containing three businesses — and no nuclear line.
- **The two "medical radioisotopes" series as a headline.** Grand Research Store's listing now
  404s; Report Prime's page carries unrelated category tags. Their agreement to within 0.5% at both
  ends is striking and is recorded, but two low-quality pages agreeing is at least as likely to be
  one copying the other as to be independent corroboration, and both give a whole-isotope-tier
  figure *below* a single-isotope figure, which cannot be right.
- **The self-contradictory Mo-99 alternates** ($4.14B/2025 at 5.50%; $5.6B/2022 at 4.6% alongside
  $6.4B → over $11.5B from the same publisher). Recorded as a defect; used for nothing.
- **The Fortune Business Insights 19.15% rate.** It implies the Tc-99m supply chain growing at four
  times NEA's measured emerging-market demand growth and 38 times its mature-market growth, in a
  chain whose newest large reactor is PALLAS in the early 2030s. Cited only as the top of the
  upper cluster.
- **A count of ORC or of actual annual Mo-99 output.** NEA discusses outage reserve capacity as a
