## The spread

**At 2025, $13.37B (MarketsandMarkets, advanced only) to $24.4B (PS Market Research, all wound care) — a 1.83x spread.** At the far end, $15.7B (2031) to $35.7B (2032).

Published CAGRs run **4.8% to 7.6%, a 1.58x spread** — and within the advanced-only group alone they run 4.8% to 7.6%, so for once even the growth rates are not agreed.

But the base-year spread here is **not a disagreement at all, and this is the one market in the batch where a publisher resolves it for us.** PS Market Research publishes the whole wound care market *and* states that **advanced is 60% of it in 2025**. Apply the publisher's own share to the publisher's own total:

```
$24.4B (PS Market Research, all wound care, 2025)  ×  60% (its own stated advanced share)
= $14.64B of advanced wound care in 2025

vs $13.37B  (MarketsandMarkets, advanced only, 2025)      = a 9.5% gap
```

**One house's whole-market figure multiplied by its own stated advanced share lands within 9.5% of another house's advanced-only figure.** Two independently constructed estimates of the same thing, agreeing to within a tenth. The 1.83x "spread" is the gauze — traditional dressings, bandages, tape, sponges and abdominal pads — plus wound closure, and both publishers are measuring consistently once you know which they are quoting.

**We publish the advanced reading and say so in the title and the first line.** The all-wound-care figure is a real quantity of roughly $24.4B for 2025, and the report states it, labelled, so that a reader who wanted that number does not have to find it elsewhere.

## Grounding arithmetic

Four companies disclose an advanced wound care revenue line or an equivalent whole-company figure. A fifth, Coloplast, discloses growth but not the dollar revenue of the relevant business, and a sixth, Mölnlycke, discloses only at whole-company level. And this market has something rarer than any of that: **a dated, numbered reimbursement price for one of its three product families**, set by CMS and effective inside our base year.

**Registers and disclosures used:** Smith & Nephew's FY2025 Form 20-F; Solventum's Q4 2025 results (8-K exhibit 99.1) and FY2025 Form 10-K; Convatec's FY25 results (24 February 2026); Mölnlycke's 2025 annual report; Coloplast's 2024/25 full-year results; the **CY2026 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule final rule** of 31 October 2025 and its November 2025 correcting amendment; and the **IDF Diabetes Atlas, 11th edition**, released 7 April 2025.

### Test 1 — four disclosures against each reading of the market

```
Sum of four disclosed advanced wound care revenue lines, 2025     $6,945M

÷ $13,370M  (MarketsandMarkets, advanced only, 2025)           =  51.9%
÷ $14,640M  (PS Market Research's own 60% of its own 2025 total) =  47.4%
÷ $24,400M  (PS Market Research, ALL wound care, 2025)         =  28.5%
÷ $14,390M  (our published 2026 base)                          =  48.3%
```

**Four companies are roughly half the advanced market and roughly a quarter of all wound care.** That is the correct shape for this industry, and it is the answer that confirms the published figures rather than breaking them — unlike every other market in this batch. The remainder is identifiable rather than mysterious: Coloplast's Wound & Tissue Repair business (growing 8% organically, revenue undisclosed), 3M's and Cardinal Health's remaining wound portfolios, Integra LifeSciences, Organogenesis, MiMedx, Essity/BSN Medical, Urgo, Hartmann, and the entire Chinese and Indian dressing supply base.

Note what the same sum does to the *all-wound-care* figure: at 28.5%, four of the largest advanced players account for well under a third of it, which is exactly what you would expect if roughly 40% of that total is gauze, tape and wound closure sold largely by other companies. **The two tests corroborate each other and corroborate PS Market Research's 60% share.**

*Caveat stated plainly:* Mölnlycke's €2,104M is whole-company revenue including Operating Room Solutions, so it is **wider** than advanced wound care and inflates the sum. Convatec's $753M includes the InnovaMatrix skin substitute business that is about to shrink by three quarters (below). Smith+Nephew's $1,934M is an inferred segment identification. Coloplast is missing from the sum entirely despite being a major player. The sum is a scale check, not an apportionment.

### Test 2 — per diabetic foot ulcer, against the IDF register

The **IDF Diabetes Atlas, 11th edition** (released 7 April 2025) gives **589 million adults aged 20–79 living with diabetes**, about one in nine adults, an **11.1%** adult prevalence rate, with **252 million undiagnosed**, **81% living in low- and middle-income countries**, over **3.4 million deaths a year**, and a projection of **853 million by 2050** (+46%). Global diabetes-related health expenditure passed **one trillion US dollars** for the first time in 2024.

Annual diabetic foot ulcer incidence is the contested part, and the notes record the contest rather than picking a number quietly:

| Measure | Figure | Source character |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual DFU incidence** | **1.9%–2.6%** | Most underlying studies conducted in the **1990s**, per a Dutch study that notes this. **Stale — flagged** |
| Lifetime DFU occurrence | **19%–34%** of people with diabetes | Narrative review |
| Lifetime occurrence, contested | A commentary notes the 34% figure was earlier estimated at **15%** and argues Scottish national data give a **past-or-present prevalence static at about 4.7%** of the diabetes population | A published objection; **the lifetime model is stated by that commentary to need validation against real-world data** |
| Lifetime prevalence, falling, Norway | **11.0% → 7.5% → 5.3%** across HUNT2, HUNT3 and HUNT4 (1995–2019) | Population surveys, one country |
| Five-year survival after ulcer onset | **50%–60%** | Narrative review |

Take the **low end** of the annual incidence range, because it is the figure least likely to flatter the market:

```
589M adults with diabetes (IDF, 2025)  ×  2.0% annual DFU incidence
= roughly 11.78M new diabetic foot ulcers a year

$6,945M  (four disclosed advanced wound care revenue lines)  ÷ 11.78M  =   $590 per DFU per year
$13,370M (MarketsandMarkets, whole advanced market, 2025)    ÷ 11.78M  = $1,135 per DFU per year
```

**About $1,135 of advanced wound care revenue per new diabetic foot ulcer a year — and that is before a single pressure injury, venous leg ulcer, burn or surgical wound is treated.** Diabetic foot ulcers are one of at least five wound types in every published segmentation of this market. Spread the same $13.37B across all of them and the per-wound figure falls well below $1,135.

That is a dressings-and-NPWT number. It is not a cost-of-care number: the total cost of treating a diabetic foot ulcer runs to many thousands of dollars per episode in hospital, clinician and amputation costs. **The test therefore rules out reading the published advanced wound care market as the cost of treating chronic wounds**, and rules it in as what it says it is — the products. A reader who sees "$13 billion" and imagines the money spent on chronic wound care is out by at least an order of magnitude in the other direction from the mistake most of these reports invite.

### Test 3 — the CMS price register, and the shock nobody's CAGR priced

This market has what almost none does: a **single published administered price**, for one of its three product families, dated inside our base year.

```
CY2026 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule final rule, issued 31 October 2025:
  payment for most skin substitutes                 $127.28 per square centimetre
  November 2025 correcting amendment                $127.14 per square centimetre
  effective                                         1 January 2026
```

CMS set a **single rate for most skin substitutes**, treating most products as **incident-to supplies**; **discarded quantities are no longer separately billable** and will not be reimbursed; products holding Section 351 biologics licences keep separate product-specific payment. CMS separately **withdrew its Local Coverage Determinations for skin substitutes on 24 December 2025**. (One billing guide still prints $127.28 for the physician fee schedule and $127.14 for OPPS, which conflicts with the correcting-amendment sources — **flagged; the Federal Register correcting document is the primary source and we did not read it directly.**)

Two companies have now put a number on what that does:

| Company | Disclosure | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Convatec** | InnovaMatrix revenue **$69M in 2025**, FY26 guided to **about $20M** — a **71% fall**, a headwind of roughly **2% of group revenue** in FY26 and about **3% in H1 2026**. CFO stated the new rate was about an **80% reduction** for InnovaMatrix. Recorded a **$72M non-cash impairment** of the Triad Life Sciences intangible | Priced |
| **Smith+Nephew** | Advanced Wound Bioactives full-year growth "impacted by a soft fourth quarter due to a strong comparator period from the launch of GRAFIX PLUS in Q4 2024 and **softness in skin substitutes ahead of the implementation of reimbursement changes in 2026**" | Named, not quantified |

```
Convatec InnovaMatrix:  $69M (2025)  →  ~$20M (FY26 guidance)  = -71%
```

**One company's skin substitute line is guided to fall 71% in the first year of our forecast, and the impairment is already booked.** None of the five published CAGRs in the estimates table — 4.8%, 5.6%, 5.6%, 6.6%, 7.6% — was written with this in it; MarketsandMarkets' and PS Market Research's pages carry no mention of it, Allied's base year is 2022 and SNS Insider's release is from 2024. Skin substitutes are one of the three product families in every published advanced segmentation, and the largest single administered price in the category just fell by roughly 80% for at least one product.

The report states this plainly as the reason to treat the upper half of the published CAGR range with suspicion in the near term, while noting the offsetting fact that CMS **withdrew** the restrictive Local Coverage Determinations, which widens access even as it cuts price — a volume-for-price trade whose net effect nobody has yet measured.

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope and plausibility tests. They establish that four disclosed advanced wound care revenue lines are 51.9% of the published advanced market and 28.5% of the published all-wound-care market; that one publisher's own 60% advanced share applied to its own total lands within 9.5% of another publisher's advanced-only figure; that the published advanced market works out at roughly $1,135 per new diabetic foot ulcer a year before any other wound type; and that one company's skin substitute revenue is guided to fall 71% on a dated CMS price change inside the base year. They do not establish the correct market size for either scope. Mölnlycke's figure is whole-company and wider than wound care; Smith+Nephew's segment identification is our inference from an unlabelled table; Coloplast is absent from the sum; the DFU incidence rate rests on studies mostly conducted in the 1990s and is actively contested in the literature at between 1.9% and 34% depending on whether annual or lifetime; the CMS rate conflict between $127.28 and $127.14 was not resolved against the Federal Register itself; and dividing company revenue by a third party's market total compares differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $14.39B (2026) → $22.33B (2032) at 7.6%, scoped explicitly to advanced wound care.**

Source: MarketsandMarkets, *Advanced Wound Care Market*, read from the publisher's own press-release page on **16 October 2026**, which states **$13.37B for 2025, $19.32B for 2030 and a 7.6% CAGR**.

The 2026 base is **derived by advancing that house's own published 2025 figure one year at its own stated rate**: 13.37 × 1.076 = **14.39**. The 2032 endpoint continues the same rate: 14.39 × 1.076^6 = **22.33**. Both are labelled as interpolation in the report. Internal check: the implied rate between our endpoints is **7.60%**, identical to the stated rate, and interpolating forward lands 2030 on **19.29** against MarketsandMarkets' published **19.32** — a 0.15% difference, which is as close as rounding allows.

Chosen because: (a) it is **advanced-only by construction** — its segmentation contains dressings, NPWT, debridement devices, biological skin substitutes and topical agents, and **no traditional category at all**, which is the scope question in this market; (b) it was read **directly from the publisher's own page**, as was the corroborating PS Market Research series, where Allied and SNS Insider came through search summaries and a newswire; (c) it carries a **stated base-year value**, which Meticulous and SNS Insider do not; and (d) PS Market Research's own 60% advanced share applied to its own 2026 total gives **$15.36B**, within **6.7%** of our $14.39B — two independently built estimates of the same scope agreeing inside 7%.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Medical Devices.

**We state the alternative reading rather than hiding it.** All wound care including traditional gauze and wound closure is **$24.4B for 2025 and $25.6B for 2026** on PS Market Research's own published figures, and the report says so, labelled as the whole category rather than the advanced one.

The report states plainly in `executive_summary`, `market_context`, `forecast_analysis` and `research_methodology` that four disclosed advanced wound care revenue lines sum to $6.95 billion and are 51.9% of the published advanced market; that one publisher's own advanced share reconciles the two readings to within 9.5%; that the market works out at roughly $1,135 per new diabetic foot ulcer a year before any other wound type; and that CMS's $127.14 per square centimetre skin substitute rate has already produced a guided 71% revenue fall at one supplier and a booked $72M impairment.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Four companies disclose a wound care revenue line; none discloses a share. The 51.9%, 48.3%, 28.5% and 47.4% ratios are **our own division** of disclosed revenue lines by third parties' published totals, stated in prose with both inputs named. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **A Coloplast Wound & Tissue Repair revenue figure.** Coloplast disclosed **+8% organic growth** for 2024/25 and a group total of DKK 27,874M, but the business-area revenue in DKK did not appear in the material we could read. **Flagged, and nothing is inferred.** Its absence from the four-company sum is stated.
- **A Mölnlycke wound care revenue figure.** €2,104M is **whole-company** revenue including Operating Room Solutions. Used as an over-inclusive proxy inside Test 1, labelled, and never published as a wound care figure.
- **Segment shares.** PS Market Research's **advanced = 60% of 2025** is quoted in prose as the bridge between the two readings, with the publisher named, because that is the scope finding. It is **not** reproduced in `market_segmentation`, which goes out as plain strings. Its "advanced grows at about 5.8%" is likewise quoted as a rate, not as a share.
- **Smith+Nephew's franchise split as full-year figures.** Q1 2025 gave Advanced Wound Care $173M, Bioactives $120M and Devices $92M against an AWM total of $385M. Those are **one quarter**, and we do not extrapolate them to the year. The franchise structure is described in words; no franchise-level annual figure is published.
- **Regional splits.** Both publishers name North America as largest in 2024; neither gives a numeric regional share of the global total. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`. `country_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **A diabetic foot ulcer count.** 11.78M rests on the **low end of a 1.9%–2.6% annual incidence range drawn mostly from 1990s studies**, applied to the IDF's 589 million. The literature's lifetime figures range from 4.7% to 34% and one published commentary disputes the high end outright. It is a labelled plausibility denominator and no ulcer count is published.
- **A per-wound or per-patient market size.** The $590 and $1,135 figures are ratios used to identify what the market measures. They are stated as such and never published as spend per patient.
- **A cost-of-care figure for chronic wounds.** Widely quoted in policy literature; not registered at a level we could check. The report says only that total cost of care is far above the product spend, without a number.
- **The $127.28 versus $127.14 skin substitute rate as settled.** Sources conflict and we did not read the Federal Register correcting document itself. Both figures are given with their dates and the conflict is stated. **Flagged.**
- **A net effect for the CMS change.** The price cut and the 24 December 2025 withdrawal of the Local Coverage Determinations push in opposite directions. Convatec's guided 71% fall is a disclosure and is reported as one; **no net market impact is estimated.**
- **Meticulous Research's $18.19B as a dated forecast.** The publisher gives the same figure for **2031 in its 2024 release and 2032 in a later one**. Recorded in the estimates table with the inconsistency named; used for range only.
- **Allied Market Research and SNS Insider as current estimates.** Allied's base year is 2022; SNS Insider's release is from 2024 and gives no base value. Both read from summaries. Range only.
