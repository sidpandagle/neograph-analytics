# Inpatient Rehabilitation Facilities — research notes
**Slug:** inpatient-rehabilitation-facilities-market   **Drafted:** 2026-10-18

Pipeline row 52, round 7A. Industry: Healthcare IT.

This market has an unusual shape: the **register is excellent and the published
literature is absent.** The United States measures the inpatient rehabilitation
facility in more detail than almost any other care setting — a statutory price
published annually in the Federal Register, a case-mix weight table with 94
groups, a facility census split by ownership and by freestanding-versus-unit, and
claims-derived spending, stays, occupancy, length of stay and margin published by
MedPAC each March. Against that, **no market-research publisher found in this
pass sizes the inpatient rehabilitation facility in dollars at all.** Several
size "medical rehabilitation services" or "post-acute care" and name IRF as a
segment; two publish an IRF segment growth rate; none publishes an IRF segment
value. That asymmetry is the finding, and this report is built around it rather
than around a citation.

## Scope boundary against `rehabilitation-robotics-market`

This corpus already carries a published report at slug
`rehabilitation-robotics-market` — headline $1.77 billion in 2026 to $4.56
billion in 2032 at 17.10%, on Mordor Intelligence's series. **That report is
devices: robots that assist, guide or measure patient movement.** This report is
the **facility and the service** — the licensed hospital or hospital-based unit,
its beds, its staff and the per-discharge payment it receives. The two do not
overlap and must not be summed. A rehabilitation robot is a capital purchase
*inside* an IRF; the IRF's revenue is the per-discharge payment for the stay.
At the 2026 base years the facility market constructed here is **13.4x** the
robotics market ($23.72B / $1.77B), and the robotics market grows at 17.10%
against 7.80% here, because one is an equipment adoption curve and the other is
a regulated payment stream.

## The spread

Three comparisons, kept apart because they answer different questions.

**1. Across the two category names at the 2026 base year — 6.36x.** The lowest
verified 2026 figure is Business Research Insights' USD 204.54 bn for the "global
rehabilitation market"; the highest is GMI's USD 1,300 bn for "global post-acute
care."

```
$1,300B / $204.54B = 6.356x
```

That is not a disagreement about rehabilitation. It is two different industries
sharing a shelf: one counts equipment and therapist labour, the other counts
every skilled nursing bed, home health visit and hospice day on earth.

**2. Within one category name at one base year — 1.48x.** Confining the
comparison to houses that both say "global rehabilitation / medical
rehabilitation services" and both publish a 2026 figure:

```
$303.40B (Grand View) / $204.54B (Business Research Insights) = 1.483x
```

**3. Against the thing this report is actually about — 12.79x to 54.81x.** The
United States all-payer IRF market constructed below is USD 23.72 bn for 2026.

```
$303.40B / $23.72B = 12.79x    Grand View's global medical rehabilitation services
$1,300B   / $23.72B = 54.81x   GMI's global post-acute care
$303.40B / $11.62B = 26.11x    against the measured FFS Medicare IRF tier alone
```

And in the other direction, against the one publisher who measures the same
country and the same parent category:

```
$23.72B / $513.9B = 4.62%    the IRF as a share of GMI's US post-acute care market, 2026
```

**Verdict: the spread here is not a growth disagreement and not even a scope
disagreement. It is an absence.** Published CAGRs for the parent categories run
6.3% to 7.5% — a 1.19x spread, tight by the standards of this corpus. The houses
agree on growth and agree roughly on the parent category's size. What none of
them does is divide it. A buyer who quotes "the rehabilitation market is USD
204.54 bn in 2026" and means inpatient rehabilitation facilities is off by
roughly 8.6x before any growth assumption is applied.

## Grounding arithmetic

### (a) The rule's own two tables reconcile exactly

This is the rare market where the unit price of the service is a published legal
fact. The FY2026 IRF PPS final rule (90 FR 37678, Vol. 90 No. 148, Tuesday 5
August 2025, effective 1 October 2025) sets, at Table 5 (90 FR 37695-37696):

```
FY 2025 IRF standard payment conversion factor            $18,907
x 1.026   market basket update for FY 2026
          (3.3% IRF market basket less 0.7 pt productivity adjustment)
          = $19,399
x 1.0001  budget neutrality factor, wage index and labor-related share (74.4%)
          = $19,401
x 0.9985  budget neutrality factor, revisions to the CMG relative weights
FY 2026 IRF standard payment conversion factor          = $19,371
```

Table 2 of the same rule publishes relative weights and average length of stay
for **94 case-mix groups** across four comorbidity tiers. The extremes, and what
Table 6 of the same rule pays for them:

```
19,371 x 0.7244 = $14,032.35   CMG 0201, no-comorbidity tier  (lowest non-expired weight)
19,371 x 4.5328 = $87,804.87   CMG 0407, Tier 1, traumatic spinal cord injury, ALOS 42 days
```

Both products equal the published Table 6 payment rates **to the cent**. The
lowest weight in the table is 0.1755 (CMG 5001, patient expired, orthopaedic,
stay of 13 days or fewer), which is a terminal-stay group rather than a course of
rehabilitation. Excluding the five expired groups, the rule's payment range
across case mix is:

```
$87,804.87 / $14,032.35 = 6.257x
```

**What it rules in:** the price side of this market is not an estimate. It is a
table, and the table is internally consistent. Anyone quoting an IRF market size
can be asked which case mix they assumed, and the answer has a 6.26x range.

### (b) Realised FFS Medicare payment per stay against the statutory rate

Now the volume denominator, all from MedPAC's March 2026 Chapter 9 for FY2024:
USD 11.0 billion of FFS Medicare spending on IRF services (USD 10.8 bn program
plus USD 0.2 bn beneficiary cost-sharing), on about **435,000** FFS
Medicare-covered stays, in about **1,170** IRFs (1,169 exactly, Table 9-1).

```
$11,000,000,000 / 435,000 stays = $25,287.36 per FFS Medicare stay
$25,287.36 / $19,371 (FY2026 conversion factor at CMG weight 1.0) = 1.3054
```

**What it rules in:** USD 11.0 bn is the right order of magnitude for the service
it claims to describe. A realised payment 1.31x the weight-1.0 conversion factor
is what an average case-mix index above 1.0 plus wage-index uplift, the
low-income-patient adjustment, teaching and rural adjustments and high-cost
outlier payments would produce. Had the division returned $5,000 or $200,000 one
of the two series would be wrong.

**Caveat stated plainly:** the spending is FY2024 and the conversion factor is
FY2026, so the comparison crosses two payment updates — the 3.0% FY2025
regulatory update (3.5% market basket less 0.5 pt productivity) and the 2.6%
FY2026 update. The bias runs one way: the rates actually in force in FY2024 were
lower than $19,371, so the true multiple against FY2024 rates is **larger** than
1.3054, not smaller. The 435,000 is MedPAC's own rounding, so the per-stay result
carries about three significant figures of denominator uncertainty.

### (c) The headline construction, and a second method that agrees within 3.92%

No publisher sizes this market, so the headline is **constructed**, not cited,
and every step uses MedPAC's own figures and MedPAC's own stated rates.

MedPAC endnote 3 of Chapter 9: among freestanding IRFs in FY2024, **about 49
percent of all payments were for FFS Medicare patients.** (MedPAC states the same
share could not be calculated for hospital-based IRFs because of cost-report data
limitations; applying 49% nationally is therefore an assumption, and it is
labelled as one on the page.)

```
Step 1   $11.0B          FFS Medicare IRF spending, FY2024                 (MedPAC, measured)
Step 2   / 0.49          FFS Medicare share of all payments, freestanding  (MedPAC, measured)
         = $22.449B      implied US all-payer IRF revenue, FY2024
Step 3   x 1.030         MedPAC's stated FY2025 regulatory update
         = $23.1225B
Step 4   x 1.026         MedPAC's stated FY2026 regulatory update
         = $23.7236B  ->  $23.72B, United States all-payer IRF, FY2026
```

Second method, from the stay side, using MedPAC's other published share — FFS
Medicare beneficiaries accounted for about **51 percent of IRF stays** in FY2024:

```
435,000 / 0.51 = 852,941 all-payer IRF stays, FY2024
852,941 x $25,287.36 = $21.5686B implied US all-payer IRF revenue, FY2024
x 1.030 x 1.026 = $22.7933B  ->  $22.79B for FY2026

($22.449B - $21.5686B) / $22.449B = 3.92% divergence between the two methods
```

**What it rules in:** two ratios MedPAC publishes for different reasons — a
payments share from cost reports and a stays share from claims — gross the same
measured USD 11.0 bn up to within 3.92% of each other. That is a real
corroboration, not a restatement, and it is why USD 22.45 bn is usable as a
FY2024 all-payer figure.

**What the construction is NOT.** Steps 3 and 4 advance the figure at the
**price** updates only. MedPAC also reports that FFS IRF stays rose **7.6%** in
FY2024 and stays per 10,000 FFS beneficiaries rose **9.7%** to 132. Volume
growth of that order is excluded, so USD 23.72 bn for 2026 is a **floor**, not a
central estimate. The direction of the error is known; its magnitude is not
claimed. This is stated in `forecast_analysis` and in a FAQ.

### (d) The construction against two operators' disclosed revenue per day

Independent instrument: SEC-filed operator statistics, which have nothing to do
with MedPAC's cost reports or with any market-research house.

MedPAC endnote 5: in FY2024 there were about **23,000** beds in freestanding IRFs
and about **19,000** in hospital-based IRFs — about **42,000** IRF beds
nationally — at an aggregate occupancy rate of **71 percent** (freestanding 74%,
hospital-based 67%).

```
42,000 beds x 0.71 occupancy x 365 days = 10,884,300 occupied bed days, FY2024
$22,449,000,000 / 10,884,300 = $2,062.51 per occupied bed day
```

Now the two listed operators, FY2025:

```
Encompass Health:  $5,756,300,000 inpatient revenue / 263,299 discharges = $21,862.20 per discharge
                   $5,756,300,000 / (263,299 x 12.1 days ALOS)           =  $1,806.79 per patient day
Select Medical:    $1,288,954,000 rehabilitation hospital segment revenue
                   / 36,787 admissions                                   = $35,038 per admission
                   disclosed revenue per patient day                     =  $2,260
                   510,127 patient days / 36,787 admissions              = 13.87 days ALOS
```

**The national construction sits between the two operators: $1,806.79 <
$2,062.51 < $2,260.** That is the cleanest available demonstration that USD
22.45 bn is the right size for US all-payer IRF revenue. If the construction were
wrong by a factor, it would fall outside a range set by the two largest
disclosed books in the sector, and it does not.

The two operators also explain each other. Encompass's 11,465 licensed beds at
75.9% occupancy and 12.1 days are a high-volume, stroke-and-orthopaedics book
with Medicare at 65.4% of revenue. Select Medical's rehabilitation hospital
segment runs 13.87 days at $2,260 a day — **25.1% more revenue per day than
Encompass** — because the segment carries catastrophic and neuro-transitional
cases. Same payment system, different case mix, and the 6.26x range in section
(a) is what makes that possible.

### (e) Three independent denominators agree on how concentrated this sector is

MedPAC states that in calendar year 2024 Encompass Health "owned or operated
about 42 percent of freestanding IRFs and accounted for about 34 percent of all
FFS Medicare IRF stays." Check that against the company's own filings:

```
166 Encompass hospitals (31 Dec 2024) / 392 freestanding IRFs (FY2024, MedPAC Table 9-1) = 42.3%
```

MedPAC's 42% reproduces exactly from the company's own hospital count and the
federal facility census. Then the same company against three different
denominators:

```
11,465 licensed beds (31 Dec 2025) / ~42,000 national IRF beds        = 27.3%   of beds
263,299 discharges (FY2025) / 852,941 all-payer stays (FY2024)        = 30.9%   of stays
$5,935.2m net operating revenue (FY2025) / $23.72B (2026 construction) = 25.0%  of revenue
```

Adding Select Medical's rehabilitation hospital segment:

```
($5,935.2m + $1,288.954m) / $23.72B = 30.5% of the US all-payer IRF market
```

**What it rules in:** one operator is a quarter to a third of this market on
every denominator available, and the two listed operators together are
approaching a third. **What it rules out:** any reading in which the IRF sector
resembles the fragmented skilled nursing sector. It also rules out a vendor share
table — see "Do not publish" — because the 25.0% and 30.5% above are ratios of a
*constructed* denominator to disclosed revenue, not measured shares, and the
denominators disagree with each other by 5.9 points.

### (f) The 60 percent rule is a boundary condition on the register itself

Verified from MedPAC Chapter 9 and its endnote 4 rather than assumed. The
compliance threshold requires that **no less than 60 percent of patients admitted
to an IRF required intensive rehabilitation services for treatment of one or more
of 13 specified conditions**, as either principal diagnosis or comorbidity. The
13: stroke; spinal cord injury; congenital deformity; amputation; major multiple
trauma; hip fracture; brain injury; certain other neurological conditions;
burns; three arthritis conditions that have not improved after outpatient or less
intensive therapy; and hip or knee replacement only when bilateral, or BMI >= 50,
or the patient is aged 85 or older. MedPAC states the threshold's intent plainly:
to distinguish IRFs from acute care hospitals. **If an IRF fails it, Medicare pays
for all its patients' stays under the inpatient hospital PPS rather than the IRF
PPS** — the facility does not merely lose a bonus, it leaves this market.

So the 1,169-facility register is not a list of hospitals that do
rehabilitation. It is a list of hospitals whose case mix passes a federal test
every year. MedPAC's FY2024 case mix shows how close the binding constraint runs:
the three most common admitting conditions were stroke (15.1% of stays), other
neurological conditions (15.0%) and **debility (14.7%)** — and debility,
generalised deconditioning not attributable to another condition, **is not one of
the 13**. Nearly 15% of IRF volume sits outside the qualifying list by
construction.

### (g) The register's own trend, against the published growth rates

From MedPAC's March 2026 Chapter 9, FY2024 unless stated:

- **1,169 IRFs**, up less than 1% on FY2023 (1,160). Table 9-1: 1,119 (2019),
  1,114 (2020), 1,118 (2021), 1,132 (2022), 1,160 (2023), 1,169 (2024); average
  annual change 0.9% 2019-2023, 0.8% 2023-2024.
- **392 freestanding** (+4.3%) and **777 hospital-based** (-0.9%). **66 percent of
  IRFs are hospital-based**, but freestanding IRFs take **64 percent of FFS
  stays** and hospital-based 36%. Ownership: **552 for-profit** (+4.3%), **516
  nonprofit** (-2.8%), **101 government** (+1.0%). Geography: 1,025 urban (+0.9%),
  144 rural (0.0%); 88% of IRFs are urban.
- **Bed size is the sector's fault line.** 95% of freestanding IRFs have 25 or
  more beds; 67% of hospital-based IRFs have fewer than 25.
- **Occupancy 71%** (69% in FY2023), freestanding 74%, hospital-based 67%. Beds
  grew about 3.5% in FY2024, driven by freestanding for-profit beds.
- **435,000 FFS stays** (+7.6%); **132 stays per 10,000 FFS beneficiaries**
  (+9.7%) against 107 in 2019; ALOS **12.4 days** (12.6 in 2019). Stays per capita
  rose 14% at freestanding IRFs against 3% at hospital-based.
- **FFS Medicare margin 17.1%** (14.8% in FY2023), **projected about 18% for
  FY2026**. Freestanding **25.0%**, hospital-based **4.1%**; for-profit **24.6%**,
  nonprofit **2.9%**; IRFs with fewer than 10 beds **-4.9%**. Facility at the 25th
  percentile **-6.3%**, at the 75th **24.9%**.
- Payments per stay rose **3.4%** in FY2024 while costs per stay rose **0.6%**.
  Average cost per stay **$25,000** hospital-based against **$18,000**
  freestanding — a ratio of **1.389**.
- All-payer total margin for freestanding IRFs **11.6%** (10.4% in FY2023);
  for-profit freestanding about 14%, nonprofit freestanding about 4%.
- Hospital-based IRFs took 36% of FFS stays but **78% of high-cost outlier stays**.
- Quality, FY2023-FY2024 combined: median risk-adjusted successful discharge to
  community **67.5%** (67.2% in the prior period), interquartile 64.3% to 70.6%;
  median potentially preventable readmissions **9.2%** (8.8% prior), interquartile
  8.7% to 9.8%.
- Access is geographically thin: in 2022 **fewer than 30 percent of hospital
  service areas had one or more IRFs** against 97% for SNFs, and only about
  **30 percent of FFS Medicare beneficiaries lived in an HSA with an IRF**.
- **MedPAC recommends Congress reduce the 2026 IRF base payment rate by 7 percent
  for FY2027**, 17 votes to nil — the same recommendation as the prior year. It
  estimates the cut would lower spending by **USD 2 billion to USD 5 billion in
  one year** and **USD 10 billion to USD 25 billion over five years**.

A register with a 17.1% payment margin, 71% occupancy, a recommended 7% rate cut
and a facility count growing under 1% a year does not obviously support 7.8%
compound revenue growth. What it does support is growth in *spending* through
volume: stays per capita up 9.7% in a single year, over 12% growth in other
neurological and non-traumatic brain injury rehabilitation, and capacity added by
for-profit operators into markets that already had an IRF. That is the honest
mechanism, and it is a volume and case-mix mechanism, not a price one — the price
updates were 3.0% and 2.6%, and MedPAC is asking Congress to make the next one
negative.

## What we publish

**Headline: USD 23.72 bn (2026) → USD 37.22 bn (2032) at 7.80%, United States,
all-payer inpatient rehabilitation facility revenue.**

- The **base year is a construction, not a citation**, and the report says so in
  the summary, the executive summary, the forecast analysis, the methodology and
  a FAQ. It is MedPAC's measured USD 11.0 bn of FY2024 FFS Medicare IRF spending,
  grossed to all-payer by MedPAC's own published 49%-of-payments statistic, and
  advanced at MedPAC's own two stated regulatory updates (3.0% FY2025, 2.6%
  FY2026). Section (c) prints every step; section (c)'s second method agrees
  within 3.92%; section (d) places the result between two operators' disclosed
  revenue per patient day.
- The **growth rate is a citation**: 7.8%, Global Market Insights report GMI5203,
  *U.S. Post-acute Care Market*, published January 2026, stated for the IRF
  service segment over the report's 2026-2035 forecast period. It is the only
  published IRF-specific growth rate on a United States, all-payer scope that
  this pass could find. Using it over 2026-2032 truncates GMI's own period; it
  does not alter the rate.
- **The two halves come from different publishers and the report says so.** A
  constructed base at a cited rate is weaker than a single house's series, and
  pretending otherwise would be the dishonest move here. The alternative was to
  publish Grand View's USD 303.4 bn global medical rehabilitation services figure
  under an IRF title, which would overstate this market by **12.79x**.
- `market_forecast` interpolates geometrically at 7.80%: 23.72, 25.57, 27.56,
  29.71, 32.03, 34.53, 37.22. The intermediate years are interpolation, not
  separately sourced observations, and the chart title says so. Recomputed from
  the endpoints the series gives **7.7979%**, 0.0021 points from the stated rate;
  year-on-year rates span 0.026 points.
- The **companion figure** published alongside it is the measured FFS Medicare
  tier: **USD 11.62 bn for FY2026** (USD 11.0 bn x 1.030 x 1.026), which is the
  part of this market that is measured rather than constructed, and is **49.0%**
  of the headline by construction.

Everything else in the JSON is a register figure, a Federal Register figure or an
SEC-filed operator disclosure, each traceable to a numbered source below.

## Do not publish

- **No IRF market size attributed to any market-research publisher.** Five
  searches across roughly twenty houses found none. Two publish an IRF segment
  CAGR with no value; the rest do not segment by setting at all, or segment
  "inpatient / outpatient" without naming the facility type. Attributing a
  dollar figure to a house that did not publish one is the single most tempting
  move in this market and it is refused outright.
- **`regional_analysis`: empty.** The headline is United States only and is
  built from US federal instruments; there is no global IRF model to split. GMI
  publishes North America at 43.2% of its global *post-acute care* market and
  Europe at USD 359.5 bn, but those are a different category and splitting a
  different market would imply a global IRF model that does not exist in public.
- **`country_analysis`: empty.** Same reason. Outside the United States the IRF
  is not even a defined payment category — there is no equivalent of the 60
  percent rule, the CMG table or the IRF-PAI — so a country table would be
  comparing a regulated class against loosely similar hospital departments.
- **No `market_share` anywhere.** Section (e) computes 25.0%, 27.3%, 30.9% and
  42.3% for Encompass Health against four different denominators. Those spread
  17 points, three of the four denominators are constructed, and MedPAC's own
  42%-of-freestanding and 34%-of-FFS-stays are facility and stay counts rather
  than revenue. Encompass's position is stated in words and the figures are given
  as what they are — counts and ratios against named denominators — never as a
  revenue share.
- **The outlier threshold amount.** The FY2026 final rule states it twice and
  disagrees with itself: section V gives "$12,043 for FY 2025 to $11,971 for FY
  2026" while the regulatory impact analysis gives "from $12,043 in FY 2025 to
  $10,062 in FY 2026." Until the discrepancy is resolved against the CMS addenda,
  neither figure is published.
- **The USD 1.3 trillion and USD 303.4 bn figures as this market's size.** Both
  are published and both are cited in the report — as the parent categories they
  are, with the multiples printed. Neither is offered as an IRF figure.
- **Every row of the unverified table above.** Including Allied Market Research's
  statement that inpatient rehabilitation services were more than half of 2022
  medical rehabilitation services revenue, which is the single most useful
  unverified claim in this pass: multiplied against any 2026 parent figure it
  would produce an IRF number an order of magnitude above the register. The
  publisher page returned HTTP 403, the edition is 2023, and the claim is not
  used.
- **Astute Analytica's series as printed.** USD 315.6 m for 2023 is roughly 1/35th
  of US FFS Medicare's IRF spending alone, which the register rules out
  absolutely. Recorded as evidence that unit errors survive in this category;
  not restated as billions.
- **Per-bed-day and per-discharge figures as market estimates.** Sections (b),
  (d) and (e) are plausibility tests computed from published totals and published
  denominators. They establish what the figures cannot mean. They are not market
  estimates and `data_validation` says so.
- **Volume growth inside the base year.** Steps 3 and 4 of the construction
  advance at price only, although MedPAC reports stays up 7.6% in FY2024. The
  omission makes the base a floor and is stated rather than quietly corrected,
  because advancing at an observed one-year volume change would be this report's
  own assumption rather than any publisher's.
- **Any claim that the 8.5% global IRF CAGR and the 7.8% US IRF CAGR describe the
  same thing.** Both are GMI's, from two reports a month apart, and the report
  cites the US one because the headline is a US figure. The 70-basis-point gap is
  noted, not reconciled.
