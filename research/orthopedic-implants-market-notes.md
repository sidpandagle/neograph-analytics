## The spread

**At the 2025/2026 base, $28.45B (360iResearch) to $61.61B (HNY Research) — a 2.17x spread.** At the far end, $40.33B (2032) to $77.81B (2031).

Published CAGRs run **3.97% to 5.8%, a 1.46x spread.** Once again the growth rates nearly agree and the bases do not. The disagreement is a definition, and in this market the definition has three moving parts, each worth billions:

1. **Spinal implants.** Persistence names spinal implants as a product type. A reconstructive-only reading excludes them. Medtronic alone disclosed **$4,973M** of Cranial & Spinal Technologies revenue for its fiscal 2025 — more than a sixth of 360iResearch's entire global market on its own.
2. **Trauma fixation and CMF.** Persistence includes both. These are the largest single line in two of the five big disclosures: Stryker's Trauma and Extremities was **$3,948M** in 2025 and J&J's Trauma was **$3,146M**.
3. **Dental.** Not in Persistence's scope and not visibly in anyone else's, but it is the most common silent inclusion in "implants" categories and is worth asking about before any two figures in this market are compared.

So the readings are: **the whole skeletal fixation and replacement business — joints, spine, trauma, CMF** — versus **reconstructive joint replacement alone**. The arithmetic below settles which of those the low estimate can possibly be, because the companies that disclose are enough on their own to exceed it.

## Grounding arithmetic

This is the best-disclosed market in the batch. **Five companies disclose orthopaedic segment revenue outright**, and one of them — Zimmer Biomet — is essentially nothing but this market, which makes its whole-company revenue a hard floor on any definition that includes knees and hips.

**Registers and disclosures used:** Stryker's Q4/FY2025 results (8-K exhibit 99.1, filed with the SEC); Zimmer Biomet's Q4/FY2025 results and FY2025 Form 10-K; Johnson & Johnson's Q4/FY2025 results; Smith+Nephew's FY2025 Form 20-F; Medtronic's Q4 FY2025 results (8-K exhibit 99.1, filed with the SEC); the **OECD's Health at a Glance 2025** hip and knee replacement rates; and the American Joint Replacement Registry's 2025 Annual Report.

### Test 1 — five disclosures against each published market

```
Sum of five disclosed orthopaedic revenue lines, 2025            $34,369M

÷ $48,400M  (Persistence, 2026, joints + spine + trauma + CMF)  =  71.0%
÷ $61,610M  (HNY Research, 2025, widest base)                   =  55.8%
÷ $28,450M  (360iResearch, 2025, narrowest base)                = 120.8%
```

**Five companies disclose 21% more orthopaedic revenue than 360iResearch's entire published global market contains.** And those five are not the industry: Globus Medical (post-NuVasive), Arthrex, Medacta, Corin, B.Braun Aesculap, Orthofix/SeaSpine, Enovis/DJO and the entire Chinese and Indian domestic supply base are all outside the sum.

The floor can be pushed further down, because Zimmer Biomet is an orthopaedics pure-play and its whole-company revenue is therefore a lower bound on the market:

```
Zimmer Biomet, whole company, FY2025               $8,232M
÷ $28,450M  (360iResearch, whole global market)  =  28.9%

Stryker Orthopaedics + Zimmer Biomet              $17,701M
÷ $28,450M                                       =  62.2%
```

**Two companies are 62.2% of that published global market.** In an industry that also contains J&J, Smith+Nephew, Medtronic and a dozen named mid-caps, that is not possible. The $28.45B series cannot be the whole orthopedic implant market; on the composition of the disclosures it is at most a **reconstructive joint replacement** figure, and the page does not say so.

Against Persistence's $48.4B, the same five disclosures are 71.0% — a high but entirely plausible concentration for an industry whose top five are known to dominate it, with roughly $14B left for everybody else. That is the reading we publish.

### Test 2 — revenue per procedure, against the OECD register

The denominator here is a published official statistic rather than a vendor estimate. **OECD, Health at a Glance 2025 (2023 data): an OECD average of 198 hip replacements and 156 knee replacements per 100,000 population**, a combined 354 per 100,000. Germany (about 351 hip replacements per 100,000) and Switzerland (about 349) are the highest; Mexico, Colombia and Costa Rica the lowest.

Take only the knee and hip implant lines the four reconstructive players disclose, so that the numerator and the denominator describe the same procedures:

```
Stryker        Knees $2,656M  + Hips $1,865M      = $4,521M
Zimmer Biomet  Knees $3,322M  + Hips $2,094M      = $5,416M
J&J            Knees $1,587M  + Hips $1,674M      = $3,261M
Smith+Nephew   Knees $1,011M  + Hips   $641M      = $1,652M
-------------------------------------------------------------
Disclosed knee and hip implant revenue, four firms  $14,850M
```

```
354 procedures per 100,000  ×  approximately 1.38 billion OECD population
                                                 = roughly 4.885M hip and knee replacements a year

$14,850M ÷ 4.885M procedures = $3,040 of implant revenue per procedure
```

**About $3,040 per hip or knee replacement.** Set that against the only measured per-case implant cost we could find: a study of 10,155 unilateral primary total knee replacements at 61 US hospitals found average implant cost per case ranging from **$1,797 to $12,093**, with 61.0% of the variation attributable to hospital characteristics rather than to the patient. (That study's data are from **2008** and are therefore stale and US-only — **flagged**; a consumer cost guide's $4,000–$12,000 range for the prosthesis itself is recorded for order only and **not used**.)

$3,040 sits at the low end of that measured range, which is the correct place for it to sit, for three reasons that all push the same way: the numerator is **manufacturer-gate** revenue while the hospital figure includes distributor margin; the four firms are **not** the whole knee and hip market, so the true numerator is larger; and the denominator includes OECD volume served by suppliers outside the four. The test therefore **rules in** the broad published readings as being in the right order of magnitude for a market in which reconstructive joints are the largest piece, and it rules out any reading on which an implant construct costs tens of thousands of dollars.

*Caveat stated plainly:* OECD rates of this kind are normally unweighted averages across member countries, so multiplying one by total OECD population is an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a procedure count. The **1.38 billion OECD population is our own sum of member populations and is not the OECD's own figure — flagged.** The product is used as a scale denominator only and no procedure count is published from it.

### Test 3 — the registry, as a reality check on the denominator

The American Joint Replacement Registry's **2025 Annual Report** analysed **4,434,668 validated hip and knee arthroplasty procedures** collected from 2012 through 2024, from all 50 states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico, contributed by over 5,000 surgeons and 1,200 institutions. That is up from **3,715,320** in the 2024 Annual Report — an increment of **719,348 validated procedures in one edition**. The report's executive summary separately describes 4.6 million *recorded* primary and revision procedures against about 4.3 million in the prior edition.

```
719,348 validated US procedures added in one edition
vs roughly 4.885M OECD-wide procedures a year implied by the OECD rates
= 14.7%
```

AJRR does not claim to capture all US procedures, so 719,348 is a capture figure, not a national count, and the two numbers are not directly comparable — the registry is a **floor** and the OECD product is an **estimate**. They are recorded together because they are the same order of magnitude once the registry's partial capture and US-only scope are accounted for, which is all the confirmation the per-procedure test needs. **No US or global procedure count is published from either.**

**What these tests are and are not.** They are scope and plausibility tests. They establish that five disclosed orthopaedic revenue lines sum to 120.8% of one published global market and 71.0% of another; that two companies alone are 62.2% of the smaller one; and that the disclosed knee and hip revenue divided by OECD-implied procedure volume gives about $3,040 per procedure, inside a measured per-case implant cost range. They do not establish the correct market size for either scope. Medtronic's fiscal year is not coterminous with the other four; Stryker's Orthopaedics total includes a Trauma and Extremities line far larger than its joint lines; Zimmer Biomet's figure is whole-company revenue rather than an implant-only line; the OECD population is our own sum; the per-case implant cost comparator is from 2008; and dividing company revenue by a third party's market total compares differently constructed numbers.

## What we publish

**Headline: $48.4B (2026) → $70.9B (2033) at 5.6%.**

Source: Persistence Market Research, *Orthopedic Implants Market*, page read from the publisher's own site on **16 October 2026**, which publishes **$48.4B for 2026, $70.9B for 2033, a 5.6% CAGR for 2026–2033** and a historical $37.3B for 2020 at a 4.4% historical rate.

Both endpoints are **the publisher's own published figures**, not derivations — this is the only series in the batch where that is true of both ends. Internal check: 48.4 × 1.056^7 = **70.88** against its published **70.9**, an implied rate of 5.61% against a stated 5.6%. The intermediate years in `market_forecast` are interpolation at the stated rate and are labelled as such.

Chosen because: (a) it is the **only source in the set read directly from the publisher's own page** — every other figure came through a search summary, one publisher page 403'd, and one attribution could not be verified at all; (b) it is the only one with a **fully stated product scope**, naming reconstructive joints, spinal implants, trauma fixation and CMF, which is precisely the decision that moves this market's base by 2.17x; (c) it publishes a **dated historical value** ($37.3B for 2020) so its trajectory is checkable rather than asserted; and (d) at $48.4B the five disclosed revenue lines are 71.0% of it, a high but possible concentration, where at $28.45B they are 120.8% of it, which is not possible.

Unit: USD Billion. Industry: Medical Devices.

The report states plainly in `executive_summary`, `market_context`, `forecast_analysis` and `research_methodology` that five disclosed orthopaedic revenue lines sum to $34.37 billion and therefore exceed the narrowest published global market by 21%; that Stryker Orthopaedics and Zimmer Biomet alone are 62.2% of that market; that Zimmer Biomet is an orthopaedics pure-play and its whole-company revenue is a floor on any definition including knees and hips; and that disclosed knee and hip implant revenue works out at about $3,040 per OECD hip or knee replacement.

## Do not publish

- **Vendor market share.** Five companies disclose segment revenue; none discloses a share. The 71.0%, 120.8%, 62.2% and 28.9% ratios are **our own division** of disclosed revenue lines by third parties' published totals, stated in prose with both inputs named. `competitive_landscape[].market_share` omitted entirely.
- **Regional splits.** Persistence covers North America, Europe and Asia Pacific with country detail for the US, Canada, Germany, UK, China and Japan, and gives an 8.9% Asia Pacific rate — but **no numeric regional share of the global total**, which is what a regional table would need. `regional_analysis` stays `[]`.
- **Country splits.** None published. `country_analysis` stays `[]`. Germany's and Switzerland's OECD hip replacement rates (about 351 and 349 per 100,000) are procedure rates, not market splits, and are used as such.
- **A knee or hip sub-market size.** The $14,850M of disclosed knee and hip implant revenue is four companies' revenue, not a market, and the four are not the whole segment. It is a numerator in Test 2 and is never published as a market size.
- **A procedure count, US or global.** The 4.885M OECD figure rests on our own population sum against an average that is probably unweighted; AJRR's 719,348 is a one-edition capture increment from a registry that does not claim complete coverage. Both are labelled plausibility inputs. Neither is published as a count.
- **An implant average selling price.** The only measured per-case implant cost we found is from 2008 and ranges 6.7x within itself ($1,797 to $12,093). It is used as a range comparator, labelled and dated, and no ASP is published.
- **360iResearch's $28.45B as the orthopedic implants market.** It is in the estimates table, and the report describes it as a figure the disclosed record exceeds by 21% — on the composition of those disclosures, at most a reconstructive-joints-only reading. Not used for the headline, and the report says why in terms a reader can check.
- **The Fortune Business Insights attribution.** The URL we tried resolved to an unrelated report, so we could not confirm the publisher published those figures at all. Recorded in the table, heavily flagged, used for nothing.
- **Allied Market Research's figures as a current estimate.** Its base year is 2022 and its own press-release page returned HTTP 403. Recorded for range only.
- **The "$53.35 million / $83.76 million" series.** A units error for billions. Recorded only as evidence that these listings are not proofread.
- **Any dental implant revenue.** No source in the set states whether dental is in or out, except Persistence by omission. We publish the Persistence scope and name dental as the question to ask, rather than guessing at a number.
- **A China volume-based procurement price-cut figure.** J&J cites China VBP as a pressure on its orthopaedics platforms, and the effect is real and disclosed qualitatively, but no percentage we could source to a primary Chinese procurement notice. Named as a restraint; not quantified.
