# Hospice Care Market — research notes
**Slug:** hospice-care-market   **Drafted:** 2026-10-18

Pipeline row 46, round 6A. Industry: Healthcare IT.

This market is unusually checkable because the unit price is set by statute. CMS
publishes four prospective per-diem rates each fiscal year, MedPAC publishes the
total Medicare spend and the total number of patient-days, and two public
companies disclose their own revenue per patient-day. That means a published
market size can be divided against a real denominator rather than assessed by
feel. The exercise below does that, and it eliminates most of the published
literature.

## The spread

**On the base year the published estimates spread 25.4x. On the growth rate they
spread 2.48x.** The disagreement is therefore about **scope**, not growth.

- 2026 base values run from **$5.93B to $150.40B** — `150.40 / 5.93 = 25.4x`.
  Both of those figures are published by the **same house**, Research and
  Markets, in two different reports. One publisher, one base year, 25x apart.
- On 2025 bases: `$90.63B / $5.31B = 17.1x`.
- CAGRs run from **4.71% to 11.7%** — `11.7 / 4.71 = 2.48x`, and even that
  2.48x is itself a scope artefact: the low rates belong to the US-only reports
  and the high rates to the global and the sub-$10B reports.

### The tiers, named

The grounding arithmetic in the next section places each estimate in one of four
tiers. The tier, not the growth assumption, is what a quoted headline is
reporting.

**Tier 1 — $4.7B to $9.1B (Data Bridge; Research and Markets / TBRC).** Cannot
be hospice care delivery under any geography. US Medicare fee-for-service hospice
spending alone was $28.3B in 2024, i.e. `28.3 / 5.31 = 5.33x` larger than the
whole of Tier 1's 2025 world market. Whatever Tier 1 counts, it is not the
delivery of hospice care; the most likely readings are a software, staffing or
single-sub-segment definition, or a definitional error carried between editions.
**Unusable as a market size.**

**Tier 2 — $28B to $33B (Grand View $32.60B 2026; Precedence $32.72B 2026;
Reports and Data $32.05B; Dataintelo $28.00B).** US hospice care delivery. Grand
View and Precedence are **0.4% apart** on 2026 (`32.72 / 32.60 = 1.0037`) despite
being independent houses with different forecast horizons, and both reconcile
against MedPAC's $28.3B of 2024 Medicare FFS spending once two years of growth
and non-FFS payers are allowed for. Note that Reports and Data and Dataintelo
label their figures *global* at this level, which is not reconcilable with a
US-only number of the same size — the value is right for the US and wrong for
the world.

**Tier 3 — $44B to $91B (Fortune $47.25B 2026; Mordor $88.31B 2026;
Market Data Forecast $90.63B; SNS Insider $89.04B).** Global hospice on a broad
definition. Internally inconsistent in the one case where the components are
published: Fortune's North America component for 2025, $27.85B, is **1.6% below**
US Medicare FFS hospice spending for 2024 alone (`27.85 / 28.30 = 0.984`), before
any allowance for Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, private pay, or Canada. The
$81-91B figures require a rest-of-world hospice spend of roughly
`81.75 − 31.2 = $50.6B`, i.e. **61.8% of global** spending outside North America,
in a world where the US puts 52.9% of its decedents through a statutory hospice
benefit and most countries have no comparable one. No public register supports
that residual.

**Tier 4 — $150B+ (Research and Markets, broad).** Sits inside the range of
published **palliative care** market estimates ($138.83B for 2024 per Precedence;
$243.2B by 2030 per Grand View), which is a materially wider category than the
hospice benefit. A $150B 2026 "hospice" figure is a palliative-care-scope figure
wearing a hospice label.

So: a **scope disagreement**, four tiers, and only Tier 2 survives division
against a public register.

## Grounding arithmetic

Three independent divisions. The first is the headline one; it works because the
denominator is published by MedPAC and the unit price is set by statute.

### 1. Statutory per-diem vs. measured spend per patient-day

Countable register (MedPAC, March 2026 Report to the Congress, Chapter 10, Table
10-3, for calendar 2024):

- Medicare hospice expenditures: **$28.3 billion**
- Hospice days furnished, all hospice beneficiaries: **148.2 million**
- Medicare hospice users: **1.82 million**
- Medicare-certified hospices (Table 10-1): **6,706**

Statutory FY2026 per-diem rates (CMS FY2026 Hospice Wage Index and Payment Rate
Update final rule, CMS-1835-F, published 5 August 2025; effective 1 October 2025
to 30 September 2026), for hospices that submit required quality data:

| Level of care | FY2026 rate |
|---|---|
| Routine Home Care, days 1-60 | **$230.83** /day (from $224.62 in FY2025) |
| Routine Home Care, days 61+ | **$181.94** /day (from $176.92) |
| Continuous Home Care | **$1,674.29** /24h = **$69.76** /hour |
| Inpatient Respite Care | **$532.48** /day |
| General Inpatient Care | **$1,199.86** /day |
| Aggregate cap per beneficiary | **$35,361.44** (FY2025: $34,465.34) |

Payment update 2.6% = 3.3% market basket − 0.7pp productivity adjustment; about
$750m more than FY2025 in aggregate. Non-submitters take a 4.0pp reduction.

**The division:**

```
$28,300,000,000 / 148,200,000 patient-days = $190.96 per patient-day
```

That measured national figure sits **between the two statutory routine-home-care
tiers** — 4.96% above the days-61+ rate of $181.94 and 17.3% below the days-1-60
rate of $230.83. Solving the two-tier blend:

```
230.83x + 181.94(1 - x) = 190.96
48.89x = 9.02
x = 0.184  ->  18.4% of days priced at the days-1-60 tier
```

And because the national average also contains CHC, IRC and GIP days priced at
$1,674.29, $532.48 and $1,199.86 respectively — all far above either RHC tier —
the true share of routine-home-care days falling in the first 60 days must be
**below** 18.4%. The arithmetic says this is a long-stay book: the great majority
of Medicare hospice days are paid at the lower, day-61-and-after rate.

Two cross-checks on the same number:

```
$28,300,000,000 / 1,820,000 users      = $15,549 per user per year
148,200,000 / 1,820,000 users          = 81.4 days per user   (consistent: 15,549 / 190.96 = 81.4)
$15,549 / $35,361.44 aggregate cap     = 44.0% of the cap for the average user
$35,361.44 / $230.83                   = 153.2 days of day-1-60 RHC before the cap equivalent is reached
$35,361.44 / $181.94                   = 194.4 days of day-61+ RHC
```

MedPAC independently reports average lifetime length of stay among decedents of
**99.6 days** (median 19 days) in 2024, up ~3 days from 96.2 in 2023. The 81.4
days-per-user figure derived above is for *all* users rather than decedents only,
so a lower figure is the expected relationship, and the two are consistent.

### 2. Company disclosure vs. statute

Chemed's VITAS Healthcare, fourth quarter 2025 (a quarter entirely inside FY2026,
so the rates above apply), disclosed:

- Average revenue per patient day: **$208.01** (+86bp year on year)
- Routine home care reimbursement averaged **$187.19**
- High-acuity care averaged **$1,153.55**
- Net revenue $418.8m (+1.9%); days of care +1.3%; admissions +6.0%
- Medicare Cap billing limitation accrual $2.4m in the quarter
- Full year 2025 VITAS revenue **$1,630.1m** (+6.5% on $1,530.98m in 2024);
  Q4 2025 average daily census 22,462

Checks:

```
VITAS blended      $208.01  vs national $190.96   ->  8.9% above the national average
VITAS RHC-only     $187.19  lies between $181.94 and $230.83
  implied tier-1 share: (187.19 - 181.94) / (230.83 - 181.94) = 10.7%
VITAS high-acuity  $1,153.55 / $1,199.86 GIP      =  96.1% of the unadjusted GIP rate
```

Every one of VITAS's disclosed per-day figures falls inside the statutory
envelope, and its RHC blend implies an even more long-stay-weighted book than the
national average. The gap between $187.19 and the day-1-60 rate of $230.83 is the
single most important economic fact about this market: **the marginal day is paid
at roughly 79% of the opening-day rate**, and the aggregate cap truncates the tail
on top of that. These are not national rates applied to VITAS exactly — the wage
index adjusts the labour share county by county, and VITAS is concentrated in
Florida, Illinois, Ohio and California — so the 10.7% is directional, not a
measurement of VITAS's day mix.

Scale check on the largest publicly traded operator:

```
22,462 ADC (VITAS Q4 2025) x 365 = 8.20 million patient-days per year
8.20m / 148.2m Medicare hospice days (2024) = 5.5%
```

Addus HomeCare, full year 2025: hospice net service revenue **$262.5m** (from
$228.2m in 2024, +15.1%); hospice gross profit 48.7% of hospice revenue (47.0% in
2024); Medicare 93.1% of hospice revenue (91.2% in 2024); hospice 18.8% of group
revenue in Q4 2025. Group revenue ~$1.42bn (+23.2%). Addus's hospice line is
**16.1% the size of VITAS's** (`262.5 / 1,630.1`), and the two together are
`(1,630.1 + 262.5) / 28,300 = 6.7%` of Medicare hospice spending. Mixes
calendar-2025 company revenue against calendar-2024 MedPAC spending, so read as
an order of magnitude, not a share.

### 3. Spend per certified provider

```
$28,300,000,000 / 6,706 Medicare-certified hospices = $4.22 million per provider per year
148,200,000 / 6,706                                 = 22,100 days per provider per year
22,100 / 365                                        = average daily census of 60.5 per provider
```

A $4.22m average revenue line and an average daily census of 60 is a very small
business. MedPAC's composition data explains it: in 2024, **82% of hospices were
for profit** but they furnished care to only **60%** of Medicare hospice patients,
because for-profit providers carry smaller censuses; 88% were freestanding
(furnishing 84% of patients); 88% urban, 12% rural. The provider count went
4,840 (2019) -> 6,706 (2024), with for-profits 3,435 -> 5,497, and growth
averaged 7.8% per year from 2019 to 2023 before slowing to 2.6% in 2024 as
California (which imposed a licensing moratorium in 2022), Arizona and Texas
decelerated. Nevada still grew 20% in 2024.

### What this rules in and out

- **Rules out Tier 1 entirely.** `28.3 / 5.31 = 5.33x`: US Medicare FFS alone is
  more than five times Tier 1's global 2025 base.
- **Rules out Tier 3's internal consistency.** Fortune's own North America
  component ($27.85B, 2025) is below US Medicare FFS 2024 ($28.3B), which that
  component must by construction contain.
- **Rules out Tier 4 as hospice.** $150B for 2026 is a palliative-care-scope
  figure.
- **Rules in Tier 2.** Grand View's $32.60B for 2026 implies that Medicare FFS
  was `28.3 / 32.6 = 86.8%` of the US total two years earlier, which is the
  right order given Chemed's disclosure that Medicare and Medicaid are "in excess
  of 95%" of VITAS revenue and Addus's that Medicare alone is 93.1% of its
  hospice revenue.

This is a plausibility test, not a market measurement. It establishes which
published figure can be divided by a real denominator without producing an
absurdity. It does not establish that $32.60B is correct.

## What we publish

**Grand View Research's U.S. series: $32.60B in 2026 -> $45.30B in 2033.**

- `(45.30 / 32.60)^(1/7) − 1 = 4.8121%` -> published CAGR **4.81**. Grand View
  publishes "4.8% from 2026 to 2033"; 4.81 is that same figure taken to two
  decimals from Grand View's own two endpoints, so the series reconciles to
  under 0.01 points rather than to the rounding.
- Base year **2026**, forecast period **2026-2033**, historical **2022-2025**
  (Grand View's actuals run 2021-2025). Unit Billion, USD.
- Chosen because it is the **only tier that survives division against MedPAC's
  patient-day count and CMS's statutory per-diem**, and because the independent
  US estimate (Precedence, $32.72B for 2026) agrees with it to 0.4%.
- Series, modelled at the constant 4.8121% implied by the two published
  endpoints — interpolation, not separately sourced observations:

| 2029 | 37.54 |

The headline is explicitly **United States**, and the report says so in the

Also published, all traceable above: the $190.96/day grounding division; the five

itself names — Addus, Amedisys, Chemed (VITAS), Enhabit, Pennant Group — in words,
with no share figures.

## Do not publish

- **`regional_analysis` = `[]`.** The published regional splits are exactly the
  thing the grounding arithmetic falsifies: Fortune's North America figure is
  below US Medicare FFS alone, and Dataintelo's North America $13.6B is below it
  by half. There is no defensible regional table for a market whose published
  global totals cannot be reconciled with their own components.
- **`country_analysis` = `[]`.** No source gives a country split that survives
  the same test. The US is covered as the headline scope, not as a share of a
  global total.
- **`recent_developments` = `[]`.** MedPAC records that in 2025 two publicly
  traded hospice companies expanded by acquisition and one closed or consolidated
  branches, but does not name which did which; naming them would be a guess. No
  entry has a verifiable company + year + event triple.
- **No `market_share` anywhere** — not in `competitive_landscape`, not in
  `regional_analysis`, not in `country_analysis`, not in `market_segmentation`.
  Hospice share is not publicly disclosed. The 5.5%-of-Medicare-days and
  6.7%-of-spending figures in the grounding section are divisions of two
  disclosed public numbers, labelled as such and as cross-year, and appear only
  as prose arithmetic.
- **No segment percentages.** `market_segmentation` carries plain strings. Grand
  View and Precedence both segment by the four statutory levels of care, by
  location and by diagnosis, but neither publishes the shares in public material.
- **No global headline.** Tier 3 and Tier 4 are not published as this report's
  market size; they are described and placed.
- **No Tier 1 figure as a market size.** Data Bridge's $4.66B and Research and
  Markets' $5.31B are reported in the estimates table for completeness and then
  explicitly ruled out.
- **No "number of hospices" from the CMS Care Compare provider file.** The
  August 2026 release of that file was queried directly and returned 453,492
  rows, but those are provider-measure rows (one per CCN per measure per period),
  not providers. The distinct-CCN count could not be obtained before data.cms.gov
  began returning HTTP 403 to repeated queries, so the provider count published
  here is MedPAC's CMS-derived **6,706** for 2024, which is cited as MedPAC's
  count and not as an independent one.
- **No FY2027 rates.** A FY2027 hospice final rule was published in the Federal
  Register on 3 August 2026 and is in effect from 1 October 2026, but its rate
  tables and cap were not retrieved. The report states FY2026 rates and dates
  them.
- **No MedPAC margin after 2023.** MedPAC states plainly that hospice FFS
  margins are presented only through 2023 because of the data lag required to
  calculate cap overpayments. The 2023 figure (8.0%, down from 9.8% in 2022) and
  MedPAC's own projection for 2026 (~9%) are published as those two things; no
  2024 or 2025 margin is asserted.
